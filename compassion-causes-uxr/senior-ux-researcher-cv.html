Compassion Causes product discovery and donor decision program

Compassion Causes is a consumer giving experience where supporters browse and fund urgent needs submitted by church partners around the world. The research program focused on a core product question: how can new visitors quickly find a cause that feels personally meaningful, understand why it matters, trust the information, and continue toward giving?

The work addressed discovery and navigation friction in the live experience, including categories that described internal or functional structures such as Technology, Infrastructure, and Income Generation more clearly than the human outcomes supporters cared about. The program connected baseline usability, outcome-based IA, motivational segmentation, prototype testing, and post-launch measurement.

Selected Research Programs

Compassion Causes product discovery and donation experience

Owned research planning, task design, metric selection, analysis, synthesis, and stakeholder readouts across baseline usability, taxonomy, motivational segmentation, and prototype benchmarking.

  • Designed repeatable usability benchmarks for discovery, cause detail comprehension, donation simulation, filters, trust indicators, and error recovery.
  • Benchmarked Story Behind the Story against the existing Causes browse experience with a counterbalanced within-subject Lyssna study.
  • Recommended advancing SBS browse patterns while preserving Causes trust, proof, and cause-detail strengths.

Outcome-based cause categorization and IA validation

Translated participant mental models into a five-category outcome-based navigation model for supporters browsing causes.

  • Used open card sorting, similarity clustering, entropy review, and label normalization.
  • Validated category hypotheses with a closed card sort using 20 cause cards.
  • Resolved ambiguous causes through outcome-forward subcategory framing.

Story Behind the Story mobile-first browse redesign

Evaluated a redesigned browse model intended to make cause discovery clearer for mobile-first exploratory supporters.

  • Cleaned sample: n = 50.
  • Methods: prototype evaluation, comparative within-subject usability, qualitative review, paired statistics.
  • Measures: clarity, CSAT, trust, confidence, continue intent, NPS, SUS-equivalent usability.

Sponsor and donor motivations MaxDiff

Used best-worst scaling to understand what first-time prospects and existing donors valued most in a giving experience.

  • Prospects prioritized clarity, autonomy, simplicity, and tangible impact.
  • Donors placed relatively more emphasis on faith alignment, urgency, and emotional storytelling once trust was established.
  • Product strategy shifted toward clarity, control, and tangible impact before deeper emotional appeals.
