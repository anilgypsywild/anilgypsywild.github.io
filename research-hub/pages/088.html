[Draft] CVG UX Research Update (Nov 2024 – Mar 2025)

CVG User-Centered Design Framework

image source: Chad Dant


Provide CVG with a concise, evidence-based update on recent UX research (Research) across the Causes and Community platforms, highlighting how user insights are driving product decisions, surfacing emotional engagement opportunities, and identifying critical risks when research is underutilized.

At a Glance: What’s the Value of Research?

10 Research Probes, 2 Platforms, 0 Guesswork.
UX Research is actively shaping how Compassion builds products that serve donors, frontline partners, and internal teams. Our goal: bridge insights into design impact, elevate trust and satisfaction, and remove friction for high-value users.

Platform: Causes

1. Causes Pre-Launch UX Baseline (Nov 2024)

Goal: Benchmark user experience before Giving Tuesday
Findings:

  • Mobile UX scored lowest across metrics (NPS = 50; Ease = 3.83)
  • Key blockers: donation flow complexity, unclear navigation

Action: Prioritize mobile-first changes. Implement feedback loops post-launch.

Platform: Community App (FCP-Facing)

2. FCP Beta Usability + Role Analysis (Dec 2024)

Goal: Understand adoption and pain points by role
Findings:

  • CSAT = 6.1/7 overall, strongest among Project Directors (NPS = 70)
  • Health Workers and Accountants need offline mode, upload fixes, and faster approvals

Quote: “Finally, a way for us to speak directly to donors.”

Next: Build role-specific dashboards; test offline workflows; track changes in low-bandwidth settings.

3. Psychometrically Validated Survey Redesign (Mar 2025)

Goal: Improve actionability of FCP feedback
Results:

  • Retained only α ≥ 0.7 items for reliability
  • Introduced MaxDiff for prioritizing offline mode + uploads
  • Added SURQ (System Usability & Reliability Questionnaire)

Why it matters: We now have a replicable, rigorous survey framework aligned with product KPIs.

Platform: Community (Supporter/Donor-Facing)

4. Supporter Platform Navigation & IA Study (Jan 2025)

Goal: Assess emotional resonance and trust signals
Findings:

  • SUS = 59.2 (below norm); NPS = –64 (critical)
  • Users wanted storytelling and clarity — not vague job titles like “Relationship Manager”

Next: Redesign Impact Timeline and onboarding flow. Retest for emotional clarity.

5. A/B Test: Vertical vs. Horizontal Timeline (Jan 2025)

Winner: Vertical

  • SUS = 103.1, preferred by 85%
  • Chi-square p < .001; confirmed statistical significance

Sticky Insight: Vertical wins for clarity, flow, and reduced cognitive load.

6. Donor Interview Themes (Feb 2025)

Goal: What sustains emotional engagement?
Findings:

  • Stories > Stats. Photos > Charts.
  • Trust = transparency + frequency + personalization
  • Friction: vague updates, unclear fund usage, infrequent communication

Impact: Personal storytelling is now a core UX pillar for Supporter Beta.

7. HID Persona Development (Feb 2025)

Goal: Serve our most mission-aligned donors
Personas: N detailed segments (e.g., Legacy Builders, Faith-Driven Stewards)
Top Needs:

  • Real-time fund tracking
  • Personalized communication
  • Impact visibility
    Next: Journey maps and testing per segment.

8. Icon Trust Perception Study (Mar 2025)

Goal: Avoid brand confusion among Christian audiences
Finding:

  • 33% saw shading in Version A as potentially LGBTQIA+ coded
  • Version B felt more “calm,” “clean,” and “professional”

Action: Revise shading in A, validate with follow-up test.

Summary: Research is Working When…

  • We replace opinion with data (e.g., timeline layout, survey design)
  • We triangulate usability with emotion (e.g., storytelling, trust)
  • We design for the field, not the boardroom (e.g., FCP workflows)
  • We build personas that shape features (e.g., HID segmentation)

What’s Next?

  • MaxDiff + KANO rollout for FCP and Supporter updates
  • Emotional engagement metrics in development
  • High-fidelity designs validated with segmented donors
  • Unified UX survey framework for scalable insights

2. Research Is NOT Working When...

Despite clear wins, research loses traction when it’s treated as optional, late-stage, or cosmetic. These are the moments where we lose product momentum, trust, or decision quality.

❌ Insights Are Ignored or Delayed

Example: Supporter Platform IA feedback showed –64 NPS and critical trust gaps, yet improvements were deprioritized.

  • Impact: Risk of expanding a broken experience → erodes donor trust at scale.

❌ Research Is Asked to Validate Half-Baked Concepts

“We don’t know if this experience will resonate — can we just test it?”

  • Reality: If there’s no user-centered hypothesis, we’re not testing—we’re outsourcing product thinking.

❌ Metrics Are Gathered Without Meaning

e.g., surveys without psychometric validation or clear links to behavior

  • Impact: Leads to noise, not knowledge. No signal = no impact = no action.

❌ Research Findings Are Not Tied to Roadmaps

Findings get documented, but backlog doesn't move.

  • Result: Knowledge sits in slides, not in code. User friction stays unaddressed.

❌ Emotional Insight Is Treated as “Soft”

Supporters told us “Stories matter more than stats” — but some teams still default to dashboards.

  • Missed Opportunity: Emotional engagement is not a ‘nice-to-have’ — it’s what drives giving, retention, and trust.

⚕️ Fix It by Re-Centering Research:

  • Tie insights directly to design specs and sprints
  • Treat emotions as data — and measure them
  • Involve research early, not retroactively
  • Ask not just “Did we test it?” but “What did we learn—and build—from it?”
