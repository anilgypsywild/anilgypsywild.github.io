Appendix: Rationale for Collecting User Stories

📌 Why Are We Collecting User Stories?

Currently, our user stories are not documented centrally, which creates gaps in our understanding of how people use our products. By formally capturing these, we can:

  • Map user journeys to better understand workflows and pain points.
  • Clarify Jobs to Be Done (JTBD) so we know what success looks like for our users.
  • Refine personas by validating user motivations, behaviors, and engagement patterns.

📝 How We Define User Stories

A user story typically follows this format:

As a [user type], I want to [goal] so that I can [desired outcome].

For example:

• Causes Platform (Donors) → As a high-impact donor, I want to see clear impact statements so that I feel confident in my contribution and continue giving.

• Community Platform (FCP Staff) → As an FCP worker, I want an easy way to upload images and reports so that I can efficiently share updates with donors.

By structuring stories this way, we create a shared understanding of who our users are, what they need, and what drives them.

🔍 Connecting User Stories to Psychographics


Beyond the basic user stories, we also need to consider why users behave the way they do. This is where engagement, adoption, and retention come in.

  1. • Engagement: What motivates users to explore and interact with the product?
  2. • Adoption: What makes them commit to using it?
  3. • Retention: What keeps them coming back over time?

Different people on our team may refer to this as “motivation,” “engagement drivers,” or “behavioral insights,” but fundamentally, it’s about understanding what keeps users invested in our products.

📌 Next Steps: How You Can Help

  1. Submit User Stories → Based on your work, share the user stories relevant to your area.
  2. Validate Assumptions → Does our current thinking align with what you’ve observed in research or user interactions?
  3. Contribute to Psychographic Insights → What have you noticed about why users engage or drop off?
