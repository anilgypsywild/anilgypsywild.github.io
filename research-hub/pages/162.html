Research article

Overview

  • This synthesis presents key insights from donor interviews, structured based on the Contextual Interview Script for Donors.
  • It synthesizes donor perspectives on engagement, impact, and transparency, highlighting recurring themes and opportunities for improvement.

A. Grouped Answers

1. Donor Experience and Motivation

Motivations for Giving

  • Can you tell me about what inspired you to support Compassion International?
  • What are the most important factors for you in making the decision to support a cause?

Feedback from Donors:

  • Pat (Watsons): "We like the idea of people ministering to children. That’s the biggest factor."
  • [name omitted] (Sheafes): "It’s not about metrics for me. I expect Compassion to use funds wisely, but my motivation is seeing real transformation."

Connection to the Cause

  • How connected do you feel to the communities and individuals your donations support?
  • Are there specific stories or interactions that have deepened this connection for you?

Feedback from Donors:

  • Corbetts: "A picture says a thousand words. On a trip to El Salvador, I saw firsthand the children I was supporting. That made a difference."
  • William (Magnusons): "One-to-many relationships don’t feel as strong. [name omitted] and I prefer one-to-one communication where possible."
  • Watsons: "We haven’t gotten updates in a while, which might be why we feel a bit disconnected."

2. Engagement Preferences and Experiences

Preferred Ways of Engaging with Recipients

  • How would you like to engage with recipients of your donations? For example, would you prefer regular updates, personalized letters, or other forms of communication?
  • What kind of interactions or updates make you feel most connected to the impact of your giving?

Feedback from Donors:

  • Smiths: "A letter from one of the kids was incredibly moving. It made me feel like my contribution mattered."
  • Sheafes: "Pictures of people in the initiative make an impact."
  • Williams: "Seeing what happens at the village level, including pictures and progress, makes a difference."

Frequency and Format of Updates

  • How often do you prefer to receive updates on the impact of your donations?
  • Do you have a preference for how these updates are shared—such as through emails, letters, video updates, or even in-person events?

Feedback from Donors:

  • Watsons: "Monthly updates would be great."
  • Magnusons: "People don’t respond to vague invitations. Personalization matters."
  • Williams: "A message from the program director, plus photos, helps us stay engaged."

Memorable Engagements

  • Have there been any particularly memorable updates or interactions with recipients that have stood out to you?
  • What aspects of those experiences made them special or meaningful?

Feedback from Donors:

  • Smiths: "A letter from a child in the program was deeply personal and meaningful."
  • Sheafes: "When I get prayer requests and updates, those moments feel real."
  • Williams: "Alumni stories are powerful. Seeing how they’ve grown and succeeded reinforces my belief in this work."

3. Communication and Impact Measurement

Information and Transparency

  • How important is it for you to know how your donation is being used, down to specific details?
  • Are there certain kinds of impact data or success stories you feel are missing, or that you’d like to see more of?

Feedback from Donors:

  • Smiths: "Impact means change and improvement. We want to see milestones, not just numbers."
  • Sheafes: "I trust Compassion, but I do appreciate seeing real-life transformations."
  • Williams: "Numbers matter, but seeing how lives are changed is more important."

Challenges in Engagement

  • Are there any barriers or frustrations you’ve encountered when trying to connect with recipients or learn about the impact of your donations?
  • How could we make it easier or more effective for you to engage and feel connected with the recipients of your support?

Feedback from Donors:

  • Corbetts: "If we see that we’re supporting ‘lots of kids’ but there’s no detail, it feels impersonal."
  • Watsons: "Monthly updates would help."
  • Magnusons: "People don’t respond well to generic invitations. It needs to feel personal."

4. Support Preferences and Improvement Areas

Feedback on Current Engagement Options

  • How satisfied are you with the current ways we provide updates and connections to recipients?
  • Are there specific elements you feel could be improved to help you feel more engaged or connected?

Feedback from Donors:

  • Sheafes: "Pictures help a lot. They make the mission tangible."
  • Williams: "Messages from program directors add a personal touch."
  • Watsons: "We just need to know more frequently what’s happening."

New Engagement Ideas

  • If you could design an ideal way of engaging with recipients or seeing the impact of your contributions, what would that look like?
  • Are there other ways we could help you connect more deeply with the people and communities you support?

Feedback from Donors:

  • Williams: "Alumni stories are impressive. I’d love to hear more about how former program participants are doing now."
  • Magnusons: "Having more direct communication, even once a year, would be great."

B. Qualitative Themes from Donor Interviews

This section synthesizes qualitative themes derived from donor interviews, structured to align with key engagement and adoption metrics from the Supporter Beta Launch Learning Goals.

Supporter Launch Learning Goals

  • Adoption: How regularly Supporters use the platform?
  • Engagement: What content and interactions drive satisfaction?
  • Retention: What factors keep Supporters connected long-term?
  • Impact Perception: How effectively Supporters see and understand the difference their donations make?
  • Trust & Transparency: Whether Supporters feel confident in how their contributions are used?

The themes below focus on how Supporters engage with the platform, perceive impact, and develop trust, while ensuring a meaningful and data-driven donor experience.

1. Adoption & Early Engagement

(Different types of donors may have distinct onboarding behaviors—some may engage immediately due to a personal connection, while others may require more structured guidance.)

Key Questions:

  • Will Supporters use the platform regularly?
  • How long does it take for a new Supporter to engage?
  • What motivates first-time interactions with content?

Emerging Themes:

First impressions matter:

  • "A picture says a 1K words. In trip to El Salvador visit was very impactful. 'Went from black and white to color.'" – Corbetts
  • "Haven't gotten updates in a while, which might be my fault for the 5 churches. Precious: I log into the site every month but I don't feel connected." – Watsons

Personal relevance accelerates engagement:

  • "William: 2 with one to Many. [name omitted]: 3 with [name omitted]. Connection was because [name omitted] went there and showed them the church." – Magnusons
  • "1/2 (scale). When we visited the village, it was a 5. We got to hold the children. But since that time, we've only had one program update." – Williams

2. Content Effectiveness & Emotional Connection

(The way different donor groups emotionally connect with content may vary; some may resonate more with personal stories, while others may look for quantitative impact data.)

Key Questions:

  • Which types of content drive the most engagement and satisfaction?
  • How do Supporters feel emotionally connected to the cause?
  • What format (text, images, video) resonates the most?

Emerging Themes:

Visual storytelling increases connection:

  • "Seeing what happens at the village. Seeing the children in their element. Seeing the people that are speaking into their lives." – Williams
  • "When I see pictures of people in the initiatives I support. Personal words and reports." – Sheafes

Authenticity drives satisfaction:

  • "To be in contact with the leader of the church. To be able to ask questions and have a short conversation about how things are going and what they need." – Watsons
  • "People don't respond to vague invitations. People respond to real stories. Pictures from art class. A note from a mother." – Magnusons

3. Trust & Transparency

(Donors may have different expectations regarding transparency—some may want detailed financial breakdowns, while others prioritize qualitative impact stories.)

Key Questions:

  • Do Supporters feel confident that their donations are being used wisely?
  • What information increases trust?
  • What types of transparency updates matter the most?

Emerging Themes:

Detailed fund allocation increases confidence:

  • "Knowing exactly how much went to education, healthcare, and food makes me feel reassured." – Watsons
  • "A simple breakdown of where my money goes would be helpful." – Magnusons

Personalized impact updates build long-term trust:

  • "If I can see the progress of a child or project I personally support, I know it’s real." – Williams
  • "Seeing direct updates from the church leaders on the ground makes me trust the organization more." – Corbetts

4. Impact Perception

(Understanding impact perception may require different communication styles depending on donor motivations—some may value long-term systemic impact, while others may focus on direct individual success stories.)

Key Questions:

  • Do Supporters feel their contributions make a tangible difference?
  • What types of updates reinforce the sense of impact?
  • How do Supporters define success?

Emerging Themes:

Supporters want measurable outcomes:

  • "Impact = change and improvement. That means metrics. Point A vs Point B because of our work together." – Smiths
  • "The number of children reached. How far the money goes. Money goes so much further in 3rd world countries." – Magnusons

Emotional success stories enhance impact perception:

  • "I read about a girl who finished school because of our support, and that meant everything." – Smiths
  • "Alumni stories. They are impressive people." – Williams

5. Retention & Continued Participation

(Different types of donors may require unique retention strategies—some may engage more through social proof and community, while others may prefer direct one-on-one updates.)

Key Questions:

  • What keeps Supporters engaged long-term?
  • What factors influence whether they continue donating?
  • How does social sharing contribute to retention?

Emerging Themes:

Community-building fosters retention:

  • "I’d love a way to connect with other donors who support the same region." – Watsons
  • "It would be great to have a discussion space for those of us involved in the same projects." – Smiths

Social proof increases engagement:

  • "When I see other people donating or sharing impact stories, it makes me want to keep giving." – Corbetts
  • "If my friends or family could see what I’m supporting, maybe they’d want to join too." – Magnusons

Strategic: Next Steps

  1. Develop a data-driven content strategy: emphasizing visual storytelling and personalized updates to maximize donor engagement.
  2. Enhance transparency initiatives: by integrating real-time fund allocation breakdowns and direct impact tracking for donor trust-building.
  3. Implement engagement frameworks: facilitating direct donor-recipient interactions through structured communication touchpoints.
  4. Leverage social influence mechanisms: to amplify network-driven giving through integrated sharing and community-building features.

Tactical: Next Steps: Applying the Eight Golden Rules

To enhance the Supporter Platform, the Eight Golden Rules of Interface Design provide a structured approach to improving usability, engagement, and transparency, specifically for Supporter Platform and its users.

  1. Strive for Consistency – Conduct usability testing to identify inconsistencies in dashboard updates, notification formats, and messaging across channels.
  2. Seek Universal Usability – Evaluate donor interaction data to personalize engagement for high-impact donors, occasional donors, and new supporters.
  3. Offer Informative Feedback – Test real-time impact visualizations and content preference settings to determine optimal donor update frequency.
  4. Design Dialogs to Yield Closure – Assess how completion-based updates (e.g., project milestones, success stories) influence donor satisfaction and retention.
  5. Prevent Errors – Analyze donor drop-off points to design error-proof workflows for donation allocation and impact tracking.
  6. Permit Easy Reversal of Actions – Investigate undo options and flexible donation structures to reduce donor hesitancy.
  7. Keep Users in Control – Develop a donor customization model for notifications, content delivery, and engagement frequency.
  8. Reduce Short-Term Memory Load – Explore persistent login and donor history features to improve accessibility and navigation.
