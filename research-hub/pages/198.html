Field Research Protocol: AI-Assisted Workflow Evaluation

Prepared for: @SRiedner , @JCostanzo2 , @AThompson8
Project Owner: Anil
Date: Oct 28, 2025
Location: Remote or in-person testing (Community or Causes environments)
Method: Observational + Post-Session Quantitative Survey
Duration per Session: 35–45 minutes

1. Purpose of This Field Study

This study will help us understand how people think, feel, and behave when completing a work process with and without AI assistance.
You (PM and designer) are the field facilitators. You will observe, listen, and collect data, not interpret it.

We want to know:

  • Does the AI-assisted version make the process clearer or more confusing?
  • Does it lower the mental effort it takes to complete the task?
  • Does it make users feel more capable, confident, or in control?
  • Would they want to use it again?

2. Guiding Approach (Anthropological Lens)

You are stepping into each participant’s lived experience.
Treat the session like a short ethnographic interview and observation.

  • Be neutral. Don’t lead or judge.
  • Ask them to think aloud. Let them explain what’s going on in their mind.
  • Notice emotion, tone, and body language. These are qualitative data.
  • Respect silence. A pause often reveals discomfort or reflection.
  • Write down exact words. No summaries during the session.

Your goal: capture the story of their experience, supported by numbers that show intensity.

3. Participants

  • 5–7 individuals familiar with the existing workflow (e.g., frontline, moderators, or internal support staff).
  • Each session will include:
    1. A walkthrough of the current workflow.
    2. A walkthrough of the AI-assisted concept (static prototype or storyboard).
    3. A short survey to collect numeric data.

4. Field Materials Needed

  1. Baseline artifact – current workflow visuals or written steps.
  2. AI-assisted concept – mock-up, storyboard, or guided slides.
  3. Post-Session Survey (print or digital):
    • UMUX-Lite (usability & usefulness)
    • NASA-TLX (mental demand)
    • Confidence rating
    • PrEmo / Feeling word scale
    • NPS (reuse & recommend)
  4. Notebook or form for qualitative notes.
  5. Quiet room and stable internet (if virtual).

5. Data Collection Plan

Each session follows this sequence:

Step 1. Welcome and Consent (5 min)

Say this aloud:

“Thank you for helping us today. We’re studying how this workflow feels and how clear it is. We’ll look at your normal process, and then we’ll show a concept that includes some assistance. There are no right or wrong answers—just your honest thoughts.”

Ask permission to take notes or record audio if needed.
Do not collect names or personal identifiers.

Step 2. Baseline Workflow Observation (10–12 min)

Goal: See how they experience the current (manual) process.

Instructions:

  • Show them the workflow.
  • Ask them to describe what they would do at each step.
  • Encourage them to think aloud (“What are you thinking as you do this?”).
  • Write down:
    • Places they pause or look uncertain.
    • Words like confusing, unsure, long, hard.
    • Any workarounds or “hacks” they mention.

Ask after they finish:

“On a scale of 1 to 5, how easy or hard does this process feel overall?”
1 = Very easy, 5 = Very hard

Record number → (Doability measure).

Step 3. AI-Assisted Concept Walkthrough (10–12 min)

Goal: See how they interpret and react to the AI assistance.

Instructions:

  • Say:

    “Now I’ll show you a version of the same workflow that includes some assistance or suggestions. Please tell me what you notice, what makes sense, and what feels strange.”

  • Go step by step through the concept screens or mock-up.

At each key moment ask:

  1. Clarity: “How clear is what’s happening here?”
    • 1 = Very clear → 5 = Very unclear
  2. Control: “How in control would you feel of your own work here?”
    • 1 = Fully in control → 5 = Not in control
  3. Voice/Trust: “Does this sound like something you would say or submit?”
    • Record Yes / No / Not sure.
  4. Note any emotion shown (smile, frown, pause, sigh).

Step 4. Post-Session Survey (10–12 min)

Give them the survey or read aloud.

Metric Instrument Scale / Wording Purpose
UMUX-Lite (Usability & Usefulness) 2 items 7-point Likert (0 = Strongly Disagree → 6 = Strongly Agree) Dependability
“This workflow is easy to use.”
“This workflow’s capabilities meet my needs.”
Confidence Rating 1 item 7-point Likert (0 = Strongly Disagree → 6 = Strongly Agree) Doability
“I can complete this workflow on my own without help.”
NASA-TLX: Mental Demand 1 item 7-point scale (1 = Very low → 7 = Very high) Cognitive load
“How mentally demanding was this workflow?”
Emotional Tone (PrEmo / Feeling Word) 1 item Choose one word: Frustrated / Neutral / Calm / Confident / Energized Delight
NPS (Reuse & Recommend) 2 items 0–10 scale Desire
“How likely are you to use this workflow again?”
“How likely are you to recommend it to a colleague?”

Ask last:

“What’s the one thing you would fix or change first?”
Record their exact words.

Step 5. Field Notes After Each Session (5 min)

Immediately write your notes before the next participant:

  • Where they got stuck.
  • Where they laughed or looked uncomfortable.
  • Exact quotes (put quotes in quotation marks).
  • Emotional cues or gestures.
  • Their feeling word choice.
  • Their numeric answers.

Label your note sheet clearly:

Participant #: [ID]
Role / Team: [Role Type]
Session Date / Time

Do not interpret patterns yet.

6. What Data You Will Send Back

After completing all sessions, send these to Anil:

  1. A spreadsheet with numeric data (one row per participant):
    • Task ease (1–5)
    • Clarity (1–5)
    • Control (1–5)
    • UMUX-Lite items (0–6)
    • Confidence (0–6)
    • NASA-TLX mental demand (1–7)
    • NPS: reuse & recommend (0–10)
    • Feeling word
  2. Your field notes (verbatim, no summary).
  3. Any screenshots or images showing participant comments on mock-ups.

7. Researcher’s Ethical Code

  • Protect privacy: no names, only participant IDs.
  • Be neutral: never say “good” or “bad.”
  • Listen first, write second.
  • Record facts, not opinions.
  • Remember: the person, not the product, is your teacher.

8. Closing

Say:

“Thank you for helping us learn. What you shared will help us make the process clearer and easier for everyone who uses it later.”

Smile, end the session, and immediately log your data.

End of Field Protocol
Designed for the CVG UCD Validate Phase, integrating 4D UX metrics and anthropological field methods for holistic insight.
