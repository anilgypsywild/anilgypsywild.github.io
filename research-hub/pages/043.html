Card Sort Results Phase 1 (Jan 28, 2026)

Outcome Based Cause Categorization

  • Author: Anil Shankar; Audience: [name omitted], [name omitted] 
  • Sample size: 23 participants
    Method basis: similarity clustering, entropy review, label normalization
  • Card to Category Mapping

Intro and key highlights from Open Cardsort

  • This card-to-category mapping table below translates participant mental models into a clear, outcome-based navigation structure that Design/Product can act on with confidence.
  • Across the study, participants consistently organized causes by human outcomes rather than by assets or program names, enabling a stable five-category IA that minimizes overlap and cognitive load.

Key highlights

  1. Education and Skills emerge as the strongest anchors, with the lowest ambiguity and highest agreement across cards.
  2. Safety and Essential Infrastructure functions as a foundational category, successfully absorbing health, water, sanitation, and structural safety without needing separate silos.
  3. Ambiguity is concentrated in a small set of cards (health and social center, community garden, pig farming), which can be resolved through outcome-forward labels and subcategory framing rather than new top-level categories.
  4. Arts and Culture holds as a distinct but secondary path, capturing enrichment and preservation needs that users do not naturally group with core education or skills.

Design Notes

  • Five top level categories are the maximum stable structure
  • Education and Skills are the strongest anchors
  • Infrastructure should explicitly include health, water, sanitation, and safety
  • Arts and Culture works best as a distinct but secondary pat

How people want to find Causes

Multi select results (count of participants selecting each)

  • Search: 11
  • Navigation menus: 9
  • Follow recommendations or suggestions: 9
  • Use filters or tags: 8

Implication: we likely need all four patterns supported. Search leads, but the distribution is not lopsided.

How Participants Interpreted the Purpose of Each Category

Across the open ended “benefits or outcomes” response, the dominant outcome themes were:

  1. Meeting basic needs and safety first: Safety, clean water, hygiene, health care, prevention.
  2. Education as long term lift: Schooling, literacy, development, future opportunity.
  3. Skills that lead to self sufficiency: Training, employability, earning power.
  4. Community stability and dignity: Safe spaces, involvement, shared infrastructure, culture.

One participant explicitly called out faith motivated needs, emphasizing church and school and “everybody needs Christ,” which signals that for some donors, church related causes are not just infrastructure, they are mission aligned.

Clustering results from the actual sort behavior

Method Notes: 

  • For each participant, each card has a “category group” string.
  • For every pair of cards, we compute similarity = percent of participants who put the two cards in the same group.
  • Next, we cluster cards using average link agglomerative clustering on distance = 1 minus similarity.
  • Finally, we choose an interpretable cut that matches the best structure in the data (and avoids forcing unstable cards into fake certainty).

Results: 

These are the pairs that most consistently travel together across participants:

  1. 3D printing skills lab with Barber skills training
  2. Sewing and knitting workshop with Weaving skills center
  3. Classroom renovation with School security access
  4. Preschool learning center with Literacy program
  5. Cafeterias and restrooms with Classroom renovation
  6. Safe recreation area with Safe playground
