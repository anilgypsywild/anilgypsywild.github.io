Research article

Purpose

This document outlines a comprehensive, phased research strategy aimed at enhancing the usability, trust, and engagement of Causes donation platform. The focus areas span from foundational research on donor behaviors to summative testing of the end-to-end app experience. The approach integrates mixed-methods research, including qualitative insights, quantitative measures, and A/B testing, to inform design decisions and drive measurable improvements.

Research Objectives:

  1. Understand Donor Motivations and Behaviors: Develop personas and foundational insights to uncover diverse donor needs, preferences, and motivations.
  2. Optimize User Interactions: Evaluate cause selection, navigation, and checkout processes to streamline user journeys and increase donor satisfaction.
  3. Enhance Trust and Engagement: Measure and address trust-related barriers, ensuring donors feel confident and motivated to support multiple causes.

Key Deliverables:

  • Weekly findings reports with actionable insights.
  • Comprehensive A/B test results analyzing engagement, efficiency, and user preferences.
  • Final summative usability test report highlighting success rates, task efficiency, and overall satisfaction.

Business Impact:

The insights derived from this research aim to improve donor engagement, increase the number of supported causes, and maximize overall donation amounts, reinforcing the platform's role as a trusted and user-friendly tool for charitable giving.

Mixed-Methods Research Plan 

With A/B Testing and User Metrics. This combined plan integrates hypotheses, user-based metrics, and A/B testing into a unified research strategy.  Each phase ensures a balance between qualitative insights, quantitative measures, and A/B testing to validate design decisions.

UX Mixed-Methods Research Hypotheses

Cause Tile Hypotheses

  1. Users who see urgent causes marked with visual cues (e.g., badges, icons) will identify them 20% faster than those without cues.
    • A/B Test: Text-based "Urgent" label (A) vs. visual badge (B).
  2. Users provided with distinct one-cause and multi-cause checkout flows will report a 15% higher CSAT (≥6 on a 7-point scale) for ease of checkout.
    • A/B Test: One unified flow (A) vs. distinct one/multi-cause flows (B).
  3. Displaying donation amounts in ascending order will reduce confusion, as measured by a 10% decrease in usability errors and a CSAT score ≥5.
    • A/B Test: Ascending order (A) vs. descending order (B).
  4. Users who can scan credit cards will complete checkout 25% faster and report higher overall ease-of-use scores (≥6 on a 7-point scale).
    • A/B Test: Manual entry-only (A) vs. credit card scanning (B).

Cause Selection Hypotheses

  1. Providing clear explanations of funding sources will improve user trust, with ≥80% of users accurately explaining who provides financial resources after the task.
    • A/B Test: Detailed tooltip explanation (A) vs. brief inline explanation (B).
  2. Simplifying the presentation of donations made, donations needed, and the slider bar will reduce user-reported confusion to ≤10% during testing.

Checkout Hypotheses

  1. Clarifying whether donations go to Compassion or the church will increase trust, with ≥75% of users expressing confidence in their understanding of fund allocation.
  2. Reducing confusion around the slider bar will lead to ≥90% successful task completion during donation adjustment.
    • A/B Test: Labeled min/max values (A) vs. unlabeled slider bar (B).

Navigation/Findability Hypotheses

  1. Improved navigation hierarchy and filters will enable users to find causes in ≤2 minutes, with ≥85% of users reporting ease of finding a cause.
    • A/B Test: Filter-based navigation (A) vs. keyword-based search (B).
  2. Enhanced search functionality will reduce time on task for finding specific causes by ≥30%, with ≥80% CSAT scores for findability.

A/B Testing Strategy

  1. Key Variations
    • Urgency Indicators: Text-based label (A) vs. visual badge (B).
    • Donation Order: Ascending (A) vs. descending (B).
    • Slider Bar Design: Labeled (A) vs. unlabeled (B).
    • Checkout Flow: Manual entry-only (A) vs. credit card scanning (B).
    • Navigation: Filter-based (A) vs. keyword search (B).
  2. Metrics for A/B Testing
    • Engagement: Click rates on urgent causes or slider.
    • Task Efficiency: Time on task for selecting causes and checkout.
    • User Preference: Self-reported preference for A or B in post-test surveys.
    • Satisfaction: CSAT and UMUX-Lite scores.
  3. Execution
    • Recruit a minimum of 30 participants per variant for statistical significance.
    • Use session recording tools (e.g., UserZoom) for unmoderated tests.
    • Combine quantitative results with qualitative feedback for holistic insights.

Deliverables

  1. Weekly findings reports (Weeks 2–4).
  2. Final A/B test results with comparative analysis and recommendations.
  3. Comprehensive summative usability test report (Week 5), including:
    • Task success rates, time on task, and user satisfaction metrics.
    • A/B test outcomes and actionable insights.
    • Stakeholder presentation summarizing findings and next steps.

Persona Questions for Millennials

These questions focus on understanding millennials’ behavior, motivations, and preferences related to donating to causes, particularly their one-cause vs. multiple-cause selection tendencies, information needs, and trust-building factors.

General Demographics and Background

  1. What age group do you belong to?
    • Under 18
    • 18–24
    • 25–34
    • Other
  2. What is your current occupation?
    • Student
    • Employed full-time
    • Employed part-time
    • Self-employed
    • Unemployed
    • Other
  3. How often do you donate to charitable causes?
    • Once a week
    • Once a month
    • A few times a year
    • Rarely
    • Never
  4. What types of causes do you usually support?
    • Education
    • Environmental
    • Humanitarian aid
    • Health and wellness
    • Other (please specify)

One Cause vs. Multiple Causes

  1. When you donate, do you typically focus on one cause or multiple causes at a time? Why?
  2. What factors influence your decision to donate to one cause versus multiple causes?
    • Urgency of need
    • Personal connection to the cause
    • Financial considerations
    • Transparency of information
    • Other (please specify)
  3. If you’ve donated to multiple causes in one session, what made it easy or difficult for you?
  4. What would encourage you to donate to multiple causes more often?
    • Faster checkout process
    • Better organization of causes
    • Easier comparison of causes
    • Other (please specify)

Information Needs

  1. What information do you want to see before deciding to donate to a cause?
    • A breakdown of how donations will be used
    • Testimonials or stories about impact
    • The urgency or timeline of the need
    • Information about the organization or people running the cause
    • Other (please specify)
  2. How do you decide how much to donate to a cause?
    • Suggested donation amounts
    • My budget at the time
    • The total funding goal of the cause
    • Other (please specify)
  3. Do you prefer to see suggested donation amounts in ascending or descending order? Why?
  4. When selecting donation amounts, do you find slider bars intuitive, or would you prefer a different method?

Trust and Loyalty

  1. What makes you trust a donation platform enough to use it?
    • Secure payment options
    • Transparency about where the money goes
    • Recognized partnerships (e.g., with trusted organizations)
    • User reviews or ratings
    • Other (please specify)
  2. Have you ever felt hesitant to donate due to a lack of trust? If so, what caused this hesitation?
  3. What features would make you come back to our app to donate again?
    • Updates on how donations are being used
    • Rewards or gamification (e.g., badges, milestones)
    • Recommendations based on past donations
    • Discounts or promotions for recurring donations
    • Other (please specify)

Experience and Feedback

  1. What did you like most about your last donation experience (online or offline)?
  2. What frustrated you most about your last donation experience?
  3. How do you typically learn about new causes or donation opportunities?
    • Social media
    • Friends or family recommendations
    • News outlets
    • Other (please specify)
  4. Would you recommend a donation platform to your friends? Why or why not?
  5. What improvements could make your experience donating through our app better?
