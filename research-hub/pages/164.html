Research article

🏗️ 🚧 👷 WORK IN PROGRESS (INCOMPLETE DOC)

Objective

  • This is a research exploration to map real donor feedback from contextual interviews to The Eight Golden Rules of Interface Design, providing actionable insights for designers, product managers, engineers, and researchers working on the Supporter Platform.
  • The analysis leverages verified donor quotes, insights from real-world digital experiences, and strategic next steps for an engaging, effective, and transparent donor experience.

1. Strive for Consistency

Insight: Donors expect a uniform and predictable experience across communication channels, ensuring consistency in updates, dashboard interfaces, and messaging.

Donor Feedback:

  • "We haven’t gotten updates in a while, which might be why we feel a bit disconnected." – Watsons
  • "William: 2 with one-to-many. [name omitted]: 3 with [name omitted]. We don’t utilize the dashboard as much as we should, but when we do, we expect it to work the same every time." – Magnusons

Real-World Example:

  • Amazon Donation Dashboard: Amazon Smile provided a uniform tracking mechanism, allowing users to see donation history in the same format consistently.

Action:

  • Maintain consistency in update frequency, terminology, and dashboard interactions.
  • Ensure uniform visual design and interaction flow across all donor-facing interfaces.

2. Seek Universal Usability

Insight: Different donors have varying levels of engagement, and the platform should accommodate both novice and expert users.

Donor Feedback:

  • "I’d love to see more explanation about where my money is going, maybe even a video that helps me understand." – Watsons
  • "We don’t need more emails, but when we do check, it should be easy to find updates that matter to us." – Magnusons

Real-World Example:

  • Duolingo: Offers progressive engagement from simple explanations for beginners to advanced customization for frequent users.

Action:

  • Implement adaptive personalization based on donor interaction history.
  • Provide layered engagement options: Simple summaries for casual users, detailed reports for deep-divers.

3. Offer Informative Feedback

Insight: Donors need clear, timely updates about the impact of their contributions.

Donor Feedback:

  • "A picture says a thousand words. In a trip to El Salvador, we saw firsthand the children we were supporting. That made a difference." – Corbetts
  • "When I see pictures of people in the initiatives I support, it makes me feel connected." – Sheafes

Real-World Example:

  • GoFundMe Impact Reports: Provides direct feedback through visual progress bars and video updates from beneficiaries.

Action:

  • Integrate real-time donation impact meters.
  • Use automated but humanized confirmations for donations and milestone updates.

4. Design Dialogs to Yield Closure

Insight: Donors want structured updates that give them a sense of completion.

Donor Feedback:

  • "Alumni stories are impressive. They show how our donations have changed lives." – Williams
  • "If I can see the progress of a child or project I personally support, I know it’s real." – Williams

Real-World Example:

  • Kickstarter Campaign Updates: Final updates show completed projects and results.

Action:

  • Use progressive storytelling—move donors from funding a cause to seeing its final impact.
  • Provide structured updates: Donation → Project Progress → Final Impact Story.

5. Prevent Errors

Insight: Users should not struggle to recover from errors or misunderstand how their money is being allocated.

Donor Feedback:

  • "If we see that we’re supporting ‘lots of kids’ but there’s no detail, it feels impersonal." – Corbetts
  • "We shouldn’t have to dig through a dashboard to find out how our donations are being used." – Magnusons

Real-World Example:

  • Venmo Payment Confirmations: Prevents misallocated funds with confirmation prompts and easy reversals.

Action:

  • Auto-fill and smart suggestions for donation allocation.
  • Implement clear UI elements that prevent misallocation of funds.

6. Permit Easy Reversal of Actions

Insight: Donors should be able to change or update their giving preferences easily.

Donor Feedback:

  • "We had to email support to change our giving. It should be simpler." – Watsons
  • "I’d like to see a way to adjust donations dynamically without jumping through hoops." – Magnusons

Real-World Example:

  • Patreon Subscription Model: Allows easy modifications to donation amounts and frequency.

Action:

  • Implement self-service donation management with undo options.
  • Allow temporary pauses in donations rather than complete cancellations.

Recommendations

For Design

  • Standardize dashboard updates and communication flows to improveand baseline usability.
  • Introduce progressive engagement models for new and returning donors.

For Product

  • Enhance donor customization options for notifications and content preferences.
  • Improve impact visualization via live dashboards and milestone-based updates.

For Research (Personas & Sub-Personas)

  • Conduct a deep dive into persona and sub-persona development to segment donor engagement behaviors.
  • Identify key archetypes of Supporters (e.g., High-Impact Donors, Occasional Donors, Community-Engaged Donors).
  • Map donor expectations across different personas to tailor content, communication, and interaction models.

For Engineering

  • Develop real-time donation tracking with immediate visual feedback.
  • Implement easy-to-reverse donation actions to encourage risk-free exploration.
