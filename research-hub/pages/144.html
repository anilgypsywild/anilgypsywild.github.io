Icon Usability Test Report

Version A (Thread Border) vs. Version B (Solid Blue Border)

  • Sample Size: 15 participants
  • Method: Structured sequential test with open-ended, structured, and rating-based tasks
  • Platform: UserZoom
  • UserZoom Results Link: [reference omitted]

1. Executive Summary: Icon Usability Test Results (Version A vs. B)

Goal
Determine which of two new icon designs better represents Compassion’s brand and values, while ensuring no misinterpretation (e.g., LGBTQIA+ symbolism) and strong mobile clarity.

Participants
15 Compassion-aligned donors

  • 93% aged 55+
  • 66% college-educated
  • 47% use donation apps monthly
  • 80% are most concerned about transparency when donating online

Key Results

Metric Result
🦺 Safety 0% reported LGBTQIA+ associations
☯️ Preference Split Version A: 47% · Version B: 53%
Brand Alignment (Top 2 Ratings) A: 27% · B: 46%
Visual Simplicity A: 66% · B: 60%
Emotional Clarity ("Joy/Motion") A: 80% saw a person jumping or dancing
Improvement Need 53% gave feedback about border or clarity

What Each Icon Communicated

  • Version A (Thread Border): Felt joyful, expressive, and unique. Users described it as “uplifting” and “like angel wings.”
  • Version B (Solid Border): Perceived as cleaner, more professional, and more on-brand—but a few users felt the border made it feel “boxed in.”

Recommendation

Both icons are safe and perform well, but each has tradeoffs:

  • Version A brings emotional energy and distinctiveness.
  • Version B delivers a stronger sense of branding and polish.

Next Step: Explore a hybrid design that combines:

  • The expressive figure and energy from Version A
  • The clean lines and color treatment from Version B
  • A border that feels intentional but less confining

4. Appendix: Research Design and Bias Minimization Strategy

To reduce priming bias and improve the authenticity of participants’ responses, the icon tests followed a carefully structured sequence:

  1. Icon Exposure Only — Users saw the icon with no labels, context, or prompts.
  2. Free-Form Association — The first question asked users to describe what came to mind, capturing top-of-mind, unfiltered responses.
  3. Categorical Selections (Multi-Select) — Only after the open-ended prompt did we show a list of potential interpretations for selection.
  4. Follow-Up Ratings — Users then rated simplicity, brand alignment, and uniqueness.
  5. Preference and Reasoning — Finally, users chose their preferred version, explained why, and suggested improvements.

🔍 This structure was critical to ensuring that participants were not steered toward specific answers (e.g., “Does this remind you of LGBTQIA+ themes?”).

It preserved the integrity of early-stage interpretation and made the 0% LGBTQIA+ association finding methodologically strong. .

Methodological Insight

  • The two-step interpretation process (first open-ended, then structured) revealed high convergence between spontaneous responses and structured choices (e.g., over 65% alignment in “joyful action” and “jumping/dancing” across both methods).
  • This validates the construct stability of the icon concept (joy/movement) and confirms that structured prompts did not artificially inflate associations.
