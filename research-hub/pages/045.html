Research Brief

Outcome-Based Cause Categorization and Language Discovery

Surface the heart behind the need 🫶

  • Date: January 2026
  • Status: In progress – pending final test inputs

Background and Context

Compassion Causes is designed to help supporters discover and fund urgent needs submitted by church partners around the world.

Currently, causes are organized using functional or operational categories such as water, infrastructure, or education. While accurate internally, these labels often fail to communicate the underlying human outcome or why a need matters in everyday life.

As a result, supporters experience hesitation during discovery, struggle to form a personal connection with causes, and disengage before committing to action. This challenge is increasingly important as Compassion explores AI-enabled discovery and recommendation systems that require well-structured, human-centered semantic inputs.

This research establishes a supporter-informed baseline for how causes should be grouped, described, and contextualized.

Problem Definition

The current cause taxonomy describes what a need is rather than what it achieves.

This creates several downstream issues:

  • Supporters must interpret meaning on their own.
  • Emotional connection is weakened by lack of context.
  • Trust and confidence are harder to establish early.
  • Discovery drop-off occurs before giving decisions.
  • AI systems lack validated semantic structure.

Research Objectives

The objectives of this study are to:

  1. Identify how supporters naturally group causes based on meaning and outcomes.
  2. Discover the language supporters use to describe causes in their own words.
  3. Understand which outcomes and human needs matter most during discovery.
  4. Inform a revised, outcome-based cause categorization model.
  5. Provide validated semantic inputs for product, design, and AI teams.

Key Research Questions

  1. How do supporters naturally group Compassion Causes when not constrained by existing categories?
  2. What outcomes or human needs do supporters prioritize when deciding where to give?
  3. What language do supporters use to explain why causes belong together?
  4. Where do current categories fail to align with supporter mental models?
  5. What signals reduce hesitation and increase confidence during discovery?

Scope

Included in scope:

  • Cause grouping and categorization
  • Outcome-based framing
  • Descriptive language and rationale
  • Early signals of clarity, confidence, and motivation

Out of scope:

  • Donation checkout or payment flows
  • Visual design polish
  • Feature-level usability testing
  • Performance benchmarking

Methodology Overview

  • The primary method for this study is open card sorting.
  • Example Card Sort here is for you to try it out: [reference omitted]

Participants will be shown a representative set of Compassion Causes, each accompanied by a short, neutral description. They will be asked to:

  • Group causes in ways that make sense to them.
  • Describe or name each group.
  • Explain why specific causes belong together.

This approach allows supporters to reveal their mental models without imposing predefined structures or labels.

If needed, follow-up methods may include:

  • Closed card sorting to validate candidate categories.
  • Language preference testing to compare existing and proposed labels.
  • Short qualitative interviews to clarify ambiguous findings.

Participants

Participants will be:

  • U.S.-based supporters
  • A mix of donors and potential donors
  • Representation across Current-Gen and Next-Gen segments

Target sample size for the initial open card sort is approximately 30 participants.

Test Artifacts and Open Items

The following study inputs are required prior to launch and are currently pending alignment with Product and Design:

  1. Final list of causes to include in the card sort
  2. Cause descriptions used on each card
  3. Any initial category concepts or constraints from Design
  4. Confirmation of how many categories the UI is expected to support

These artifacts will be finalized in collaboration with Design and the Product and documented here prior to study launch.

Until these inputs are confirmed, the study design is complete, but execution is intentionally on hold to ensure alignment and traceability.

Data Collected and Outputs

Data collected will include:

  1. Cause groupings across participants
  2. Category names and descriptions generated by users
  3. Qualitative explanations for grouping decisions
  4. Natural language expressions of need and outcome

Key outputs from this study will include:

  • Outcome-based cause categories grounded in supporter mental models
  • Supporter-authored language patterns for labels and descriptions
  • Product and design recommendations for information architecture
  • Semantic inputs for AI-enabled discovery and recommendations

Success Criteria

This study will be considered successful if it produces:

  1. Clear and repeatable grouping patterns
  2. Language that supporters can easily understand and explain
  3. Reduced ambiguity around why causes matter
  4. Actionable insights for product and design decisions
  5. Defensible documentation for leadership and AI teams

Timeline

  • Study setup and alignment: Week 1
  • Data collection: Week 2
  • Synthesis and stakeholder readout: Week 3

Initial directional insights are expected within 8–10 days of study launch.

Risks and Mitigations

  • Risk: Participants struggle with abstract grouping
    Mitigation: Clear instructions and neutral examples.

  • Risk: Categories remain too broad or overlapping
    Mitigation: Iterative closed card sorting.

  • Risk: Premature interpretation of early findings
    Mitigation: Treat initial results as directional.

  • Risk: AI expectations overshadow human needs
    Mitigation: Keep Phase 1

    explicitly human-centered.

Tldr;

  • This research establishes a human-centered foundation for Compassion Causes.
  • By grounding categorization and language in supporter mental models, it enables clearer discovery today and more trustworthy AI-driven experiences in the future.
