Research Brief: Evaluating AI-Assisted Workflow Efficiency for FCP's in Thailand.

Source Doc: November 2025 - Thailand Research Plan.pdf

1. What is the problem we are trying to solve?

The existing workflow for creating and submitting content is manual, repetitive, and cognitively demanding. Users and moderators expend unnecessary effort navigating unclear steps, redundant actions, and uncertain expectations of quality.

From recent field studies (e.g., Thailand FCP research), we know that:

  • Frontline partners and moderators spend significant time rewriting submissions to meet Compassion’s clarity and tone standards.
  • This moderation bottleneck reduces local voice, slows turnaround, and adds operational cost.
  • The absence of guided assistance or structured feedback results in low task confidence and high cognitive load during submission.

This study will test whether introducing an AI-assisted workflow:

  1. Improves users’ perception of clarity, confidence, and independence.
  2. Reduces mental workload and friction in completing tasks.
  3. Maintains or strengthens trust and perceived usefulness of the system.

Metrics are drawn from the CVG 4D UX Scorecard and Summative Metrics framework:

  1. Doability: Task ease, self-reported independence.

  2. Dependability: UMUX-Lite (usability, usefulness), Confidence rating.

  3. Delight: Emotional tone (PrEmo or feeling word scale).

  4. Desire: Willingness to reuse and recommend (NPS).

    Workload: NASA-TLX (Mental Demand).

2. Why are we solving the problem now?

We are in the Design–Build–Validate stage of the UCD Strawman Framework, where new concepts must be tested with real users before scaling.

AI capabilities are being introduced into workflows that were previously human-moderated. Without evidence-based validation, we risk:

  • Introducing complexity disguised as simplicity.
  • Reducing trust if users perceive AI output as inaccurate or impersonal.
  • Shifting moderator workload without actually improving user experience.

Testing now allows us to:

  • Quantify baseline workload and trust before rollout.
  • Evaluate whether AI assistance reduces mental effort and increases dependability.
  • Gather qualitative insights about how AI guidance affects user voice and ownership.

This aligns with CVG’s strategic pillars to solve real neighbor needs, create and simplify, and own for the long haul.

3. What do we know about this problem?

From previous Compassion research:

  • Manual submissions cause confusion and repeated rework cycles.
  • Moderators act as translators, converting FCP submissions into organizational language.
  • Training and templates help, but not enough to produce independent, confident submissions.
  • AI assistance (when informally tested by moderators) improves speed but not trust—users fear that AI-generated language does not sound authentic.

Preliminary indicators: (say...)

  • CSAT averages 5.5–6/7 on current processes (moderate satisfaction).
  • Mental demand averages 5/7 (NASA-TLX, high workload).
  • Confidence ratings hover around 4/7 (“somewhat confident”).
  • NPS for reuse is below +20 (indicating low advocacy).

These data suggest there is room for improvement across Doability, Dependability, and Desire, particularly in reducing cognitive load and ensuring authenticity.

4. What will we do with the results?

Study Purpose

To determine whether AI-assisted workflows produce measurable improvement in:

  1. Perceived usability and usefulness (UMUX-Lite).
  2. Reduced mental demand (NASA-TLX).
  3. Increased confidence and independence.
  4. Improved emotional satisfaction and trust.
  5. Higher intent to reuse and recommend (NPS).

Study Design

  • Participants: 5–7 internal users simulating or using Frontline roles (non-technical staff).
  • Method: Guided walkthrough using static AI-assisted prototypes, followed by post-session surveys.
  • Instruments:
    1. UMUX-Lite (7-point Likert) – usability, usefulness.
    2. NASA-TLX (7-point Mental Demand) – workload.
    3. Confidence Rating (7-point Likert) – task independence.
    4. PrEmo / Feeling Scale – emotional tone.
    5. NPS (0–10 scale) – reuse and advocacy.

Intended Outcomes

  1. Quantitative validation of whether AI assistance reduces workload and increases usability.
  2. Identification of trust or authenticity concerns in AI language.
  3. Recommendations for UX copy, design language, and training materials to close the gap between AI support and human moderation.
  4. Integration of these validated measures into future UX Scorecards and AI design guidelines.

Next Step

Results will feed directly into the next Validate sprint milestone:

  • If metrics improve ≥10–15% on UMUX-Lite and ≥1 point reduction on NASA-TLX, the workflow proceeds to live prototype.
  • If not, redesign and retraining interventions will precede the next test cycle.
