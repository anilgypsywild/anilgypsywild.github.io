High Impact Donor Walkthrough: Experience the Platform and Provide Feedback

Objective:

This walkthrough will allow you ([name omitted] or any High Impact Donor) to experience the Supporter App as a first-time user.

It will guide you step-by-step through the platform while capturing key usability, engagement, and impact-related feedback.

Each step includes:

  • Instructions on what to do.
  • Feedback prompts to guide your thoughts.
  • Two easy ways to submit feedback (WhatsApp/SMS or Voice Call).
  • A day-by-day breakdown for structured feedback collection.
  • Estimated time commitment so users know this is a quick process.

Day-by-Day Walkthrough for High Impact Donors

Day 1: Login & First Impressions

Estimated Time: 5 minutes

Instructions:

  1. Open the Supporter App and log in.
  2. Observe the homepage layout, navigation, and what draws your attention first.

Feedback Prompts:

  • What was your first impression of the homepage?
  • Was it clear what to do next, or did you feel lost?
  • Did any elements confuse you?

Feedback Submission:

  • Option 1: Send a quick WhatsApp/SMS message describing your experience.
  • Option 2: Record a 1-minute voice memo summarizing your thoughts.

Day 2: Navigation & Finding Key Features

Estimated Time: 5-7 minutes

Instructions:

  1. Explore the Impact, Notifications, and Profile sections.
  2. Try to find where to view past donations or track how contributions are being used.

Feedback Prompts:

  • Were the menu options intuitive, or did you struggle to find anything?
  • How easy was it to track your impact?
  • If something felt frustrating, what specifically was difficult?

Feedback Submission:

  • Option 1: Send a text message with 1-2 things you liked and 1-2 things that felt confusing.
  • Option 2: Hop on a 5-minute voice call to discuss what worked and what didn’t.

Day 3: Engaging with Impact Stories

Estimated Time: 5-7 minutes

Instructions:

  1. Go to the Impact section and scroll through the updates.
  2. Click on a few stories to read details about how donations are making a difference.

Feedback Prompts:

  • Did the impact stories feel compelling and personal, or generic?
  • Did the story format (text, images, numbers) help you feel engaged?
  • Would you be inspired to donate again based on what you saw?

Feedback Submission:

  • Option 1: Send a text message with your top insight about the stories.
  • Option 2: Send a picture/screenshot of a story you liked or found confusing with a brief comment.

Day 4: Notifications & Engagement Reminders

Estimated Time: 3-5 minutes

Instructions:

  1. Look at the notifications you received in the app.
  2. Open any alerts or messages and assess their relevance.

Feedback Prompts:

  • Were the notifications useful or annoying?
  • Did they make you feel connected to your contributions, or were they generic?
  • How often would you want to receive updates like these?

Feedback Submission:

  • Option 1: Reply via text message with a simple thumbs-up/down and why.
  • Option 2: Drop a voice message explaining what works or what’s distracting.

Day 5: Commenting & Community Engagement

Estimated Time: 5 minutes

Instructions:

  1. Try leaving a comment on an impact post.
  2. If your comment isn’t posted, check the moderation message.

Feedback Prompts:

  • Was it clear why your comment wasn’t posted, if applicable?
  • Did the comment system make you feel part of the community, or disconnected?
  • Would you want to interact with other donors or community members?

Feedback Submission:

  • Option 1: Send a text message about what worked/didn’t work.
  • Option 2: If something was confusing, send a screenshot of the issue.

Day 6: Closing & Final Thoughts

Estimated Time: 3-5 minutes

Instructions:

  1. Reflect on your overall experience using the platform.
  2. Think about whether you would return to use it again.

Feedback Prompts:

  • What is one thing you liked about the platform?
  • What is one thing you would change?
  • Would you recommend this platform to other donors? Why or why not?

Feedback Submission:

  • Option 1: Send a final text message with a score from 1-10 on your overall satisfaction.
  • Option 2: Record a 2-minute voice memo summarizing your experience.

Summary of Feedback Methods

Method When to Use Estimated Time Commitment
WhatsApp/SMS (Quick Text Response) Fastest way to provide short insights (e.g., “navigation was confusing” or “impact stories were great”). 1-2 minutes
Voice Call (5-minute discussion) For more detailed insights when text isn’t enough. 5 minutes
Screenshot + Text Comment Best for reporting a specific issue (e.g., “I couldn’t find donation tracking here”). 2-3 minutes

Purpose of This Walkthrough

This structured walkthrough allows [name omitted] or any High Impact Donor to:

  • Experience the platform as a first-time user.
  • Identify usability pain points.
  • Provide actionable feedback through simple, fast methods.
  • Simulate real user testing with minimal effort (total estimated time commitment: 25-30 minutes over 6 days).

This document serves as a practical guide to ensure the team understands how High Impact Donors perceive and interact with the app before launch.

Appendix: Research Approach w/ Alternative Feedback Collection Methods due to Lack of Instrumentation

Since no in-app feedback tools have been implemented, data will be gathered using:

  1. WhatsApp or SMS check-ins (short direct messages asking 1-2 key questions).
  2. Phone calls (5 minutes max) (quick follow-ups on engagement and usability).
  3. Users sending pictures of their screens (when they encounter issues).

This ensures we still collect meaningful usability and engagement data with minimal effort from users.

Week 1: Establish Baseline Metrics & User Behavior Tracking

Day Focus Area Key Activities Research Questions Scientific Rationale
Monday Initial Login & Onboarding Track login motivations, time to first interaction - What motivated you to log in today? Self-determination theory (Deci & [name omitted], 1985) suggests motivation types (intrinsic vs. extrinsic) impact engagement. Identifying initial motivation predicts retention.
Tuesday Navigation & Feature Discovery Identify friction points in movement between sections - Were you able to find what you were looking for? - What, if anything, was frustrating? Norman’s (1988) theory of affordances: If users struggle with navigation, it indicates poor signifiers or lack of discoverability.
Wednesday Content Engagement Analyze engagement with impact stories & community posts - Which content type is most compelling? - What content type drives reactions and shares? Media richness theory (Daft & Lengel, 1986) states that richer media (videos, testimonials) enhance engagement more than text-based content.
Thursday Notifications & Retention Track notification interactions & response times - How often do you prefer updates? - Have you recommended the platform? Fogg’s Behavior Model (2009): If notifications don’t trigger action, the issue is with timing, frequency, or motivation.
Friday Drop-Off Risks Identify high drop-off areas - Where do users disengage? - What stopped you from continuing? Attrition theory (Lalonde, 1993): Users drop off when effort exceeds perceived value. Identifying these points allows targeted fixes.

Week 2: Usability, Satisfaction & Trust Measures

Day Focus Area Key Activities Research Questions Scientific Rationale
Monday User Satisfaction (CSAT, SUPR-Q) Collect satisfaction scores via WhatsApp/text - How satisfied are you with navigation? - How confident are you that your donation makes a difference? CSAT & SUPR-Q (Sauro, 2015) are validated UX measures predicting long-term adoption.
Tuesday Trust & Impact Perception Measure perceived impact, donation confidence - Do you feel connected to the mission? - Do you trust how donations are used? Trust Theory (Mayer et al., 1995): Donors engage more when transparency is high.
Wednesday Attitudinal Engagement Measure emotional connection - Does the platform make you feel part of the community? - Is the content timely and relevant? Social Presence Theory (Short et al., 1976): Emotional connection increases retention.
Thursday Feature Testing A/B test notification frequency via WhatsApp - Did the new experience improve usability? - Did it reduce friction? Hick’s Law (1952): Too many notifications increase cognitive load, reducing engagement.
Friday Engagement Correlations Compare satisfaction with engagement behavior - Does high engagement correlate with satisfaction? - What would improve your experience the most? UX research shows engagement correlates with perceived usefulness (Davis, 1989, Technology Acceptance Model).

Week 3-4: Iteration & Final Refinements

Day Focus Area Key Activities Research Questions Scientific Rationale
Monday Address Top UX Issues Implement changes for key pain points - Have usability improvements increased engagement? - Are users reporting fewer frustrations? Nielsen’s usability heuristics (1994): Fixing error prevention & efficiency boosts retention.
Tuesday Personalized Engagement Features Test different notification timings - Do personalized updates increase engagement? - What type of notification drives the most response? Personalization increases emotional investment (Kaptein et al., 2015).
Wednesday Content & Communication Quality Evaluate impact storytelling - Does content feel authentic? - Is communication clear and relevant? Authenticity drives donor retention (Bekkers & Wiepking, 2011).
Thursday Post-Interaction Behavior Measure return visits and sustained engagement - What makes users come back? - How long before users engage again? The Hook Model (Eyal, 2014): Retention is based on trigger-action-reward cycles.
Friday Launch Readiness Review Summarize findings, finalize changes - Are we meeting launch goals? - What’s left to refine before full rollout? Pre-launch UX validation prevents churn (Sauro & Lewis, 2016).

This approach demonstrates best-practice user research methods while adapting to the lack of product instrumentation.

References

1. Self-Determination Theory (Deci & [name omitted], 1985)

Summary: This theory posits that individuals have innate psychological needs—autonomy, competence, and relatedness—that drive motivation and engagement. Understanding these needs helps in designing experiences that foster intrinsic motivation, leading to sustained user engagement.

Citation: Deci, E. L., & [name omitted], R. M. (1985). Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior. Springer.

2. Affordance Theory (Norman, 1988)

Summary: Affordances refer to the perceived and actual properties of an object that determine how it can be used. In design, understanding affordances helps create intuitive interfaces where users can easily discern possible actions, reducing learning curves and errors.

Citation: Norman, D. A. (1988). The Psychology of Everyday Things. Basic Books.

3. Media Richness Theory (Daft & Lengel, 1986)

Summary: This theory evaluates communication media based on their ability to convey information effectively. Richer media (e.g., face-to-face communication) can handle complex messages better due to immediate feedback and multiple cues, whereas leaner media (e.g., emails) are suitable for straightforward information.

Citation: Daft, R. L., & Lengel, R. H. (1986). Organizational information requirements, media richness, and structural design. Management Science, 32(5), 554–571.

4. Fogg Behavior Model (Fogg, 2009)

Summary: This model asserts that behavior occurs when three elements converge: motivation, ability, and a prompt. For a desired behavior to happen, individuals must be sufficiently motivated, have the ability to perform the behavior, and be prompted at the right moment.

Citation: Fogg, B. J. (2009). A behavior model for persuasive design. Proceedings of the 4th International Conference on Persuasive Technology, 40.

5. Attrition Theory (Lalonde, 1993)

Summary: This theory examines the reasons behind individuals discontinuing participation in activities or organizations. Understanding attrition helps identify factors that lead to disengagement, allowing for the development of strategies to improve retention.

Citation: Lalonde, R. N. (1993). Evaluating the attrition hypothesis: The case of Jewish out-marriage. Journal of Marriage and the Family, 55(1), 135–143.

6. Customer Satisfaction (CSAT) and Standardized User Experience Percentile Rank Questionnaire (SUPR-Q) (Sauro, 2015)

Summary: CSAT measures customer satisfaction with a product or service, while SUPR-Q assesses the quality of the user experience across several dimensions, including usability, trust, and appearance. Both metrics provide insights into user perceptions and areas for improvement.

Citation: Sauro, J. (2015). SUPR-Q: A Comprehensive Measure of the Quality of the Website User Experience. MeasuringU.
