Persona Overview [Work in Progress...]

  • The High-Impact Donor (HID)  validated with contextual interview data represents a core user group in the Supporter Platform initiative.
  • These donors contribute significant financial resources, expect transparency, and seek meaningful engagement with the impact of their giving.
  • Using real qualitative interview data, this persona captures donor motivations, behaviors, and pain points to inform research and product development.

    • Source: Supporter Contextual Interviews Data from Dec 2024 - Feb 2025

    • Ideation from Persona Workshop (see attached PDF)

      Persona Workshop Support Platform.pdf

This data-backed persona definition ensures that the Supporter Platform is aligned with real donor needs, expectations, and motivations, ultimately improving engagement and retention.

🗒️ Key Characteristics

  1. Financial Commitment: Large-scale, long-term donations supporting multiple FCPs or specific initiatives.
  2. Engagement Style: Prefers personalized, data-backed updates over generic messaging.
  3. Trust & Transparency Focused: Needs clear, tangible proof of impact through stories, metrics, and direct communication.
  4. Community-Oriented: Many HIDs influence others to contribute and participate.
  5. Long-Term Relationship: Views giving as a sustained partnership rather than a one-time act.

🗣️ Behavioral Segments with Donor Quotes

1. The Strategic Philanthropist

  • Motivation: Data-driven, impact-focused giving. Wants to see measurable outcomes.
  • Pain Points: Frustration with lack of detailed impact metrics.
  • Needs: Clear financial allocation and milestones showing effectiveness of contributions.

Example Donor: [name omitted] (Sheafes)

"It's not about metrics for me. I expect Compassion to use funds wisely, but my motivation is seeing real transformation."

Example Donor: Smiths

"Impact means change and improvement. We want to see milestones, not just numbers. Point A vs. Point B because of our work together."

Product Implications:

  • Visual dashboards tracking fund allocation & real-time impact.
  • Goal-based giving opportunities with defined milestones.

2. The Faith-Driven Steward

  • Motivation: Rooted in Christian mission and spiritual transformation.
  • Pain Points: Prefers updates that focus on spiritual and community growth, not just financial reports.
  • Needs: Personal connections with the communities they support.

Example Donor: Pat (Watsons)

"We like the idea of people ministering to children. That’s the biggest factor. I want a child to know Jesus. That is most likely to happen through Compassion."

Example Donor: [name omitted] (Williams)

"If I get a sense that this FCP is well-equipped… that is success. It’s not about metrics but about spiritual growth."

Product Implications:

  • Faith-centered storytelling featuring testimonies and transformation narratives.
  • Live Q&A with pastors and FCP leaders to create personal connections.

3. The Community Connector

  • Motivation: Giving is a social activity; they inspire others to contribute.
  • Pain Points: Limited options for sharing impact with their network.
  • Needs: Better tools for inviting others, social giving, and community engagement.

Example Donor: William (Magnusons)

"One-to-many relationships don’t feel as strong. [name omitted] and I prefer one-to-one communication where possible."

Example Donor: Corbetts

"A picture says a thousand words. On a trip to El Salvador, I saw firsthand the children I was supporting. That made a difference."

Product Implications:

  • Referral & peer-invitation features to bring others into giving.
  • Social proof & giving leaderboards to encourage community participation.

🩹 Pain Points & Research Considerations

  1. Lack of Timely Updates: Delayed or impersonal updates lead to disengagement.
  2. Fragmented Communication: Donors receive updates in multiple formats, making it difficult to track impact.
  3. Low Engagement Opportunities: Donors want to go beyond financial contributions and interact more with beneficiaries.
  4. Trust & Transparency Concerns: Some donors are unsure how their money is used or want better reporting.

How the Supporter Platform Can Address HID Needs

Pain Point Proposed Solution
Lack of Timely Impact Updates Real-time personalized updates from FCPs
Fragmented Communication Unified platform for impact stories, engagement, and reporting
Limited Engagement Interactive features like Q&As, impact events, and discussion groups
Transparency & Trust Verified fund tracking & progress milestones

🛤️ Next Steps for Research & Product Development

  1. Validate HID Sub-Personas through longitudinal tracking & engagement behavior analysis.
  2. Develop User Journey Maps to align platform features with donor motivations.
  3. Test Social Giving Concepts to optimize referral and network-based participation.
  4. Assess Trust & Transparency Features to ensure financial clarity in reporting.

🚧 Sub Personas (Proto Personas)

From Neilsen-Norman Group

There are 3 different ways that teams can create personas, depending on the research data in which they are rooted:

  1. Proto personas, meant to quickly align the team’s existing assumptions about who their users are, but not based on (new) research
  2. Qualitative personas, based on small-sample qualitative research, such as interviews,  usability tests, or field studies
  3. Statistical personas, where initial qualitative research informs a survey instrument that is used to gather a large sample size, and the personas emerge from statistical analysis

All three of these have different pros and cons and will be applicable for different situations.

Overview

  • Through qualitative analysis of donor interviews, additional sub-personas have emerged beyond the broad categories of Current-Gen and Next-Gen High-Impact Donors (HIDs).
  • These sub-personas highlight distinct motivations, engagement patterns, and expectations, allowing for a more precise segmentation of donor needs.
  • This expanded segmentation ensures data-backed engagement strategies tailored to the diverse motivations and expectations of High-Impact Donors (HIDs).

1. The Legacy Builder

Definition:

A donor who sees giving as part of their legacy and wants to create long-term, generational impact.

Motivations:

  • Wants to fund scholarships, church construction, or long-term social initiatives.
  • Seeks multi-year engagement and wants to ensure continuity of support.
  • Often involved in estate planning with charitable giving components.

Real Donor Quotes:

  • Smiths: "Impact means change and improvement. We want to see milestones, not just numbers. Point A vs. Point B because of our work together."
  • Watsons: "To be in contact with the leader of the church and to ask how things are going—that’s what makes giving meaningful."

Product Considerations:

  • ✅ Endowment programs and multi-year sponsorship options.
  • ✅ Personalized reports tracking long-term progress.
  • ✅ Direct donor-family engagement opportunities with communities.

2. The Entrepreneurial Giver

Definition:

A donor who approaches philanthropy with a business mindset, looking for efficiency, scalability, and measurable ROI.

Motivations:

  • Wants to see their donation as an investment in human capital.
  • Prioritizes projects with clear, data-backed success metrics.
  • Interested in matching donations, impact multipliers, and efficiency.

Real Donor Quotes:

  • [name omitted] (Sheafes): "It’s not about metrics for me. I expect Compassion to use funds wisely, but my motivation is seeing real transformation."
  • Magnusons: "People don’t respond to vague invitations. They respond to real, measurable stories."

Product Considerations:

  • ✅ Impact calculators showing ROI of donations (e.g., $X supports Y children)
  • ✅ Data-rich insights on how money is spent, financial breakdown.
  • ✅ Opportunity to fund scalable, high-impact projects.

3. The Relationship-Oriented Donor

Definition:

A donor who prioritizes deep personal connections with beneficiaries and communities over financial metrics.

Motivations:

  • Seeks authentic, personal engagement with children, families, or FCP staff.
  • Values letter exchanges, video updates, and direct communication.
  • Interested in visiting supported communities or hosting donor meetups.

Real Donor Quotes:

  • Corbetts: "A picture says a thousand words. On a trip to El Salvador, I saw firsthand the children I was supporting. That made a difference."
  • [name omitted] (Williams): "If I get a sense that this FCP is well-equipped… that is success. It’s not about metrics but about spiritual growth."

Product Considerations:

  • ✅ Enhanced donor-child communication tools.
  • ✅ Video calls or Q&A sessions with FCP leaders and beneficiaries.
  • ✅ Virtual and in-person donor trips to supported regions.

4. The Mission-Driven Activist

Definition:

A donor motivated by social justice, advocacy, and humanitarian causes, looking to amplify awareness and drive systemic change.

Motivations:

  • Wants to mobilize others and raise awareness about pressing global issues.
  • Prefers to support causes like climate resilience, refugee aid, maternal health.
  • Uses donations as a form of advocacy and activism.

Real Donor Quotes:

  • Watsons: "We want ROI on our investment. Not just for us—but to facilitate others doing the same."
  • Magnusons: "People don’t respond to generic messaging. They respond to urgency and impact."

Product Considerations:

  • ✅ Campaign-based giving with social-sharing capabilities.
  • ✅ Opportunities to sponsor advocacy initiatives and policy efforts.
  • ✅ Integration with cause-driven movements and global NGO networks.

5. The Social & Digital Donor

Definition:

A donor who engages primarily via digital platforms, preferring quick, shareable impact stories over detailed reports.

Motivations:

  • Wants seamless mobile engagement and social media integration.
  • Prefers short-form video updates, interactive stories, and dynamic content.
  • Likely to influence friends and family through digital outreach.

Real Donor Quotes:

  • Corbetts: "Seeing pictures and updates in real time is what keeps me engaged."
  • Williams: "If my friends or family could see what I’m supporting, maybe they’d want to join too."

Product Considerations:

  • ✅ Mobile-first content strategy (Instagram-style stories, TikTok-length videos)
  • ✅ Tools for peer-to-peer fundraising and donor challenges.
  • ✅ Push notifications with real-time impact updates.

🔎 Key Differences Between Sub-Personas

Persona Type Key Focus Engagement Preference
Legacy Builder Long-term, sustainable giving Deep impact tracking, family engagement
Entrepreneurial Giver Efficiency, ROI-driven giving Data dashboards, financial reports
Relationship-Oriented Personal connections, direct updates Letters, visits, video messages
Mission-Driven Activist Advocacy, systemic change Campaigns, movement-driven giving
Social & Digital Donor Real-time engagement, shareability Mobile-first, bite-sized content

🧩 Next Steps for Research & Product Development

  1. Validate Sub-Personas by tracking digital vs. direct engagement preferences.
  2. Develop Custom Giving Journeys for each sub-persona within the platform.
  3. Test Social & Advocacy Features to measure adoption by activists and digital donors.
  4. Ensure Transparency & Data-Backed Reports to appeal to the ROI-driven segment.
