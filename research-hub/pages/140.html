🔬 Research-Informed Innovation: Informed, Not Driven

Why?

Research serves Jesus Christ with data, trends, or metrics. We let faith, wisdom, and love lead, while research informs rather than drives decision-making. Our goal is not efficiency for its own sake, but impact for God’s kingdom—improving the User eXperience ,UX, with compassion to liberate children from poverty in the mighty name of Jesus Christ.

"Commit your work to the Lord, and your plans will be established."
— Proverbs 16:3 (ESV)

Research Informs, But Mission Leads

This philosophy shapes how we apply self-service research methods within Compassion Causes and the Compassion Community Platform. Every study, every metric, and every feature must ultimately serve:

  • God’s mission of Compassion
  • The needs of our donors and front-line partners
  • The goal of making generosity seamless, transparent, and deeply meaningful

"Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory, for the sake of your steadfast love and your faithfulness!"
— Psalm 115:1 (ESV)

📖 Glossary of Self-Service Research Methods

Objective

Empower non-Researchers to apply self-service research methods to continuously improve the research team’s User Experiences and drive meaningful innovation.

📈 Innovation Group Product Phases

❓ Problem

Understanding the needs of donors, supporters, and front-line church partners - what challenges they face in funding causes, connecting with communities, or navigating the platform. Who is excluded today, and how can we make giving more seamless, transparent, and impactful?

💡 Solution

Designing and validating solutions that remove friction in donor engagement and empower front-line partners. What tools, insights, and experiences best support millennial donors, major supporters, and field staff?

🛠️ Feature

Building and refining key platform features - from impact tracking and giving workflows in Compassion Causes to improving community engagement tools in the Compassion Community Platform. Can users easily navigate, trust, and engage with the experience?

🚀 Launch + Growth

Measuring adoption, iterating on live user feedback, and optimizing the experience post-launch. Are donors giving more? Are front-line church partners supported effectively? What growth strategies will maximize engagement and impact?

Self-Service Research Methods

🧠 Brainstorming

  • 📈 Product Phases: 💡 Solution
  • Structured methods to generate, collect, group, and prioritize solution ideas.
    May include 📌 posting, ❓ focus questions, ⏳ time boxing, 🗂️ affinitization, or 🗳️ voting.

🗂️ Card Sorting

  • 📈 Product Phases: 💡 Solution
  • Helps design or evaluate information architecture by having participants organize topics into open or closed categories.

Cognitive Walkthrough

  • 📈 Product Phases: 🛠️ Feature , 🚀 Launch + Growth

  • A usability evaluation where evaluators work through a series of tasks and answer questions from the perspective of the user.

    📊 Competitive Analysis

  • 📈 Product Phases: ❓ Problem

  • Review and analyze competitor products to understand your product’s relative strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats (SWOT).

🎨 Inclusive Design Toolkit

  • 📈 Product Phases: ❓ Problem, 💡 Solution
  • Resources to ensure inclusive practices in design.
  • Supports:
    • 🏗️ Concept Development
    • ✍️ Prototyping
    • 🎭 User Skill Practice

🗣️ Interviews

  • 📈 Product Phases: ❓ Problem, 💡 Solution
  • Talking directly with customers to understand their:
    • 🛒 Experiences
    • 🎭 Motivations
    • 🔎 Behaviors

📚 Literature Review

  • 📈 Product Phases: ❓ Problem, 💡 Solution
  • Read, synthesize, and build on previous research. Start with a database of insights.

🤝 Participatory Design

  • 📈 Product Phases: 💡 Solution, 🛠️ Feature
  • Collaborate with customers to get their input throughout the design process.

☯️ Preference Testing

  • 📈 Product Phases: 💡 Solution, 🛠️ Feature
  • Determine customer preferences among a series of options, including:
    Design | 🎨 Icons | 🏷️ Logos | 🎨 Color Palettes | ✍️ Copy

Social Intelligence

  • 📈 Product Phases: ❓ Problem, 🚀 Launch + Growth
  • Review sentiment and discussions around a product in public forums like Facebook, X, TikTok, or Reddit.

☎️ Support Call Review

  • 📈 Product Phase: ❓ Problem
  • Review support calls from recent product releases to identify unsolved or latent issues.

🚨 Support Call Triage

  • 📈 Product Phases: 🚀 Launch + Growth
  • Immediate evaluation of support calls shortly after release to prioritize high-impact fixes or design changes.

💬 System-Initiated User Feedback

  • 📈 Product Phases: ❓ Problem, 🚀 Launch + Growth
  • Review customer-submitted feedback to identify problems that need to be alleviated or investigated.

☑️ Design or Heuristic Review

  • 📈 Product Phases: 🛠️ Feature
  • Evaluate a feature against a pre-defined list of design heuristics to identify usability issues.

🧵 Telemetry Usage Analysis

  • 📈 Product Phases: ❓ Problem, 🚀 Launch + Growth
  • Analyze usage logs to understand customer engagement with different product features.

RIF Example

How a PM Can Set Up a Mid-Fi A/B Test Using RIF

A PM can follow the Research-Informed Innovation Framework (RIF) to set up a Mid-Fi A/B Test for Peer-to-Peer Fundraising. Here's a simple step-by-step guide using the methods outlined in the doc:

1️⃣ Define the Hypothesis

  • Example: "Design A will lead to a 15% higher donor conversion rate than Design B because it simplifies the donation flow."

2️⃣ Select the Self-Service Methods

From RIF, these methods are most relevant:
✔️ Preference Testing → Gather user preference between Design A & B
✔️ A/B Testing → Quantitatively compare conversion rates
✔️ Surveys & System-Initiated Feedback → Capture qualitative insights

3️⃣ Set Up the Test

  • Randomly assign users to either Design A or Design B
  • Track key metrics: Time to complete donation, conversion rates, and user satisfaction (via a survey or system feedback)

4️⃣ Analyze & Apply Insights

  • Use Telemetry Analysis to see real-time user behavior
  • Run a Choice Model Survey (if needed) to further validate preference
  • Select the higher-performing flow based on data

Why This Works?

By following RIF, a PM doesn’t need a researcher to validate this—they can independently execute a structured, data-driven A/B test while ensuring the final design is informed, not just assumed.
