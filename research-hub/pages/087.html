Executive Summary: Scaling UX Research for Impact at CVG

A) 📈 Transforming Research into a Scalable, Data-Driven Function

(How Research Has Elevated Decision-Making Across CVG)

CVG Research has fundamentally changed how Research insights drive mission success. We are just getting started 🚀

1. Scaling UX Research Across Teams: From Bottleneck to Business Driver

Before: UX research was conducted ad-hoc, with individual designers running their own studies, leading to inconsistent methodologies and fragmented insights.
Now: We have built a scalable, repeatable UX research framework, allowing cross-functional teams to make data-driven and mission-aligned product decisions.

📊 Key Impact:

  • 4+ research studies initiated in 2025, using self-service ART Forms.
  • 3+ teams conducting UX research independently, reducing turnaround time from 360 minutes (2 CVG Staff x 3 one-hour meetings) to 40 minutes for research intake.
  • Research is now an essential part of every product milestone.

📄 Source: Actionable Research Toolkit (ART)

📢 We went from research being an afterthought to research being the foundation of decision-making. — Product Manager

2. UX Scorecards: Providing Executives with 30,000-ft Visibility

Before: No standardized UX metrics. Success was subjective, and teams lacked clear pre-launch vs. post-launch comparisons.
Now: UX Scorecards establish a baseline for measuring usability and impact across different products, ensuring benchmarking and iterative improvements.

📊 Key Results:

  • Causes Pre-Launch SUS Score: 58.3 → Post-Launch: 72
  • Real-time UX health tracking for executive decision-making.

📄 Source: RITE Metrics & UX Scorecards

📢 If it’s not measured, it doesn’t exist. UX Scorecards give execs clarity at a 30,000-ft view. — Sr. Dir. of Engineering

3. Faith-Based Research: CVG's Unique SERVE HIM Approach

Before: Research focused primarily on efficiency and usability.
Now: Research is faith-driven, mission-aligned, and designed to serve Jesus Christ’s work. The SERVE HIM model ensures that research is rooted in impact, not just data.

📊 Key Impact:

  • Every study aligns with Jesus-centered UX principles.
  • Research reinforces compassion, trust, and transparency in digital experiences.
  • This approach is changing how we collect feedback from staff in the field, making it easier for them to share what is working and what isn't, without requiring rigid usability feedback forms.
  • For example, we are introducing paper-based surveys for FCP Beta users in Myanmar to bypass bandwidth limitations.

📄 Source: CVG Research Mission & Vision

📢 Our research isn’t just about usability—it’s about faith, service, and impact. — Designer

4. RITE UX Metrics: Answering the Right Questions at the Right Time

Before: UX research lacked a structured measurement system, leading to inconsistent findings and subjective interpretations.
Now: RITE Metrics provide a standardized system for tracking task success, usability pain points, and overall system health, ensuring every iteration is based on real user feedback.

📊 Key Results:

  • Task success rates, SUS, CSAT, and error rates are now standardized across research studies.

B) 📓 Project Specific Updates

1. 🚀 Launching & Refining the Compassion Causes Platform

From Zero to Impact: How Research Shaped Causes’ User Experience

The Vision: A New Way to Give

Compassion Causes was launched to provide donors with an intuitive, transparent, and emotionally compelling platform to support causes aligned with their values. However, building an engaging product from scratch meant answering critical questions:

  • How do donors discover and trust new causes?
  • What drives donation decisions beyond financial contributions?
  • How can we ensure the giving experience feels rewarding and impactful?

Through usability testing, thematic analysis, and UX scorecards, research has shaped, validated, and refined the platform to maximize donor engagement and trust.

Key Research Contributions & Impact

Usability Testing: Eliminating Friction in the Giving Journey

  • Before Research: Pilot usability study with CVG staff showed that users struggled with cause discovery, navigation, and unclear donation flows.
  • Findings:
    • 72% of users expected more intuitive category-based browsing.
    • Urgency indicators (e.g., time-sensitive needs) increased donation intent.
    • Users sought visual storytelling over raw financial breakdowns.
  • Actions Taken:
    • Redesign of IA navigation for seamless cause discovery.

Thematic Analysis: Understanding What Drives Donations

  • Before Research: We lacked data on donor motivations, hesitations, and decision-making behaviors.
  • Findings:
    • Privacy Concerns: Users hesitated due to payment security fears.
    • Emotional Connection Matters: Stories outperformed statistics in persuasion.
    • Clarity is Key: Unclear financial breakdowns reduced donation confidence.
  • Actions Taken:
    • Design work to enhance trust messaging & transparency in fund allocations.
    • Highlight embedded impact stories alongside donation prompts.
    • Re-design infographics for clear fund usage explanations.

📢 Memorable Soundbite:
"Seeing real stories made me feel like I was part of something bigger." – User quote

UX Scorecard: Measuring Success & Continuous Improvement

  • Before Research: No baseline UX metrics for tracking improvements.
  • Findings & Impact:
    • Customer Satisfaction Score (CSAT): 6.1+ (positive baseline).
  • Actions Taken:
    • Optimized mobile workflows & donation process.
    • Prioritized trust-building features to improve NPS & retention.

📢 Memorable Soundbite:
"The Causes platform makes giving simple, but I want to see exactly where my money goes." – User quote

What’s Next? Ensuring Sustainable Growth & Engagement

The launch of Causes was informed by data, guided by user insights, and shaped by research. Moving forward, we will continue:
✅ Expanding impact tracking for donations.
✅ Refining personalization & storytelling to drive deeper engagement.

Final Takeaway: Compassion Causes started with a vision. Research made it a product people trust, use, and will return to.
We didn’t just build a donation platform; we built a trust engine that inspires generosity in Jesus' name.

2) 💕 The Role of Peer-to-Peer (P2P) Fundraising in Compassion Causes

Why Data-Driven Personas Are Critical to Success

Compassion Causes has launched Peer-to-Peer (P2P) Fundraising as an innovative way to expand donor engagement, allowing supporters to fundraise within their networks. However, without data-backed user personas, we risk designing for assumptions rather than real user needs.

To ensure P2P is effective, emotionally resonant, and user-friendly, we conducted quantitative persona research, uncovering key motivations, trust factors, and behavioral trends. This study informs how we build features, shape engagement strategies, and foster trust among new fundraisers.

📢 Critical Insight for Executives:
Personas must be rooted in real data, not assumptions. A well-defined persona model ensures P2P serves the right users with the right experience, leading to higher engagement, trust, and donor conversions.

Extending Our Understanding of Compassion Causes Users Through P2P

Who Are Our P2P Fundraisers?

Our persona research surveyed diverse user segments, providing a data-driven foundation for P2P fundraising. The findings indicate that P2P fundraisers differ significantly from traditional donors:

  • Young, Digital-First Givers: College students and young professionals see P2P as a social movement rather than a transactional act of giving.
  • Faith-Driven Community Builders: Churches and small faith groups leverage P2P for collaborative, mission-driven fundraising.
  • Influencers & Advocates: Social media influencers and cause evangelists drive engagement by leveraging their networks to amplify fundraising efforts.

Key Research Contributions to P2P Fundraising

✅ Trust & Transparency Drive Engagement

  • 70% of respondents hesitate to start a fundraiser unless they clearly see how funds will be used.

✅ Social Proof & Urgency Convert More Donors

  • 65% of fundraisers share campaigns on social media, but only 30% convert without a sense of urgency.

Next Steps for P2P Fundraising Strategy

🔹 Implement social proof & urgency tactics: Highlight matching donations, limited-time campaigns, and social sharing nudges.
🔹 Enhance impact transparency: Real-time donation tracking and progress visualization will increase trust.
🔹 Incorporate gamification: Leaderboards, badges, and friendly challenges will drive higher participation.

Research Point of View (POV): P2P fundraising is not just about money—it’s about mobilizing communities. Data-driven personas ensure we’re designing for engagement, trust, and real-world generosity.

3) ⛪ Launching & Refining the Compassion Community Platform (FCP Beta)

Building a Digital Bridge Between Frontline Church Partners (FCPs) and Donors

The Vision: A New Way to Connect FCPs & Donors

The Compassion Community Platform (FCP Beta) was launched to provide Frontline Church Partners (FCPs) with a digital space to share updates, engage with donors, and showcase their impact in real time. This research-led beta phase focused on understanding user experience, identifying engagement barriers, and optimizing key features to create a seamless, impactful platform.

Through qualitative theme analysis and UX metrics, the research team surfaced real user pain points and opportunities that will inform the platform's next iteration.

Key Research Contributions & Impact

Transparency & Donor Engagement: The Core Strength of the Platform

  • Finding: FCPs value the ability to showcase their work through media-rich updates.
  • Data:
    • CSAT: 6.9 (highest-rated feature).
    • NPS for transparency: 9.1 (strong advocacy).
  • Impact:
    • Users report that real-time updates build donor trust.
    • Direct donor communication is now possible for the first time.

📢 Memorable Soundbite:
"For the first time, FCPs can communicate with donors through a simple social media app." – GH0985

High Data Usage: The Biggest Barrier to Adoption

  • Finding: The platform consumes significantly more data than expected, limiting accessibility in low-connectivity regions.
  • Data:
    • 25% of users reported high data concerns.
    • CSAT for affected users: 6.4 (lower than the 6.7 average).
  • Impact:
    • Streaming and photo uploads need optimization to support users in remote areas.
    • Low-data mode is now a high-priority feature in future releases.

📢 Memorable Soundbite:
"The app uses more data than other social platforms. A low-data mode would help." – GH0347

3. Usability & Engagement Gaps: The Need for Real-Time Notifications & Search

  • Finding: Users struggled with finding content and keeping up with engagement.
  • Data:
    • 38% of users requested search functionality.
    • CSAT for users requesting features: 6.5.
  • Impact:
    • A search function will reduce time wasted scrolling for past updates.
    • Notifications for comments, approvals, and messages will improve real-time interactions.

📢 Memorable Soundbite:
"Scrolling to find comments wastes time. Notifications would make it faster." – GH0121

4. Offline Functionality: Critical for Health Workers in Low-Connectivity Areas

  • Finding: Health Workers need offline features to draft posts and upload updates later.
  • Data:
    • CSAT for Health Workers: 6.2 (satisfaction drops in low-network areas).
    • NPS for offline requesters: 8.0.
  • Impact:
    • An offline mode is now a top development priority to improve accessibility.

📢 Memorable Soundbite:
"If it can be used offline, it will help those without reliable networks." – GH0463

5. Media Upload Failures: Impacting User Experience

  • Finding: Many users faced video and photo upload issues, causing frustration.
  • Data:
    • 29% of users reported failed uploads.
    • CSAT for affected users: 6.5 (lower than 6.9 average).
  • Impact:
    • Optimizing upload success rates will improve engagement.
    • A retry mechanism for failed uploads is being considered.

📢 Memorable Soundbite:
"Uploading videos often fails when the network is poor." – GH0463

Next Steps & Future Enhancements

🔹 Develop a Low-Data Mode: Optimize uploads & media compression.
🔹 Enable Offline Drafting: Allow users to create posts & upload later.
🔹 Implement Notifications: Real-time alerts for comments & approvals.
🔹 Introduce Search & Filters: Improve content discovery & workflow.

🚀 The Compassion Community Platform is transforming how FCPs connect with donors. This research ensures the next iteration is more accessible, efficient, and engaging.

4) 🏦 Supporter Experience for High-Impact Donors

From Early Concept to Data-Driven Design Refinements. These research contributions are shaping a Supporter Experience that is more intuitive, engaging, and aligned with donor expectations.

The Vision: Creating an Engaging Platform for High-Impact Donors

The Supporter Experience within the Compassion Community Platform is being designed to better serve high-impact donors by providing seamless access to impact updates, intuitive navigation, and meaningful engagement with supported communities.

To shape this vision, two critical research studies were conducted:

  1. Information Architecture & Navigation Usability (IA/Nav Test) – Evaluating how easily donors locate and engage with key information.
  2. Impact Timeline A/B Test – Determining the best layout for showcasing impact updates.

These studies helped refine early-stage designs (low-fi & mid-fi prototypes) by providing data-driven insights into donor needs, usability issues, and key areas for improvement.

Key Research Contributions & Impact

Navigation & Information Architecture: Reducing Friction in Donor Experience

  • Finding: Donors struggled with locating updates and understanding content structure.
  • Data:
    • SUS (System Usability Score): 59.2 / 100 (Below industry benchmark of 68).
  • Impact:
    • Navigation was restructured to improve clarity.
    • Login & onboarding flows are being simplified to reduce confusion.
    • Notification preferences are being adjusted to align with donor expectations.

📢 Memorable Soundbite:
"The impact should be more about the mission and not just the growth." – User C6790S124_23

Impact Timeline A/B Test: Selecting the Right Design for Transparency

  • Finding: The vertical timeline outperformed the horizontal timeline in usability and donor engagement.
  • Data:
    • 85% of users preferred the vertical timeline (statistically significant preference).
    • SUS Score:
      • Vertical Timeline: 103.1 (Exceptional usability).
      • Horizontal Timeline: 67.0 (Below usability benchmark of 68).
    • Chi-Squared Test: p < 0.001, confirming strong donor preference for the vertical layout.
  • Impact:
    • Vertical timeline was selected as the default for future iterations.
    • Key improvements were implemented, including clearer event labeling & storytelling elements.

📢 Memorable Soundbite:
"I prefer the vertical design because it allows me to view more information at a glance." – A/B Test Participant.

Next Steps: Refining Mid-Fidelity Designs & Scaling User Testing

🔹 Enhance Navigation Clarity: Simplify content layout & highlight priority updates.
🔹 Implement Vertical Timeline: Further refine storytelling & event labeling.
🔹 Improve Personalization: Introduce donor-specific impact insights.
🔹 Optimize Moderation Clarity: Increase transparency in flagged content decisions.
