🎨 ART Purpose

This template is designed for Product Managers, Designers, and UX Researchers to structure and execute a 4-6 week mixed-methods user research study. Some sections, such as answers to research questions, methods, metrics, and hypotheses, are placeholders. Please collaborate with a UX researcher to finalize these elements.

1. Research Questions (RQ)

What is the problem we are trying to solve?

Examples (replace with your specific context):

  • What barriers prevent users from supporting multiple causes in the app?
  • What features increase user trust in donation workflows?
  • How do users decide between creating a fundraiser or directly donating?

Why solve this problem now?

Examples:

  • User engagement data shows declining adoption of fundraising features.
  • Surveys highlight user trust issues with financial transparency.

What do we know about the problem?

Examples:

  • Existing user personas indicate a lack of clarity in the "Cause Discovery" process.
  • Analytics reveal high abandonment rates during fundraiser setup.

2. Research Goals and Metrics

Goals

Examples:

  • Identify what motivates users to support multiple causes.
  • Refine personas for fundraisers and donors to guide feature prioritization.
  • Develop recommendations to streamline workflows for donations and fundraising.

Metrics

  • HEART Metrics: Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task Success.
  • Survey Metrics: CSAT, NPS, MLP (5-Hearts Likert Scale), UMUX-Lite.
  • Task Metrics: Time on Task, Error Rates, Success Rates.
  • A-B Testing Metrics:
    • Number of causes supported.
    • Total dollars raised per user.

3. Research Methods

Suggested Methods

  • Surveys: Quantitative data on user demographics, motivations, and preferences. MeasuringU: [reference omitted]
  • User Interviews: In-depth exploration of user challenges and motivations. NNG Guide: [reference omitted]
  • Usability Testing: Evaluate app workflows for trust and usability. MeasuringU: [reference omitted]
  • A/B Testing: Test variations in features or workflows. MeasuringU: [reference omitted]
  • MaxDiff Analysis: Prioritize features based on user preferences. NNG Guide: [reference omitted]

Design of the Study

  • Sampling Strategy: Recruit diverse user segments, including fundraisers and donors.
  • Randomization: Ensure balanced representation across demographics.
  • Data Analysis: Use statistical tests for significance (e.g., p < 0.05).

4. Hypotheses

Example Hypotheses

  • Product Hypothesis: Users who see transparency in fund allocation will trust the app more (measured via UMUX-Lite and CSAT).
  • Design Hypothesis: Simplified fundraiser setup workflows will reduce task completion time and errors.
  • A-B Testing Hypothesis 1: Users shown a curated list of causes based on past activity will support more causes (measured by number of causes supported).
  • A-B Testing Hypothesis 2: A progress bar showing the impact of donations will increase the total dollars raised (measured by donation amounts).
  • Mixed Hypothesis: Combining personalized cause suggestions with real-time impact tracking will improve both the number of causes supported and dollars raised.

7. Appendix

Quick Notes on Mixed-Methods Research

Mixed-methods research integrates quantitative (numeric) and qualitative (descriptive) data to provide a holistic view of user behavior and attitudes.

Quantitative Methods

  • Definition: Focuses on measurable data to identify patterns and test hypotheses.
  • Examples: Surveys, A/B Testing, Analytics.
  • Strengths: Generalizable, objective, statistically robust.

Qualitative Methods

  • Definition: Explores user motivations, emotions, and experiences in-depth.
  • Examples: User Interviews, Usability Testing, Focus Groups.
  • Strengths: Rich, detailed, context-driven insights.

Overview of Methods in Mixed-Methods Research

Method Type Purpose Example Output
Surveys Quantitative Gather broad user opinions and trends CSAT, NPS, demographic data
A/B Testing Quantitative Compare two versions of a design/feature Performance metrics (e.g., conversion)
User Interviews Qualitative Explore in-depth user motivations and behavior Thematic insights
Usability Testing Qualitative Identify usability issues in workflows Task success rates, pain points
Focus Groups Qualitative Gather collaborative user insights Group-generated themes
MaxDiff Analysis Quantitative Prioritize features based on preferences Feature ranking

General Persona Questions

  1. Demographics:
    • Age Group: Under 18, 18-24, 25-34, 35-50, 51+.
    • Occupation: Student, Church Leader, Donor, Other.
  2. Product Use:
    • How often do you use the Causes app? (Daily, Weekly, Monthly, Rarely, Never).
    • What features do you find most useful?
  3. Wants and Needs:
    • What motivates you to donate or fundraise? (Personal connection, Urgency, Community involvement, etc.).
    • What challenges do you face when exploring causes?

Example MaxDiff Questions to Quantify User Preferences

  1. Which features are most and least important to you?
    • Easy cause discovery.
    • Social media integration.
    • Clear progress tracking.
  2. Which aspects would most and least motivate you to use the app?
    • Personalized recommendations.
    • Gamification features (badges, milestones).
    • Transparency in fund allocation.

Kanban Tasks for Research Effort

Planning Phase

  • Task: Finalize research goals and questions.
    • Description: Collaborate with team members to define clear research objectives and hypotheses.
    • Status: To Do
    • Owner: PM/UX

  • Task: Develop survey and interview guides.
    • Description: Create structured guides for surveys and user interviews.
    • Status: To Do
    • Owner: Researcher
    • Due Date: Week 1, Wednesday
  • Task: Recruit participants.
    • Description: Identify and onboard a diverse participant pool.
    • Status: To Do
    • Owner: UX Team
    • Due Date: Week 1, Friday

Execution Phase

  • Task: Distribute surveys.
    • Description: Send surveys to participants and collect responses.
    • Status: To Do
    • Owner: UX Team
    • Due Date: Week 2, Monday
  • Task: Conduct initial user interviews.
    • Description: Hold one-on-one interviews to gather qualitative insights.
    • Status: To Do
    • Owner: Researcher
    • Due Date: Week 2, Wednesday
  • Task: Conduct usability tests (Group 1).
    • Description: Observe participants using the app and collect usability data.
    • Status: To Do
    • Owner: UX Team
    • Due Date: Week 3, Wednesday
  • Task: Conduct focus groups.
    • Description: Facilitate group discussions to explore user needs and expectations.
    • Status: To Do
    • Owner: Researcher
    • Due Date: Week 4, Monday

Analysis and Reporting Phase

  • Task: Analyze survey data.
    • Description: Process quantitative data to identify trends and patterns.
    • Status: To Do
    • Owner: Researcher
    • Due Date: Week 2, Friday
  • Task: Summarize usability test results.
    • Description: Compile findings from usability tests to highlight pain points.
    • Status: To Do
    • Owner: Researcher
    • Due Date: Week 3, Friday
  • Task: Finalize research report.
    • Description: Synthesize findings into actionable insights and recommendations.
    • Status: To Do
    • Owner: Researcher
    • Due Date: Week 5, Friday
