😴 Compassion Community: UX Research Strategy Proposal [Apr 2025]

1. Purpose

This research strategy outlines how we will evaluate the Compassion Community User Experience (UX) across four foundational user research pillars: Behavioral, Attitudinal, Emotional, and Adoption (analogous to HEART Metrics). Our aim is to:

  • Provide clear, measurable evidence to support the decision to scale or iterate Compassion Community.
  • Ground all insights in industry-standard UX methods.
  • Communicate research outcomes in a way that’s accessible to executives, PMs, and research newcomers alike.

2. Four Research Pillars (Communication Buckets)

Bucket What We Measure Why It Matters
Behavioral Task success, time on task, DAU/MAU Shows what users actually do and how easily they use the app
Attitudinal CSAT, NPS, ease of use ratings Captures what users say they believe about the experience
Emotional Happiness score, emotional tone Measures how users feel about the experience
Adoption Willingness to reuse, repeat logins Indicates long-term value and user intent to keep engaging
Instrument Behavioral Attitudinal Emotional Adoption
System Usability Scale (SUS) ✅ Task ease, integration of functions ✅ Confidence using the system ❌ (Emotion not directly measured) ✅ Frequent use likelihood
SUPR-Q ✅ Ease of use, navigation (Usability) ✅ Trust in information and interactions ✅ Appearance appeal (Emotion proxy) ✅ Loyalty to return/recommend
UMUX Lite ✅ Meets needs, easy to use ✅ Satisfaction with ease and fit ❌ (Emotion not directly measured) ✅ Future use implied by fit-for-purpose

3. Key Research Questions and Hypotheses

  1. How usable and intuitive is the Community app for FCPs and Sponsors?
  2. Do Sponsors feel emotionally connected and encouraged by the experience?
  3. Are FCPs able to contribute content easily and regularly? (add Q on burden?)
  4. Will Sponsors want to engage again, and would they recommend this experience?
  5. Does this experience amplify impact for Causes (CTR, conversions)?ypo

Appendix

A1. Survey Instruments & Question Bank

System Usability Scale (SUS)

  • 10 standardized items measuring ease, confidence, support needed
  • Used post-training or onboarding

(10 questions, 5-point scale from Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree)

  1. I think that I would like to use this system frequently.
  2. I found the system unnecessarily complex.
  3. I thought the system was easy to use.
  4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system.
  5. I found the various functions in this system were well integrated.
  6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system.
  7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly.
  8. I found the system very cumbersome to use.
  9. I felt very confident using the system.
  10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system.

Score: Converted to 0–100 scale; scores over 68 are considered above average.

SUPR-Q (Standardized User Experience Percentile Rank Questionnaire)

  • 8 items: usability, trust, loyalty, aesthetics
  • Used for broader UX evaluation at midpoint and recheck

(8 questions, 5-point scale from Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree)

Usability

  • The website/app is easy to use.
  • It is easy to navigate within the website/app.

Trust

  • I feel comfortable trusting the information on the website/app.
  • I feel secure in my interactions with the website/app.

Loyalty

  • I am likely to return to the website/app in the future.
  • I would recommend the website/app to a friend.

Appearance

  • I find the website/app to be attractive.
  • The website/app has a clean and simple presentation.

Score: SUPR-Q benchmarked percentile ranks across usability, trust, loyalty, and appearance.

Key TakeAways

  1. 🧪 Research-Based Development: The SUPR-Q (Standardized User Experience Percentile Rank Questionnaire) was developed by analyzing 17 existing UX questionnaires, identifying 33 candidate items, and refining them through multiple studies involving over 4,000 responses across 100+ websites. (10 Things to Know About the SUPR-Q - MeasuringU)
  2. 💠 High Reliability: The SUPR-Q demonstrates strong internal consistency, with a Cronbach's alpha of 0.86 for the overall UX quality measure. Subfactors like usability, appearance, trust/credibility, and loyalty also show acceptable reliability scores ranging from 0.64 to 0.88. (
  3. ⚖️ Validated Instrument: The SUPR-Q has high content validity, convergent validity (correlating well with the System Usability Scale and other similar instruments), and discriminant validity (effectively differentiating between high and low-quality websites).
  4. 🔍 Measures Four UX Factors: The questionnaire assesses four key aspects of user experience: usability, appearance, trust/credibility, and loyalty.
  5. 📊 Percentile-Based Scoring: SUPR-Q scores are presented as percentile ranks, making them intuitive to interpret. A score of 50 represents the average, with higher scores indicating better performance relative to other websites.
  6. 📈 Benchmarking Database: The SUPR-Q is supported by a normalized database of approximately 150 websites, updated quarterly. This allows organizations to compare their scores against industry benchmarks, including well-known sites like Amazon and Netflix. (
  7. Includes Net Promoter Score (NPS): The SUPR-Q incorporates the 11-point likelihood-to-recommend item, enabling the calculation of NPS alongside other UX metrics.
  8. Predicts SUS Scores: The SUPR-Q's usability factor correlates highly (r = 0.87) with the System Usability Scale (SUS), allowing for the prediction of SUS scores based on SUPR-Q data.
  9. ⚠️ Not Diagnostic: While the SUPR-Q provides a broad measure of user experience quality, it is not designed to diagnose specific usability issues. For detailed insights, additional methods like usability testing or expert reviews are recommended.
  10. 🍞 Widely Adopted: The SUPR-Q is utilized by hundreds of organizations worldwide, including companies in eCommerce, travel, government services, and education, to assess and benchmark their website user experiences. (SUPR-Q® Full License - MeasuringU)

For more detailed information, you can refer to the full article on MeasuringU: 10 Things to Know About the SUPR-Q.

UMUX Lite (Usability Metric for User Experience Lite)

  • 2 items: ease of use, capability alignment
  • Used for lightweight usability checks (Sponsors)

(2 questions, 7-point scale from Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree)

  1. [System Name]’s capabilities meet my requirements.
  2. [System Name] is easy to use.

Score: Converted to 0–100 scale; strong correlation with SUS and SUPR-Q with much lower respondent burden.

Notes:

  • For Compassion Community testing:
    • Use text like “Compassion Community experience” instead of just “system” for clarity.
    • Prefer UMUX Lite when survey length needs to stay minimal.
    • Use full SUS when more comprehensive usability diagnosis is needed (training phase or onboarding).
    • Consider SUPR-Q if you also want to benchmark trust and loyalty dimensions alongside usability.
    • Example Plot of Usability vs. Usefulness and a comparative grid across major consumer products

Optional Stretch Question (Post-CSAT/NPS)

"What would have made you rate your experience higher?"

Purpose:

  • Captures qualitative, actionable user expectations beyond initial satisfaction.
  • Mitigates "shiny new toy" bias during early excitement phases.
