🕸️ Design of the Persona Naming Survey: Quantitative Approach & Methodology

  • The persona naming survey is designed to ensure that the chosen names accurately reflect the motivations, behaviors, and identities of high-impact donors.
  • The survey integrates quantitative research methodologies to collect statistically reliable insights while minimizing biases in name selection.

Survey Objectives

The survey was created to:

  1. Identify the most resonant persona names based on donor identity and engagement patterns.
  2. Quantify preferences to determine statistical significance in name selection.
  3. Analyze segmentation trends across different donor demographics.
  4. Ensure validity and reliability of persona names by reducing selection bias.

Survey Structure & Design Considerations

1. Selection Criteria for Persona Names

  • Respondents were presented with a list of candidate names for each persona.
  • Each name was accompanied by a brief description to ensure respondents fully understood the persona’s role.
  • The survey was designed to test six personas, covering various donor motivations and engagement styles.

2. Question Types & Quantitative Measurement

The survey consisted of two key question formats:

(A) Ranking Questions (Forced Choice Methodology)

  • Respondents ranked their top choices among a list of persona names.
  • This method forces prioritization, reducing noise from neutral responses.
  • Example: "Rank the following persona names in order of most to least representative of high-impact donors."

(B) Likert Scale Ratings (Preference Scoring)

  • Each persona name was rated using a 5-point Likert scale (1 = "Strongly Dislike" to 5 = "Strongly Like").
  • This allowed us to quantify sentiment strength across a larger sample.
  • Scores were averaged to identify statistically significant preferences.

3. Sampling & Data Collection Approach

  • Sample Size: The survey targeted a representative sample of high-impact donor managers.
  • Segmentation: Respondents were categorized by age, digital engagement, and faith orientation.
  • Distribution Method: The survey was administered online, ensuring standardized responses.
  • Randomization: The order of persona names was randomized per respondent to mitigate order bias.

Quantitative Analysis & Insights

1. Statistical Techniques Used

To extract meaningful insights, the following quantitative methods were applied:

  • Mean & Standard Deviation Analysis: Used to compare sentiment scores across persona names.
  • Chi-Square Tests: Applied to assess whether preferences varied significantly across different age groups.
  • Factor Analysis: Identified latent patterns in how donors associated with persona characteristics.
  • Regression Modeling: Explored whether faith orientation or digital comfort levels predicted name preferences.

2. Key Findings from Quantitative Analysis

  • "Legacy Investor" received the highest average ranking (M=2.24, SD=0.97), making it the strongest candidate.
  • Faith-driven respondents showed a statistically significant preference (p < 0.05) for "Faith-Driven Steward".
  • Younger respondents (<40) preferred "Cause-Driven Donor" over "Legacy Investor" (p = 0.03).
  • Randomization testing confirmed no significant order bias in ranking choices (p > 0.05).

Conclusion & Next Steps

  1. The persona naming survey successfully provided data-driven insights into the most effective persona names for high-impact donors.
  2. Moving forward, additional A/B testing on messaging and validating persona names in user interviews will ensure that these selections remain accurate in real-world engagement scenarios.
