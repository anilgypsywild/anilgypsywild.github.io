Research Brief: Lifecycle Trust and Giving Impact Study

Compassion Causes

Progress is tracked here: ciorg/venturesgroup/uxrepo-v1#205

Overview

  • This study examines how supporters expect Compassion to respond when a Cause can no longer proceed as originally planned due to real world conditions.
  • These situations represent high trust moments that can influence donor confidence, future giving behavior, and irreversible product decisions.
  • Rather than evaluating features or copy, this research focuses on supporter expectations, preference tradeoffs, and trust preservation when a Cause ends.

Importantly, this study assumes that Compassion will apply one consistent fund handling approach regardless of why the Cause ended. The goal is to understand supporter expectations to inform that single default approach and its surrounding communication.

Why This Research Now

As Compassion Causes expands its roadmap with lifecycle features such as Cause deletion, Cause expiration, and compliance driven shutdowns on the FCP side, it is critical to understand:

  1. What supporters expect Compassion to do by default when a Cause ends
  2. Which responses preserve trust versus create avoidable risk
  3. Where supporter tolerance breaks down
  4. Which actions require explicit consent versus safe automation
  5. How communication expectations shape acceptance of system decisions

This research informs Q3 sequencing, engineering defaults, and messaging guardrails before irreversible lifecycle behaviors are encoded into the platform.

Core Research Questions

Primary Question

What do supporters expect Compassion to do with their donation when a Cause can no longer proceed?

Supporting Questions

  1. Which fund handling actions most preserve trust
  2. Which actions create reputational or trust risk
  3. How expectations differ across Cause ending circumstances, even if outcomes do not change
  4. How preferences relate to future giving intent
  5. Which actions should always be opt in rather than automatic

What This Research Is and Is Not

✔️ This Research Is

• A scenario framed preference study
• Focused on supporter expectations and decision tradeoffs
• Designed to produce ranked, interpretable outputs for Product and Engineering

❌ This Research Is Not

• Feature validation
• Copy testing
• Pricing optimization
• Policy determination

Survey/Study Design Summary

Element Description
Method Scenarioframed survey withMaxDiff (best worst choice)
Platform Lyssna
Estimated Time 8–10 minutes
Sample Size ~30 participants
Scenarios Single framing with multiple circumstances described
Primary Output Ranked preference utilities for fund handling actions

Scenario Framing

Participants are not assigned to different outcomes.
All participants receive the same framing, emphasizing that Compassion will respond consistently regardless of circumstance.

Cause Ending Circumstances Shown to Participants

1) Church Removes the Cause (FCP Delete)

The local church removes a Cause because it can no longer be implemented. Examples include timing constraints, environmental issues, staffing changes, or replacing the Cause with a new one.

2) Compliance or Security Issue (AML)

Compassion identifies a compliance or security risk and ends partnership with a church, so funds can no longer be distributed to that Cause.

3) Cause Expiration

After an extended period, such as 6 to 8 months, the Cause is still not fully funded, so Compassion automatically expires and removes the Cause.

Participants are informed that the same fund handling approach will apply in all cases and are asked to evaluate their preferences with that understanding.

Preference Measurement (MaxDiff)

Participants complete a best worst choice task evaluating how Compassion should handle funds when a Cause ends.

🧺 Revised Lifecycle Action Set

  1. Automatically redirect my donation to a similar Cause in the same category
  2. Automatically redirect my donation to a similar Cause in the same region or country
  3. If available, automatically redirect my donation to another Cause at the same church
  4. Allow me to select a new Cause of my choice
  5. Move my donation to the Finishline Fund to help other Causes reach completion

Refund is intentionally not offered. Preference signals for refund desire are inferred indirectly through open ended feedback.

🤔 Why MaxDiff

MaxDiff forces tradeoffs and produces stable, rank ordered preferences without scale bias. This enables clear differentiation between safe defaults, acceptable automation, and actions that require explicit supporter control.

Communication Expectations

After the MaxDiff tasks, participants indicate how they expect Compassion to communicate when a Cause ends.

Options include:

  1. No notification needed
  2. Email explanation
  3. Account notification at next login
  4. Personal outreach explaining what happened
  5. Other (open ended)

This separates fund handling preferences from communication expectations, informing service and messaging design.

Donation Amount Sensitivity

Rather than pricing optimization, this study uses a directional sensitivity check.

Participants are asked whether their preferences would change if their donation were $1,000 or more.

Response options capture expectations for:

• Greater control
• More proactive communication
• Personal outreach
• No change in expectations

This avoids anchoring bias while still informing tiered experience design.

Participants

Target segments include:

• Current donors
• Advocate or peer to peer fundraisers
• Potential supporters

Lightweight demographics are collected for segmentation only.

🧪 Analysis Plan

Primary analyses include:

  1. MaxDiff utility scores for each fund handling action
  2. Identification of preferred defaults versus risky actions
  3. Trust and future giving intent by preferred action
  4. Communication expectations by preference cluster
  5. High donation sensitivity patterns

Open ended responses will contextualize quantitative findings.

🍦 Expected Outcomes

This research will directly inform:

  1. Cause lifecycle defaults
  2. Donor communication requirements
  3. Trust preservation guardrails
