Compassion Causes Homepage Concept Test: Trust and First Impressions

  • Prepared for: @SLamb2 , @CDant , @AThompson8
  • Date: Oct 30, 2025
  • Lyssna Results Link: [reference omitted]

Research Objective

Evaluate first-impression trust and motivation for Compassion Causes homepage designs using a five-second exposure. The study quantifies which visual, emotional, and copy elements communicate Dependability (Trust), Doability (Clarity), Desire (Motivation), and Delight (Emotional Resonance) on the CVG 4D UX Scorecard.

0. Home page Concepts

ID Description Intent
A Current Causes homepage Baseline
B “Let’s Help Things Together” (Intentionally Bad Design) Catch trial / data quality control
C “Assumed Trust” layout New trust-focused concept
D “Trust at a Glance” layout New trust-cue emphasis concept

1. Problem We Are Trying to Solve

  • Recent CVG research shows that Supporters’ initial impressions of the Compassion Causes homepage heavily influence trust, donation likelihood, and continued engagement.
  • The current homepage (A) performs adequately, but lacks validated data on visual trust cues and clarity of purpose compared to newer concepts (C and D).
  • We also need to confirm whether the Assumed Trust and Trust at a Glance designs deliver measurable improvements in perceived reliability and motivation to act.

2. Why We Are Solving This Problem

Compassion Causes’ homepage must immediately convey credibility, clarity of mission, and invitation to act.. Current designs perform adequately but lack empirical validation of how trust, visual warmth, and emotional cues drive engagement.

3. Why This Matters

  • First-impression trust determines donation intent and supporter retention.
  • Quantifying emotional resonance ensures design and copy reflect Compassion’s mission authentically.
  • Valid data from this five-second test enables evidence-based creative direction for Q1 2026 homepage rollout.

4. What We Will Do With the Research Insights:

  • Identify which new homepage design drives the highest Trust, Desire, and Delight.
  • Use quantitative trust and emotional resonance metrics to guide design rollout decisions.
  • Share findings via the 4D UX Scorecard dashboard to inform product, design, and marketing strategy for Causes’ next iteration. (More about CVG 4D-UX here..)

Research Study Design

Method:

  • Unmoderated Ten-Second Test on Lyssna: [reference omitted]
  • U.S. adults (25–65) familiar with online giving
  • Mix of genders, devices, and prior donation experience
  • Minimum N = 50 per concept for reliable t-tests / ANOVA
  • Order: Counterbalanced (A, B, C, D randomized; B always followed by attention-check questions)
    • See more on Counterbalancing for UX research here....

Script for Lyssna

Participant Instructions

You’ll see a Compassion Causes page for ten seconds.
After it disappears, answer a few questions about what stood out and how it made you feel.
There are no right or wrong answers — we just want your honest first impression.

Question Flow (per concept)

  1. Recall: “In one sentence, what was this page about?” (Open text → Doability)
  2. Purpose clarity: “What do you think this page was asking you to do?” (Multiple choice → Doability)
  3. Emotion: “How did this page make you feel?” (Hopeful, Inspired, Neutral, Confused, Distrusting → Delight)
  4. Mission clarity: “How clearly did this page communicate that this is part of Compassion’s mission to release children from poverty in Jesus’ name?” (7-point scale → Dependability)
  5. Credibility: “How credible or genuine did this page feel as a Christian child-focused charity?” (7-point scale → Dependability)
  6. Motivation: “After seeing this page, how motivated would you feel to explore or give?” (7-point scale → Desire)
  7. Recognition check: “Which of these elements do you remember seeing?” (Select all that apply → Attention / Doability)

Catch Trial (Concept B only):

  • “Which headline was on this page?” (multiple-choice accuracy check)
  • “Did you see a yellow button that asked you to start helping?” (yes/no accuracy check)

Comparative Questions (after all concepts)

  • “Which page felt most trustworthy and inspiring?” (Dependability + Desire)
  • “Why did you choose that one?” (Open text)
  • “Which page looked least credible or confusing?” (Dependability, validity check)

Demographics Block

  1. Age group (for Current-Gen < 34 vs. Next-Gen ≥ 34 segmentation)
  2. Giving frequency (Never / Occasionally / Every few months / Monthly+)
  3. Familiarity with Compassion Causes
  4. Causes that inspire you (Select up to two)
  5. Seasonal giving behavior (Holiday / Faith-based / Year-round / Event-driven / Unsure)
  6. Device used
  7. Gender
  8. Role in giving decisions (optional)

4D UX Scorecard Mapping

4D Pillar Metric Question Source Measurement Type
Doability Task & purpose clarity Q1–Q2 % accurate recall, qualitative coding
Dependability Credibility + Mission clarity Q4–Q5 Mean 7-pt scale, t-tests / ANOVA
Desire Motivation to give + Preferred concept Q6 + Comparative Mean 7-pt scale, choice share
Delight Emotional tone + Visual appeal Q3 + Comparative % positive emotion, preference count

Success Criteria

  • Dependability: Trust score ≥ 20% higher for C or D vs. A.
  • Doability: ≥ 80% can correctly describe page purpose.
  • Desire: ≥ 50% report motivation to explore or give.
  • Delight: ≥ 60% choose positive emotions (hopeful / inspired).

Analysis Plan

  • Within-launch: Paired t-tests on Dependability, Desire, and Doability indices.
  • Across launches: Mixed ANOVA (Concept × Launch) for tracking visual trust evolution.
  • Emotion: Chi-square on positive vs. negative tone.
  • Preference: Binomial or multinomial logistic regression (Concept × Device × Generation).

Deliverables

  • Quantitative comparison tables for each concept (trust, clarity, motivation, emotion)
  • 4D Scorecard summary visualization for CVG dashboard
  • Qualitative insights (quotes + themes)
  • Recommendations for creative direction and homepage rollout (Q1 2026)
