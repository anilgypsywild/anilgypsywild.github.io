Email Engagement Optimization Research Brief

June 2025 – Causes & Community Platforms

1. 🔍 Purpose

  1. To evaluate the usability, comprehension, emotional engagement, and CTA clarity across four critical email templates used in the Compassion Causes platform.
  2. These templates form the backbone of user communication and donor engagement.
  3. The study will use a mixed-methods design, combining qualitative usability feedback with quantitative metrics such as SUS and MWTP.

✉️ Email Templates Being Tested

  1. Featured Cause – Recommendation Email
    • 🎯 Goal: Re-engagement (donate again)
    • Measures: Clicks, comprehension, MWTP, SUS
  2. Monthly Newsletter
    • 🎯 Goal: Re-engagement (explore causes, inspire action)
    • Measures: Comprehension, scan patterns, intent to explore, MWTP
  3. About Compassion (Cause Ecosystem Explanation)
    • 🎯 Goal: Inform & guide toward sponsorship
    • Measures: Clarity of message, CTA interpretation, sponsorship interest
  4. Progress Update (Fundraiser + Cause)
    • 🎯 Goal: Emotional and factual update retention
    • Measures: Comprehension of context, reengagement cues, recognition of entities

🧪 Methodology

  • Platform: Lyssna (5-second test + task flows)
  • Participants: 20 participants total
    • 👥 10 from the NextGen (18–34)
    • 👥 5 from Middle Stage (35–54)
    • 👥 5 Active Donors (FCP/HID overlap)
  • Format: Mixed-methods (task-based usability + survey)

📊 Metrics & Instruments

  • ✅ System Usability Scale (SUS) per email
  • ✅ Comprehension tasks: e.g., "What do you expect happens next?"
  • ✅ Marginal Willingness to Pay (MWTP): Estimated re-donation potential
  • ✅ CTA path mapping: Confidence and clarity ratings
  • ✅ Qualitative feedback: Pain points, emotional resonance, areas of confusion

🧩 Tasks Per Template

Each email will be followed by:

  • 1–2 multiple-choice comprehension checks
  • 1 rating of usability (SUS or UMUX-Lite scale)
  • 1 rating of emotional resonance (e.g., "How moved or inspired were you?")
  • 1 MWTP scenario-based question ("Would you donate $X to this cause?")

❓ Research Questions

  • Do users recognize what the email is about?
  • Do they understand the primary action (CTA)?
  • Do they feel trust, clarity, or inspiration?
  • Which content blocks are most/least effective?

2. 📎 Appendix: Using Marginal Willingness to Pay (MWTP) in the Email Engagement Study

🔍 What is MWTP?

Marginal Willingness to Pay (MWTP) measures how much a user is willing to give in response to a piece of content — in this case, an email. Unlike generic engagement or intent ratings, MWTP translates emotional or persuasive impact into donation-dollar intent.

In this study, it complements usability and comprehension tasks by showing how persuasive each email design is, especially in driving repeat giving.

💲 Why It Matters for Email UX

  • Most donor emails fail not because of technical usability, but because of emotional ambiguity.
  • MWTP reveals which templates lead a user to feel:
    • Trust in the cause
    • Recognition of impact
    • Willingness to give again

MWTP answers:

“How much would you give after seeing this email — and why?”

⚙️ How It’s Used in This Study

Each email block in the Lyssna test includes:

Q: Imagine you had $25 to give this month. Based on this email, how much would you be willing to give to support this cause?

  • $0
  • $5
  • $10
  • $15
  • $20
  • $25
  • More than $25
  • I would not give again

Follow-Up:

Why did you choose that amount? (Open text)

Optional follow-ups:

  • “What made you hesitate or feel confident in giving again?”
  • “Was anything missing that would increase your gift?”

📊 Example Data (Simulated)

Email Template Mean MWTP % Selected $0 % Gave ≥ $25 Insights
Featured Cause $15.25 10% 25% Strong intent to act — CTA and urgency aligned
Monthly Newsletter $12.00 20% 10% High scan rate but low specific intent
About Compassion $13.75 15% 18% Curiosity high, trust not fully converted

💡 How MWTP Informs Design

  • CTA testing: Are the buttons persuasive enough to convert?
  • Copy clarity: Does the message move users toward generosity?
  • Emotional sequencing: Are photos or testimonials strengthening MWTP?

MWTP helps prioritize what to improve:

  • 🟥 Low MWTP + high comprehension → Improve emotional resonance
  • 🟨 High MWTP + low comprehension → Improve clarity
  • 🟩 High MWTP + high comprehension → Template is ready to scale

📎 Summary

MWTP is a lightweight but powerful proxy for:

  • Content effectiveness
  • Donor activation
  • Emotional return on UX design

It turns abstract UX feedback into a dollar-based signal that bridges design, content strategy, and donor behavior.

3. 📄 Full Usability Test Script – Email Engagement Optimization

Compassion Causes – Lyssna Study (June 2025)

🧭 Test Format Overview

  • Platform: Lyssna (Unmoderated)
  • Templates tested: 4 (Featured Cause, About Compassion, Newsletter, Progress Update)
  • Total time per participant: ~15–20 minutes
  • Audience: Supporters (Next Gen, Mid-stage, FCP/HID)
  • Structure: 4 repeated blocks (1 per email), randomized order

Test Flow (Per Email Block)

1️⃣ Instruction

“Please review the following email as if it was sent to you directly. After reading it, we’ll ask you a few questions about what it’s saying and what you might do next.”

2️⃣ 5-Second Test (First Impressions)

"You’ll see this email for 5 seconds. Don’t overthink it. Just tell us:"

Q: “What do you think this email was about?”
Response Type: Open text

3️⃣ First Click Task

“Where would you click first if you were going to take action in this email?”

Response Type: Click-based heatmap capture (Lyssna image)

4️⃣ Comprehension Check (Multiple Choice)

“What do you think this email wants you to do?”

Response Type: Multiple Choice (options depend on template)
Examples:

  • Donate to a related cause
  • Learn about Compassion’s mission
  • Read a project update
  • Share the cause with others

5️⃣ CTA Clarity (Optional)

“Which of these buttons would you most likely click? Why?”
Response Type: Open text or match-to-CTA labeling

6️⃣ Engagement Rating

Q: “How likely are you to engage with this email in real life?”
Scale: 1–7 (Not at all likely → Very likely)

💰 MWTP Section (Per Email)

“Imagine you had $25 to give this month. Based on this email, how much would you consider donating to this cause or project?”

Options:

  • $0
  • $5
  • $10
  • $15
  • $20
  • $25
  • More than $25
  • I would not give again

Q: “Why did you choose that amount?” (Open text)

✅ SUS Questions (All Email Templates – End of Test)

Ask these once after all four emails have been shown:

  • “I thought the emails were easy to understand.”
  • “I felt confident using these emails to take action.”
  • “The layout made it easy to find what I needed.”
  • “These emails were consistent and well-structured.”

Scale: 1–5 (Strongly Disagree → Strongly Agree)

😊 CSAT + NPS Section (End of Study)

Q: CSAT – Overall Satisfaction

“How satisfied are you with these emails as a way to stay connected with Compassion Causes?”
Scale: 1–5 (Very Dissatisfied → Very Satisfied)

Q: NPS – Email Series Recommendation

“How likely would you be to recommend these emails to a friend or fellow supporter?”
Scale: 0–10 (Not at all likely → Extremely likely)

✍️ Final Open Feedback (Optional)

“Is there anything else you’d like us to know about these emails — what you liked, what confused you, or what you’d change?”

Response Type: Open text

4. Mapping 4D UX Scorecard to Email Engagement Study

This appendix maps each UX4D pillar (Doability, Dependability, Delight, Desire) to the methods and metrics used in the Email Engagement Optimization Study for Compassion Causes and Community.

✅ 1. Doability (Behavioral)

“Can users actually do the thing we built?”

Metric Email Study Method
✅ Task Success First Click Task + CTA Interpretation
⏱️ Time on Task Not used (unmoderated format)
❌ Error Rate Qualitative via open-ended task feedback
❓ Assist Requests Not applicable (no moderator), captured via confusion tags

→ Measured through comprehension tasks and first-click behavior.

🧠 2. Dependability (Attitudinal)

“Do users feel like this is reliable, usable, and trustworthy?”

Metric Email Study Method
📋 System Usability Scale (SUS) SUS questions after all templates
🌟 CSAT Included at end of study
📣 NPS Recommendation question at end of test
✔️ Workflow Completion Click success and CTA matching per email
🗣️ Confidence Ratings Captured via Likert in SUS and MWTP follow-up

→ Captured via post-task surveys and aggregate email ratings.

❤️ 3. Delight (Emotional)

“How do people feel when using the experience?”

Metric Email Study Method
🙂 Emotion Smiley Scale Replaced by 1–7 “How moved/inspired were you?” scale
🧠 Feeling Scale (Words) Optional open-ended follow-up to MWTP
🧾 Open-Ended Comments Used after each block
🎨 Appearance Trust Captured via SUS + MWTP intent
🎤 Spontaneous Reactions Not used (asynchronous test format)

→ Measured through resonance ratings, open feedback, and MWTP reasoning.

🔁 4. Desire (Adoption)

“Will they come back? Will they keep using it?”

Metric Email Study Method
🔁 Repeat Logins Not directly applicable in email, but click intent is a proxy
❤️ Likelihood to Reuse Captured through engagement scale + MWTP
🔄 Willingness to Recommend NPS question at end of study
💡 Feature Recall Indirectly measured via comprehension and CTA clarity
💬 What Made You Come Back? Explored via open-ended MWTP follow-up

→ Measured through intent to act, MWTP donation values, and stated recommendation.

📘 Summary Mapping Table

UX4D Pillar Captured via
Doability First click, comprehension tasks
Dependability SUS, CSAT, NPS
Delight Emotional resonance scale, open text
Desire MWTP, click intent, reuse questions

This mapping ensures full alignment with the research team’s standardized UX4D framework while supporting agile, test-driven insights for email optimization.
