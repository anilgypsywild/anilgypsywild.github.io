Causes Donation Funnel Analysis

Behavioral Analysis of the Core Donation Workflow (GA4)

  • Property: compassioncauses.com
  • Report Type: GA4 Funnel Exploration (Analytics 360)
  • Date Range: April 1, 2025 – January 15, 2026

Purpose

To analyze real donor behavior across the core Causes donation workflow and identify where users hesitate, abandon, or proceed, using longitudinal GA data as the primary evidence source.

1. Funnel Overview

This analysis examines the primary donation funnel defined as:

  1. Session Start
  2. Screen or Page View
  3. Donation Intent
  4. Checkout Start
  5. Donation Complete

The funnel is configured as open, reflecting natural user behavior rather than a forced linear flow.

Over the nine month period, the funnel captures 6,544 sessions, providing sufficient volume to observe stable behavioral patterns across devices.

2. High Level Funnel Performance

Across all users:

  • 6,544 sessions began
  • 480 users (7.33%) expressed Donation Intent
  • 213 users (3.25%) started Checkout
  • 132 users (~2.0%) completed a Donation

The most significant loss occurs before donation intent is ever formed, with over 92% of users disengaging between page view and intent.

🛤️ This indicates that most visitors consume content but do not reach a decision point.

3. Step by Step Behavioral Analysis

✈️ Step 1 → Step 2

Session Start → Screen/Page View

  • Completion rate is effectively 100%.
  • This step confirms that users are successfully landing on and viewing Causes pages. There is no measurable friction at entry.

🤔 Step 2 → Step 3

Screen/Page View → Donation Intent

This is the primary point of abandonment.

  • Only 7.33% of users progress to Donation Intent
  • 92.67% abandon at this stage
  • This pattern indicates pre commitment hesitation, not usability failure.

Users are present, reading, and browsing, but do not yet feel ready to give.

This stage represents the moment where users ask implicit questions around trust, relevance, urgency, and clarity of impact.

🛒 Step 3 → Step 4

Donation Intent → Checkout Start

Among users who express intent:

  • 44.38% proceed to checkout
  • 55.63% abandon

This suggests that even after signaling intent, over half of users hesitate before entering the transactional flow, indicating residual doubt or friction related to commitment.

💰 Step 4 → Step 5

Checkout Start → Donation Complete

  • 61.97% of checkout starters complete a donation
  • 38.03% abandon during checkout

Relative to earlier steps, checkout performance is moderately strong, particularly given nonprofit norms.

This confirms that checkout is not the dominant bottleneck in the overall funnel.

🧨 4. Device Level Differences (Critical Insight)

Desktop Users

  • Donation Intent Rate: 5.97%
  • Checkout Start from Intent: 22.44%
  • Donation Completion from Checkout: 35.71%
  • Overall completion rate: ~1.3% of desktop sessions

Desktop users show consistent hesitation at both intent formation and checkout entry, suggesting deliberation, trust seeking, or risk assessment behaviors.

Mobile Users

  • Donation Intent Rate: 12.83%
  • Checkout Start from Intent: 84.94%
  • Donation Completion from Checkout: 74.47%
  • Overall completion rate: ~10.9% of mobile sessions

Mobile users are significantly more decisive once intent is formed, and substantially more likely to complete the donation.

🔎 Interpretation

Device context strongly influences giving behavior:

  • Mobile supports emotionally driven, decisive generosity
  • Desktop supports analytical, slower decision making

These patterns are stable across the nine month window and should be treated as baseline behavioral characteristics.

💵 Executive Summary

  1. The dominant loss occurs before donation intent, not during checkout.
  2. Improving checkout alone will have limited impact on total donations.
  3. Meaning, trust, and clarity are the primary conversion constraints.
  4. Mobile performance suggests strong downstream mechanics once users commit.
  5. Desktop behavior indicates unresolved hesitation even after intent is expressed.

Appendix

⚠️ What This Analysis Does and Does Not Claim

This report:

  • Describes what users are doing
  • Identifies where they stop progressing
  • Establishes quantitative baselines

This report does not:

  • Diagnose specific design flaws
  • Prescribe UX solutions
  • Attribute causality beyond observed behavior

Those require dedicated research.
