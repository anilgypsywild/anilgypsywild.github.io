Results from Peer-to-Peer Fundraising Pilot Survey

Sample Size: 170

This research brief outlines the objectives and key findings of a pilot survey conducted to gather feedback on peer-to-peer (P2P) fundraising habits among Compassion International users. The study focused on five key personas and segmented participants into two generational groups: Next-Gen (age ≤ 34) and Current-Gen (age > 34).

The primary goal of the survey was to refine our understanding of user behavior, motivations, and platform preferences related to P2P fundraising. Insights from this research will guide actionable recommendations for Product Management, Design, and Engineering teams as they work to optimize fundraising tools and experiences.

Additionally, the survey results highlight areas for improving future iterations of survey design and methodology to ensure comprehensive and relevant data collection.

Survey Questions on UserZoom

Screener

  1. Do you identify as a Christian or practice Christianity? 
  2. Have you ever donated to a charitable organization?
  3. Have you ever fundraised for a cause? 

Questionnaire

Demographics

  1. Please let us know the country and region where you live.
  2. What’s your age group?
  3. How do you describe yourself?
  4. What’s your highest level of education?
  5. What’s your household income?

Compassion Use, Persona

  1. How often do you engage with Compassion Causes or similar platforms to support causes?
  2. Do you prefer giving monthly to ongoing causes or making one-time contributions to specific needs?
  3. Which of the following best describes your role? (e.g., Influencer or Content Creator, Church or Organization Leader, Individual Supporter)

Fundraising Habits

  1. What motivates you to start a fundraising campaign? For example, do you feel inspired by a specific need, a personal connection to the cause, or the ability to bring a community together?
  2. How often does your church participate in fundraising?
  3. How do you usually promote your fundraising campaigns? (Choose all that apply.)
  4. When setting a fundraising goal, do you base it on how much you think you can raise or on the amount needed for a cause you care about?
  5. How much time do you typically spend setting up a fundraising campaign?
  6. What tools or metrics do you find most helpful in tracking the success of your campaigns (e.g., funds raised, donors reached, or specific organizational tools provided)?

Here’s a reordered version of the questions with clearer section titles to enhance logical flow and readability:

Supporting Multiple Causes

  1. What motivates you to support more than one cause at a time? For example, is it your personal connection to each cause, their urgency, or something else?

Fundraising Habits

  1. What motivates you/your group to fundraise?
  2. What types of events does your group prefer for fundraising?
  3. What tools or resources would make it easier for you/your church or organization to organize fundraisers?
  4. How often do you communicate with the causes or organizations you support?
  5. How often do you use social media to promote causes or campaigns?
  6. How often do you use analytics tools (e.g., likes, shares, views) to evaluate the reach or success of your campaigns?
  7. What do you look for in a fundraising platform?
  8. Please select all Peer-to-Peer fundraising platforms that you are aware of or have used for a fundraising campaign.
  9. Please select your favorite Peer-to-Peer fundraising platform from this list.
  10. Please tell us why.

Persuasion Factors

  1. What would encourage you to increase your fundraising goal?
  2. What would persuade you to fundraise for multiple causes?
  3. What makes you trust a platform like Compassion Causes to give more? (e.g., transparency in fund usage, personal recommendations, or secure transactions)

Emotional Resonance

  1. What emotionally resonates with you most when choosing a cause to support?
  2. What type of content resonates most with your audience?
  3. How satisfied are you with Compassion?

Trust and Feedback

  1. How likely is it that you would recommend Compassion to a friend or colleague?
  2. Is there anything else you’d like to share to help us grow and serve better?

Sandbox Testing Results

Personas Identified

  1. Supporting Church/Organization:
    • Represents churches or larger organizations contributing as a group.
  2. Regular Supporter/Individual Contributor:
    • Individuals who consistently contribute to Compassion Causes.
  3. Major Donor:
    • High-value contributors who donate significant amounts.
  4. Influencer/Advocate:
    • Individuals who leverage their networks to promote and support causes.
  5. College/University Student:
    • Younger contributors typically associated with educational institutions.

Generational Segmentation

  • Next-Gen (age ≤ 34):
    • Focuses on younger contributors, likely more tech-savvy and connected to modern fundraising methods like social media campaigns.
  • Current-Gen (age > 34):
    • Older contributors with potentially higher financial stability and traditional giving habits.

Insights by Personas

Survey Result Interpretation

1. Supporting a Church or Organization:

  • The 55+ age group shows a significant proportion of Promoters (Blue), indicating strong advocacy and satisfaction.
  • Younger age groups (18–24) have fewer Promoters, suggesting engagement challenges with this demographic.

2. Regular Supporter or Individual Contributor:

  • The 35–44 and 45–54 age groups show a balanced mix of Promoters and Passives, indicating moderate satisfaction with room for improvement.
  • Detractors are more prominent among younger (18–24) and older (55+) age groups.

3. Major Donor:

  • The majority of Promoters are concentrated in the 45–54 and 55+ age groups, reflecting higher satisfaction among older donors.
  • Younger age groups (18–34) have a notable presence of Passives and Detractors, indicating potential dissatisfaction or disengagement.

4. Influencer or Advocate:

  • The 25–34 and 35–44 age groups have the highest proportions of Promoters, aligning with their active engagement in advocacy and digital outreach.
  • Detractors are more present in the 18–24 and 55+ age groups, highlighting possible gaps in support or alignment with this persona.

5. College or University Student:

  • A significant portion of Detractors is visible in the 18–24 age group, emphasizing challenges in connecting with younger student demographics.
  • Very few Promoters in any age group for this persona, indicating low advocacy levels among students.
  • Major Donors are predominantly in the 25–34 age group, with some representation in older age groups.
  • College or University Students are entirely within the 18–24 age group.
  • Influencers or Advocates are distributed across all age groups but show higher representation in younger cohorts.
  • Supporting a Church or Organization and Regular Supporters have broader age distributions, with significant representation in the 55+ group.

4. Influencer or Advocate

  • Balanced resonance with "Videos or photos showing transformation", "Personal stories from those in need", and "The urgency or immediacy of the need".
  • Indicates they respond to engaging, shareable, and emotionally compelling content.

5. College or University Student

  • Overwhelming preference for "A clear, measurable goal", with little resonance for other categories.

    • A smaller portion belongs to the 25–34 age group, suggesting minimal representation of slightly older millennials or younger Gen X.
  • CurrentGen:
    • Comprised of a more diverse age group distribution.
    • Significant representation from the 55+ age group, followed by balanced contributions from the 35–44 and 45–54 age brackets.
  • NextGen:
    • Prioritizes Simplicity and ease of use, indicating this generation values straightforward and efficient interfaces for engagement.
    • Inspiring and emotional storytelling comes next, showing a preference for platforms that resonate emotionally and motivate them.
    • Least importance is placed on the ability to quickly support multiple causes, suggesting that variety in cause support is not as critical for this generation.
  • CurrentGen:
    • Also emphasizes Simplicity and ease of use, similar to NextGen, indicating universal importance across generations.

NextGen:

  • Heavily influenced by videos or photos showing transformation, suggesting that visual storytelling and transformation narratives strongly resonate with this group.
  • A clear, measurable goal also holds significant weight, indicating the importance of transparency and clarity in objectives.
  • Minimal influence from personal stories from those in need and the urgency or immediacy of the need, reflecting a lesser emotional connection to immediacy or individual storytelling.

CurrentGen:

  • Strongly values personal stories from those in need, showing that this group resonates deeply with emotional, human-centered narratives.
  • The urgency or immediacy of the need is also impactful, suggesting that this generation is moved by a sense of time-sensitive action.
  • Videos or photos showing transformation and a clear, measurable goal are less influential compared to the other emotional drivers.

Key Contrasts:

  • NextGen prefers visual transformation stories and measurable goals, emphasizing a modern, results-driven perspective.
  • CurrentGen is more affected by personal stories and a sense of urgency, reflecting a more traditional, empathetic response to causes.
  • Campaigns for NextGen can prioritize compelling visuals and data, while those for CurrentGen can focus on heartfelt narratives and time-sensitive appeals.

NextGen:

  • Opportunities to explore related or bundled causes dominate, indicating that this group is most persuaded by the ability to connect and engage with a variety of causes through bundled campaigns.
  • Highlighted campaigns with urgent needs are also significant, but less so than bundled opportunities, suggesting urgency is a secondary motivator.
  • Minimal interest in the ability to split donations among several causes, showing less emphasis on flexible donation options.

CurrentGen:

  • Opportunities to explore related or bundled causes still hold strong appeal, similar to NextGen.
  • Highlighted campaigns with urgent needs are slightly more important for CurrentGen, indicating they respond well to a sense of immediacy.
  • The ability to split donations among several causes is moderately valued, showing some interest in spreading contributions across multiple initiatives.

Key Contrasts:

  • NextGen is more focused on bundled opportunities, with less emphasis on splitting donations or urgency.
  • CurrentGen displays broader preferences, responding well to bundled opportunities, urgency, and flexible donation options.
  • Campaign strategies should emphasize bundled causes for NextGen, while offering urgency-focused messaging and flexible donation options for CurrentGen.

NextGen:

  • Primarily influenced by transparent reporting on how funds are used, highlighting a strong preference for accountability and clear financial tracking.
  • Updates showing the direct impact of contributions also play a significant role, reflecting a desire to see tangible outcomes from their giving.
  • My faith and alignment with the platform’s mission holds moderate importance, while positive reviews or endorsements and other factors have minimal influence.

CurrentGen:

  • My faith and alignment with the platform’s mission is the dominant motivator, showcasing a strong connection between personal beliefs and giving behavior.
  • Transparent reporting on how funds are used and updates showing the direct impact are also significant but rank slightly lower than faith alignment.
  • Positive reviews or endorsements from others have some influence but are less important compared to the top motivators.

Key Contrasts:

  • NextGen prioritizes transparency and measurable impact, driven by a results-oriented mindset.
  • CurrentGen places greater emphasis on faith alignment, reflecting a more values-driven approach to giving.
  • Platforms targeting NextGen should focus on data transparency and impact reporting, while campaigns for CurrentGen should highlight shared values and mission alignment.
