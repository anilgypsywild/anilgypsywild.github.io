Methods and measurement

RITE: Test, refine, and verify

Rapid Iterative Testing and Evaluation connects an observed usability problem to a design change and a subsequent check of that change.

  1. 1Observe

    Watch people attempt representative tasks.

  2. 2Diagnose

    Identify the difficulty and its consequence.

  3. 3Refine

    Change the design when the problem and remedy are sufficiently clear.

  4. 4Verify

    Evaluate the revision with subsequent participants.

Organize the evidence into four experience dimensions

RITE is the evaluation method. The 4D scorecard is the reporting structure. Only the first dimension is primarily behavioral; the others combine attitudinal, emotional, and adoption evidence.

DimensionResearch questionUseful evidenceDirection of improvement
DoabilityCan people complete the task effectively?Task success, completion time, errors, assistance requests, task difficulty, mental demand.More successful completion; fewer avoidable errors and assistance requests. Interpret time against the task goal.
DependabilityDo people perceive the experience as usable and trustworthy?SUS, appropriately administered usability measures, confidence, and trust in automation when relevant.Better perceived usability and appropriately calibrated trust.
DelightHow does the experience feel?Emotion ratings, post-task satisfaction, comments, and observed reactions.Less frustration and a more appropriate emotional response to the context.
DesireWill people choose to use it again?Intention to reuse, recommendation likelihood, repeat use, and retention.Greater willingness to return, corroborated by behavior when available.

Record measures at the right level

Task

Performance and effort

Record success, time, errors, help, and task ratings against a specific scenario and design version.

Experience

Usability and perception

Collect post-session measures with their instrument wording, response scale, and scoring method.

Participant

Context of use

Record relevant experience, device, accessibility needs, and work context. Demographics alone do not define a persona.

Make each scorecard comparable

For each measure, retain the task, design version, sample size, denominator, instrument, scoring direction, and uncertainty where the design supports inference. Compare like tasks and populations. Do not pool results from changing designs as though every participant evaluated the same experience.

Turn observations into actionable issues

ClassificationEvidenceAction
Blocking issueA participant cannot complete a critical task.Document the failure, consequence, and conditions; prioritize a remedy.
Usability issueObserved confusion, errors, delay, or unnecessary effort.Identify the interaction problem and evaluate the proposed correction.
HighlightA useful interaction or explanation supports the task.Retain it and check that revisions preserve its value.
ObservationA pattern or question needs more evidence.Record it without prematurely claiming a defect or cause.

Issue severity depends on impact, frequency, persistence, and context. A satisfaction rating alone does not establish severity.
