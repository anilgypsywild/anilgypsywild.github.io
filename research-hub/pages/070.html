Overview

This study evaluates two versions of the Compassion P2P fundraising experience and is structured as a click-through A/B test on UserZoom, with participants assigned to either Design A or Design B.

Our goal is to understand how users interact with the prototype, assess ease of use, and gather insights into donation behaviors. Specifically, this research will explore how these differences in design impact user engagement and donation behaviors.

Design-A

Design-B
  1. Design A presents a structured, direct approach to fundraising.
  2. It places the fundraising goal and amount at the center of the screen, providing users with clear financial information upfront.
  3. This design emphasizes transparency by making it easy to see how much is needed to fully fund a cause.
  4. However, A does not allow users to add multiple causes in a single transaction.
  1. Design B introduces a fluid user experience with the fundraising amount positioned closer to the checkout button.
  2. This design allows users to add multiple causes to their donation, offering more flexibility in supporting various initiatives.

Executive Summary

Recommendation: Design A

  • is the stronger user preference for driving fundraising success. (not statistically significant)
  • Provides clarity, aligns users with overarching goals,
  • Builds confidence in the donation process.

While Design B offers strong flexibility, it sacrifices big-picture focus, which may be crucial for achieving fundraising targets.

Why Design A?

  1. Clarity Wins Donations: Users prioritize the overall fundraising goal and progress, ensuring contributions align with campaign objectives.
  2. Confidence Drives Trust: Design A empowers users with thorough review options, making them feel confident in their choices.
  3. Prioritization Matters: Strong cause prioritization ensures that funds are directed effectively to meet needs.

Next Steps:

  • Roll out Design A as the primary approach.
  • Enhance flexibility by integrating Design B’s intuitive “Add another cause” button, creating a hybrid solution for maximum impact.

Takeaway

Clarity drives results. Design A ensures users contribute meaningfully while staying aligned with campaign goals.

Key Research Questions

  1. How clearly can users identify a cause to support from a single screen?
  2. How well do users understand the fundraising goal and remaining amount?
  3. What factors influence their decision to donate a specific amount?
  4. How well do users understand the checkout flow?
  5. How do users engage with the ability to add another cause?

Study Design

  • Method: Unmoderated click-through test (UserZoom)
  • Participants: Compassion users familiar with donation platforms
  • Tasks: Evaluating a static design screen for usability and clarity
  • Metrics Collected: Task success rates, time on task, user confidence, decision-making patterns

Tasks & Measurement Criteria

Task 1: Identifying a Cause to Support

Objective: Assess how well users recognize and process the cause selection options within a single screen.
Instruction:
"When choosing a cause to support, what information is most important to you? Take a look at the screen and decide which cause you would support."
What’s Measured:

  • Time to selection (efficiency)
  • Information recall (elements that influenced selection)
  • Decision confidence (clarity of available options)

Task 2: Understanding the Fundraising Goal & Choosing a Contribution Amount

Objective: Assess whether users understand how much funding is needed and how they decide their donation amount.
Instruction:
"How do you determine how much to give when supporting a cause? Look at the fundraising goal and remaining amount—how much would you contribute, and why?"
What’s Measured:

  • Clarity of fundraising goal display
  • Factors influencing the selected donation amount
  • Perceived ease of adjusting contribution levels

Task 3: Adding Another Cause (For A and B Users)

Objective: Evaluate whether users can discover and use the multi-cause donation feature. In Design A, users will likely not find this option, which will help assess feature discoverability.
Instruction:
"If you wanted to support more than one cause, what would you expect to do? Find a way to add another cause before finalizing your donation."
What’s Measured:

  • Task success rate (whether users complete or fail the task)
  • Perceived ease of use for multi-cause donation
  • Frustration or confusion in cases where the feature is absent

Task 4: Reviewing Checkout Details & Confidence in Donation

Objective: Evaluate how well users understand and trust the final donation summary screen.
Instruction:
"Before finalizing a donation, what details do you usually check? Review your final donation details—do you feel confident that everything looks correct? What, if anything, would you double-check or change?"
What’s Measured:

  • Clarity of final donation summary
  • Elements users focus on when reviewing the donation
  • Identified concerns or missing information

Task 5: Overall Experience & SUPR-Q

Objective: Gather standardized usability and trust feedback using the SUPR-Q framework (Standardized User Experience Percentile Rank Questionnaire).
Instruction:
"Thinking about your experience using this donation platform, how would you rate the following statements on a scale of 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree):"

  1. I found this donation experience easy to understand.
  2. I feel confident using this platform to donate.
  3. I trust this platform to handle my donation correctly.
  4. I would recommend this platform to a friend looking to donate.

What’s Measured:

  • Perceived usability, trust, and credibility using SUPR-Q
  • Qualitative insights into platform experience
  • Comparative ranking between A and B

UserZoom Results

User Insights

Question Insights

Demographic Insights

Age Distribution:

  • Nearly half of the respondents (49%) are aged 55+, making this the dominant age group.
  • The 35-44 age group forms the second largest cohort at 24%.
  • Younger groups (18-24 and 25-34) are underrepresented, with no participants under 25 and only 14% in the 25-34 bracket.

Education Level:

  • Most participants (53%) are college graduates.
  • Postgraduate degrees make up a significant 16%.
  • The remaining 32% have some college or high school education, indicating a moderately educated user base overall.

Roles in Fundraising:

  • Majority identify as fundraising advocates (41%) or church/organization leaders (29%).
  • Only 8% are major donors, emphasizing that the platform’s primary user base is more focused on organizing and promoting fundraising rather than making large contributions themselves.

Persona Insights

Motivations:

  • A personal connection to the cause is the leading motivator, with 73% of users highlighting this.
  • Tangible impact stories and updates are also significant (55%), suggesting that storytelling and transparency drive engagement.
  • A sense of urgency or immediate need ranks third (49%), showing that timely communication can influence decisions.

Learning About Causes:

  • Word-of-mouth and faith-based networks are equally influential (59% each).
  • Social media (47%) and nonprofit emails (37%) are secondary channels but still important.

Success Metrics:

  • Total funds raised and meeting/exceeding the fundraising goal are tied at 61%, indicating that outcomes matter most.
  • Number of donors and qualitative feedback (e.g., stories from the cause) are secondary measures of success.

Takeaways

Donor Engagement:

  • The platform's users are more likely to support causes they connect with emotionally or through personal networks.
  • Immediate feedback mechanisms, such as progress tracking and personalized updates, are key to sustaining engagement.
  • Role-Specific Needs:
    • Fundraising advocates and church leaders might prioritize features that help them mobilize networks (e.g., sharing tools, goal tracking).
    • Individual donors and students are more likely to focus on simplicity and transparency.

Recommendations for Prototypes A & B

Design A:

  • Emphasize cause transparency and progress visibility to appeal to users who prioritize goal clarity and impact tracking.
  • Include personal storytelling elements to drive emotional connections.

Design B:

  • Highlight flexibility for multi-cause support, which may appeal to fundraising advocates managing multiple initiatives.
  • Integrate real-time updates and social proof (e.g., “X donors supported this cause today”).

A or B?

Design A is better if the goal is to emphasize clarity of the overall fundraising target, prioritize causes, and motivate users to meet or exceed the goal. It is well-suited for users who prefer simplicity and focus on the overarching objective.

Design B is better if the goal is to encourage multi-cause support and provide users with more flexibility in choosing and managing causes. It caters to users who value customization and detailed control over their contributions.

Recommendation

Design A appears more effective because it provides clear context around the fundraising goal and progress, which is critical for decision-making. However, Design B could be refined to make the overall goal more prominent while retaining its flexibility for multi-cause support.

Appendix

Statistical Results

  • Test Type: 1-sample proportions test with continuity correction
  • Hypothesis:
    • Null Hypothesis (H₀): Users have no preference (p = 0.5).
    • Alternative Hypothesis (H₁): Users show a preference for Design A (p ≠ 0.5).
  • Results:
    • Observed Proportion: 54.9% preferred Design A.
    • Confidence Interval (95%): [40.5%, 68.6%].
    • Test Statistic (X²): 0.31.
    • p-value: 0.5754.

Interpretation:

  • Preference Strength:
    • While 54.9% of users preferred Design A, the results are not statistically significant (p > 0.05), meaning the preference may be due to chance.
  • Confidence:
    • The confidence interval suggests variability in preference but does not definitively establish dominance of one design over the other.

Quantitative Insights:

  • User Trend: While there is a slight observed lean toward Design A, further testing is needed to confirm significance.
  • Sample Size Consideration: A larger sample size may reduce the variability in preference and improve statistical power to detect a significant difference.

Recommendations for UX Team:

  1. Proceed with Design A:
    • Despite the lack of statistical significance, Design A aligns better with user clarity and prioritization goals based on qualitative insights.
  2. Future Testing: Conduct follow-up preference tests with a larger sample size to confirm these findings.

Key Takeaway:

  • Design A shows potential preference based on observed trends.
  • However, further research is required to validate this statistically.
  • In the meantime, enhancements incorporating Design B’s flexibility features can provide a balanced approach.
