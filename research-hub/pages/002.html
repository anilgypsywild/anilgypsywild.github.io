Introduction to Kano Feature Prioritization for Causes

Supporting Document for the Causes Feature Prioritization Study

1. What is the Kano Model?

The Kano Model is a product framework used to understand how people feel about different features — not just whether they want them, but whether each feature:

  • Must be there for the experience to work
  • Improves the experience linearly
  • Delights and surprises users
  • Does not matter
  • Creates frustration when present or missing

Kano helps teams avoid building features that sound important but have little real impact — and instead invest in what truly shapes trust, clarity, and motivation.

For Compassion Causes, Kano helps answer:

“Which features matter most to donors, and which ones should product prioritize next?”

2. Why is Kano valuable for Causes?

2.1 Causes features serve emotional and trust-centered needs

Donors care deeply about:

  • trust
  • clarity
  • transparency
  • emotional connection
  • a sense of stewardship
  • seeing real impact

Kano is uniquely suited for environments where emotion, trust, and motivation drive decision-making — exactly the world Compassion serves.

2.2 Kano prevents wasted effort

It helps identify:

  • Features donors expect (and lose trust if missing)
  • Features that meaningfully increase likelihood to give
  • Features that delight donors emotionally
  • Features donors do not value (even if we assume they do)

This directly supports [name omitted]’s 2025 roadmap and prevents “feature drift” or internal bias from guiding prioritization.

2.3 Kano is lightweight and fast

A Kano study can run with:

  • a small donor sample
  • minimal engineering or product involvement
  • rapid analysis

Perfect for the holiday window.

3. The Five Kano Feature Categories

Category Meaning Example (generic)
Must-Have If missing, users are upset. If present, they don’t notice. Clean drinking water at a restaurant
Performance More of it = more satisfaction. Less = less satisfaction. Fast page load or delivery speed
Attractive Unexpected delighters that create joy or emotional lift. Free dessert on your birthday
Indifferent Users don’t care either way. Button color or small UI variations
Reverse Feature actively creates frustration. Autoplay videos

Kano helps classify features into emotion-driven categories rather than simple “want / not want.”

4. E.g. Restaurant Water vs. Dessert

Imagine you go to a restaurant.

Feature A: Clean Water

  • If the restaurant has clean water:
    → You barely notice. It’s expected.
  • If the restaurant does NOT have clean water:
    → You are upset.

This is a Must-Have.
It does not increase satisfaction — but missing it causes frustration.

Feature B: Delivery Speed

  • Faster service = better experience
  • Slower service = worse experience

This is a Performance Feature.
Investment directly increases customer satisfaction.

Feature C: Free Dessert

  • If the restaurant offers it unexpectedly → You are delighted.
  • If the restaurant does not offer it → You don’t mind.

This is an Attractive / Delighter Feature.

5. Why this matters for Causes

Direct donor parallel:

A. Must-Haves

Transparency, clarity, and basic trust signals.
If missing, donors hesitate or bounce.
If present, they simply feel “safe.”

B. Performance Drivers

Stories, timelines, progress updates.
The more consistently delivered, the more donors feel connected and motivated.

C. Delighters

Video updates, personalized thank-yous, sharing kits.
Unexpected emotional touchpoints that deepen connection and spiritual encouragement.

D. Indifferent Features

Small UI features, less noticeable donor recognition, or optional visual add-ons.
Great to have, but not priority for engineering.

This makes Kano perfect for:

  • identifying donor trust drivers
  • strengthening emotional connection
  • guiding January roadmap
  • aligning with Compassion’s mission to serve with excellence, clarity, and honesty

6. What a Kano Questionnaire Measures

(No survey items included — just the conceptual explanation.)

For each feature, Kano captures:

  • How donors feel if the feature exists
  • How donors feel if it is missing
  • How important the feature is to their giving
  • How it affects likelihood to donate

From these inputs, we classify features into:

  • Must-Haves
  • Performance Drivers
  • Emotional Delighters
  • Low-Value Features
  • Reverse / Harmful Features

This gives us a prioritized, donor-centered roadmap.

Kano + Causes

Kano helps Causes:

  1. Prioritize like a mission-driven organization
  2. Invest engineering where donors feel it most
  3. Make trust visible
  4. Build emotional connection intentionally
  5. Reduce internal debate around “what feature matters”
  6. Deliver a January roadmap grounded in donor reality, not assumptions

It is fast, low-cost, high-value, and tightly aligned with the Compassion mission.

Lin, L., et al. (2017), Journal of Science and Technology

Why this matters:
Shows that the Kano model is reliable for identifying which features increase satisfaction and which only prevent dissatisfaction. Confirms Kano’s empirical validity for product design and prioritization.

takeaway:
Supports using Kano to determine which Causes features contribute to trust, clarity, and donor satisfaction.

2. Applying Kano to Evaluate Service Quality in Retail

Kermanshachi, S., et al. (2022)
🔗 [reference omitted]

Why this matters:
Applies Kano in a service experience model (shopping environments), similar to how donors experience Causes. Shows how feature classification leads to prioritization.

takeaway:
Useful analog for donor journeys: retail service quality maps well to digital donor service quality.

3. Analytical Kano Model for Quantitative Need Analysis

🔗 [reference omitted]

Why this matters:
Provides a mathematically structured version of Kano. Helpful for quant teams and reinforces that Kano can be integrated with statistical models, scores, and regression.

takeaway:
Strong justification for your data-science version of Kano. Gives legitimacy to integrating Kano into the Causes analytics ecosystem.

4. Kano Model in Education Service Quality

Madzík, P., et al. (2019), Administrative Sciences
🔗 [reference omitted]

takeaway:
Confirms that Kano works in values-driven, impact-oriented spaces beyond commercial settings — ideal for donor trust, emotional connection, and clarity.

Tldr;

⚖️ What Kano Measures

  • ➕ How donors feel when a feature exists
  • ➖ How donors feel when it does not exist
  • 💞 How emotionally meaningful each feature is
  • 💲 How strongly a feature influences trust and giving behavior

📊 Why It’s Relevant for Compassion Causes

  • Donors choose Causes based on transparency, emotional impact, trust, and clarity
  • Kano distinguishes “expected basics” from “differentiating storytellers”
  • Ideal for a spiritually-driven, trust-based donation environment
  • Allows for evidence-based prioritization rather than intuition or stakeholder assumptions

What Kano Outputs

A simple classification of features into:

  • Must-Haves (Basic Trust Signals)
  • Performance Drivers (More → Better)
  • Delighters (Emotional Lift)
  • Indifferent (Low Impact)
  • Reverse (Feature harms experience)

Ideal for roadmap shaping.
