🧩 How User Stories, Product Stories, Research Stories, and JTBDs Connect

1️⃣ Understanding the Relationship

User stories, product user stories, research user stories, and jobs-to-be-done (JTBD) are all frameworks used to define, guide, and prioritize user-centered design and product development.

While they each serve different functions, they are interconnected in how they shape product strategy and research direction.

Core Relationship Overview

  1. User Stories describe a need from the user’s perspective, providing a simple, goal-oriented statement.
  2. Product User Stories adapt this concept to product development, defining how a feature should work to meet the user’s need.
  3. Research User Stories refine the concept further by framing what needs to be studied about user behaviors and motivations.
  4. Jobs-To-Be-Done (JTBD) shifts focus from individual features or research insights to fundamental goals and pain points that drive user actions.

Each of these approaches builds on the others, ensuring that products are developed based on real user needs, tested with valid research methods, and ultimately deliver meaningful outcomes.

2️⃣ User Stories (General Definition)

A user story is a simple statement capturing what a user wants to achieve and why, usually structured as:
“As a [user], I want to [action] so that I can [goal].”

Example:

As a donor, I want to receive updates about the children I sponsor so that I can see the impact of my contributions.

🔹 Purpose: Provides a simple and structured way to articulate user needs. 🔹 Limitations: Does not specify implementation details or research requirements.

3️⃣ Product User Stories (Feature-Centric)

A product user story translates a general user story into a feature-oriented requirement that guides development.

Example:

As a donor, I want to receive monthly push notifications with personalized updates so that I can stay informed without logging into the platform.

🔹 Key Differences from General User Stories:

  • Focuses on how a system should behave.
  • Defines product-specific functionality that engineers and designers can build.
  • Often includes acceptance criteria (e.g., “Notifications must include images and a summary”).

🔹 Connection to Research & JTBD:

  • Helps align product strategy with user needs.
  • Requires validation through research user stories and JTBD insights.

4️⃣ Research User Stories (Behavior & Data-Centric)

A research user story focuses on understanding user behavior, motivations, and challenges, providing data to refine product decisions.

Example:

As a donor, I want to understand how different update formats (video, text, images) impact my emotional connection to my sponsorship.

🔹 Key Differences from Product User Stories:

  • Does not define a feature but instead asks questions about user needs.
  • Guides research methodologies such as A/B testing, usability studies, and behavioral analytics.
  • Ensures that product features are designed based on validated user insights.

🔹 Connection to JTBD:

  • Research identifies underlying motivations and pain points, which help define broader jobs-to-be-done.
  • Helps bridge the gap between assumed user needs (product story) and actual user needs (research findings).

5️⃣ Jobs-To-Be-Done (Goal-Oriented, Broader User Perspective)

JTBD shifts the focus from features to the underlying reasons why users engage with a product. It identifies their deeper motivations beyond the immediate interaction.

Example:

“When I donate, I want to feel a deep emotional connection to my impact, so that I am motivated to continue giving.”

🔹 Key Differences from User Stories:

  • Not feature-specific – focuses on the fundamental reason behind user actions.
  • Timeless and solution-agnostic – does not assume a specific platform or medium.
  • Guides research and product design at a strategic level.

🔹 Connection to User & Research Stories:

  • JTBD informs research stories by defining the user’s core motivation.
  • Research stories validate the assumptions about JTBD with real data.
  • Product stories implement features that help users accomplish their jobs.

6️⃣ Visualizing the Connection

User Story → Product User Story → Research User Story → JTBD

Framework Purpose Example
User Story Captures a user’s goal in simple terms “As a donor, I want to receive updates about my sponsorship so that I can see the impact.”
Product User Story Defines how a feature supports the user’s need “As a donor, I want to receive monthly personalized updates via notifications.”
Research User Story Identifies what research is needed to validate the feature “As a donor, I want to understand how different content formats influence my engagement.”
JTBD Defines the underlying user motivation beyond the product “When I donate, I want to feel connected to my impact so that I am motivated to continue giving.”

7️⃣ Conclusion: Why This Matters for research team

  • User stories alone are not enough—they must be validated through research.
  • Product stories must be backed by research stories to ensure real user needs are met.
  • JTBD provides the strategic foundation that aligns research and product decisions.
  • By using all four frameworks together, we create a research-driven, user-centered product strategy that prioritizes what actually matters to users.

📌 Next Steps for Research & Product Teams:

  • Ensure every product feature request has a corresponding research validation process.
  • Use JTBD as a guiding principle to prioritize features and research questions.
  • Align product and research roadmaps to ensure data-driven decision-making.
