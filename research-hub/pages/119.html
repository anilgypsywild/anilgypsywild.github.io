🎡 Using GitLab for Research in practice

Overview

This guide introduces a systematic approach to running a user research study using GitLab. It provides researchers, PMs, and designers with clearly defined stages, tasks, milestones, and deliverables to ensure consistency and collaboration.

Stages of a User Research Study

  1. Research Intake Request
    • Tasks:
      • Review the request submitted by stakeholders (e.g., PMs, designers).
      • Understand the objectives and scope of the research.
      • Create a GitLab issue for the research study.
    • Deliverable: GitLab issue with details about the request, objectives, and questions to clarify.
  2. Write a Research Brief
    • Tasks:
      • Define the study’s goals, methods (quantitative, qualitative, or both), and target participants.
      • Outline timelines, roles, and responsibilities.
      • Share the draft in GitLab for feedback from stakeholders.
    • Deliverable: Finalized research brief uploaded to GitLab.
  3. Study Design and Setup
    • Tasks:
      • Develop research materials: discussion guides, surveys, or usability tasks.
      • Set up tools (e.g., survey platforms, remote testing tools).
      • Sandbox or pilot test the study with internal participants to troubleshoot issues.
    • Deliverable: Approved test plan and materials.
  4. Conduct the Study
    • Tasks:
      • Execute the research (e.g., interviews, surveys, usability testing).
      • Monitor the process and address any issues.
    • Deliverable: Data collected (e.g., recordings, transcripts, survey results).
  5. Analyze Data
    • Tasks:
      • Organize and synthesize findings.
      • Use mixed-methods approaches to interpret quantitative and qualitative data.
      • Draft a report highlighting key insights and recommendations.
    • Deliverable: Draft analysis report uploaded to GitLab.
  6. Share Results
    • Tasks:
      • Review findings with stakeholders in a feedback session.
      • Revise the report based on feedback.
      • Present final results and actionable recommendations.
    • Deliverable: Finalized research report and presentation.

Checklist for Researchers

  1. Before You Start:
    • Create a GitLab issue for the study.
    • Assign milestones and tasks.
  2. During the Study:
    • Regularly update the GitLab issue with progress.
    • Add comments or upload documents (e.g., briefs, scripts, and reports).
  3. After the Study:
    • Close the GitLab issue once deliverables are shared.
    • Add lessons learned or suggestions for improvement.

Best Practices

  • Communicate Regularly: Use GitLab comments and updates to keep stakeholders informed.
  • Be Iterative: Share drafts early and revise based on feedback.
  • Stay Organized: Use GitLab’s labels and milestones to track progress.
  • Document Everything: Upload all key documents and raw data to GitLab for transparency.

Appendix

Here are some specific examples tailored for Compassion Causes to illustrate the process described in the tutorial:

Example 1: Testing Usability of Donation Flow

Scenario: The product team wants to test the usability of the donation process on the Compassion Causes platform.

  • Stage: Research Intake Request
    • GitLab Task: Create an issue titled “Usability Test: Donation Flow” with objectives like understanding user pain points and identifying opportunities to streamline the process.
    • Objective: Ensure users can complete the donation without confusion.
  • Write Brief:
    • Deliverable: A document outlining the goals, participants (e.g., 5 existing donors), and tasks (e.g., navigate the platform, select a cause, and complete a donation).
    • Tools: UserTesting for remote usability tests.
  • Study Design:
    • Create tasks like “Find and donate to a campaign supporting child education.”
    • Conduct a pilot test with an internal team member to ensure the study works smoothly.
  • Execution:
    • Collect user feedback (e.g., task completion time, errors, and satisfaction).
  • Analysis:
    • Identify key friction points, such as confusing navigation or unclear instructions.
  • Deliverable: A report with insights like “Users struggled to find the donation summary page” and recommendations like “Add a progress indicator.”

Example 2: Survey on Emotional Resonance of Campaigns

Scenario: The marketing team wants to understand what inspires donors emotionally when choosing campaigns.

  • Stage: Research Intake Request
    • GitLab Task: Create an issue titled “Survey: Emotional Resonance of Campaigns” with a timeline and key stakeholders tagged for input.
    • Objective: Identify the type of content (e.g., photos, stories, or stats) that resonates most with donors.
  • Write Brief:
    • Include survey objectives, the target audience (e.g., active and lapsed donors), and key questions:
      • “Which type of content inspires you to donate?”
      • “How important is seeing personal stories of beneficiaries?”
  • Study Design:
    • Design the survey in Qualtrics/UserTesting/UserZoom/Microsoft Forms and pilot it with a small group.
  • Execution:
    • Distribute via email and social media. Track response rates and send reminders.
  • Analysis:
    • Synthesize findings into trends like “80% of respondents preferred personal stories over statistics.”
  • Deliverable: A presentation highlighting key insights and a recommendation to focus future campaigns on personal beneficiary stories.

Example 3: Improving Collaboration Tools for Churches

Scenario: Churches using the Compassion Causes platform request better tools for organizing group donations.

  • Stage: Research Intake Request
    • GitLab Task: Create an issue titled “Interview Study: Church Collaboration Needs” with a list of stakeholders, objectives, and proposed timeline.
    • Objective: Understand pain points and prioritize tool enhancements.
  • Write Brief:
    • Target audience: Church leaders actively using the platform.
    • Method: Semi-structured interviews with 6 participants.
    • Questions:
      • “What tools are currently missing?”
      • “How do you track progress for group campaigns?”
  • Study Design:
    • Prepare a discussion guide and test interview questions with a colleague.
  • Execution:
    • Conduct interviews and record insights using Otter.ai.
  • Analysis:
    • Look for patterns like “Churches struggle with tracking contributions from multiple members.”
  • Deliverable: A prioritized list of tool enhancements, such as progress tracking dashboards and templates for outreach.
