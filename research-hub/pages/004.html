🚥 MaxDiff Simulation: Donor Motivations & Strategic Takeaways

Sandbox Survey is here

Executive Summary:

  • Universal motivators (trust, clarity, impact updates) are brand pillars.
  • Segment motivators diverge - proof of need for personalized donor journeys.
  • Strategic takeaway: investments must balance core universals with segment-level tailoring. This is a roadmap and resource allocation decision, not just a UX tweak.

The 15 donor motivation statements that formed the MaxDiff question bank

  1. I clearly understand where my money is going
  2. I trust Compassion to use my donation wisely
  3. I know I will receive updates about the impact of my donation
  4. The cause feels urgent and needs support right away
  5. I can see a specific project or child I am supporting
  6. A progress bar or percentage funded helps me see impact clearly
  7. I can make a tangible impact through my donation
  8. Supporting this cause aligns with my Christian faith and values
  9. The cause feels authentically connected to the local community
  10. I see that other people in my network are supporting this cause
  11. The story or images move me emotionally
  12. Compassion thanks me in a meaningful way after I give
  13. The donation process is simple and quick
  14. I have the option to give once
  15. I can choose how much I want to donate

MaxDiff Insights

1. Overall Relative Importance (Bar Chart)

• PM: Donors prioritize trust, clarity, and impact updates. Campaign messaging should double down on transparency and post-donation communication. Social proof and gratitude messaging are weak levers; minimize spend there.

• Designer: Donation flow must emphasize clarity on where funds go and impact updates. Incorporate visual trust cues (security icons, transparent breakdowns) directly in the flow. Avoid cluttering UI with “others are giving” signals.

• Director/Exec: The data shows a universal donor hierarchy. Across segments, top drivers are trust, clarity, and tangible feedback. This validates trust-building as a strategic brand pillar.

2. NextGen vs CurrentGen (Segmented Top-10)

  • PM: NextGen responds to urgency, visuals, and emotion; CurrentGen values trust, clarity, and faith. Campaigns must be segmented; dual messaging tracks are needed to avoid misalignment.
  • Designer: NextGen flows should highlight urgency and progress bars, CurrentGen flows should highlight credibility statements and alignment with values. Personalization modules should be prioritized.
  • Director/Exec: Confirms two distinct donor psychologies. Without adaptive content, we risk leaving money on the table. Strategic direction: enable dynamic personalization at scale; this is where tech and marketing investment must converge.

3. High Impact Donors vs Casual Supporters

  • PM: High Impact Donors want detailed transparency and specificity; Casuals need emotional resonance and ease. Campaigns should serve HIDs with reports and dashboards, Casuals with compelling stories and urgency.
  • Designer: Build HID flows with detail-rich dashboards. For Casuals, keep donation entry fast and emotionally compelling. Two pathways, same product.
  • Director/Exec: Confirms dual archetypes: rational investors vs. emotional givers. Strategy requires a bifurcated but integrated product approach. This is not just UX; it’s portfolio strategy for how we sustain both deep-pocket and broad-base donors.

4. First-Time vs Repeat Donors

  • PM: First-timers need urgency and reassurance; repeat donors demand ease and acknowledgment. Acquisition campaigns should lean urgency, retention campaigns should lean simplicity and gratitude.
  • Designer: First-time donation flows should reduce uncertainty (impact transparency, reassurance copy). Repeat flows must streamline checkout to near one-click with meaningful thank-you touchpoints.
  • nDirector/Exec: This maps directly to growth vs. retention economics. Investments in acquisition UX must focus on reassurance features. Retention depends on ease + gratitude loops. Clear roadmap split: one drives new inflows, the other maximizes lifetime value.

5. Rank Heat Map Across Segments

  • PM: Regardless of segment, trust and clarity rank at the top. Campaigns can universally emphasize these. Lower drivers (network support, optional gratitude) should not consume marketing budget.
  • Designer: Reinforces that UI must always lead with trust and clarity. Segment tailoring can adjust visuals/emotion, but baseline UX should never compromise transparency.
  • Director/Exec: Universal motivators = strategic safe bets. Segment tailoring adds efficiency, but trust and clarity are the non-negotiable backbone. C-level decision: treat these as brand equities to defend long term.

6. Cluster-Level Importance

  • PM: Two dominant clusters emerge: Trust/Transparency and Impact/Urgency. Messaging should organize around these clusters, not scatter across weaker motivators.
  • Designer: Structure UI around clusters—dedicated modules for trust signals, impact urgency, and ease. Avoid “feature soup.”
  • Director/Exec: Clusters represent strategic pillars. These are not just UX choices but framing strategies for the organization. Donor trust and tangible urgency are the twin levers that will differentiate us in the market.

7. NextGen vs CurrentGen: Δ Importance (Gap Plot)

  • PM: Gaps highlight where campaigns must diverge: emotion/progress visuals for NextGen; clarity/faith for CurrentGen.
  • Designer: Confirms need for differentiated flows. NextGen experiences should be more dynamic, visual, and urgency-driven. CurrentGen flows more text-based, informational, and values-anchored.
  • Director/Exec: Personalization gaps are not “nice to have”—they represent missed revenue if ignored. Exec-level implication: invest in personalization infrastructure. Otherwise, our single-track product will systematically underperform across one of the two largest donor cohorts.
