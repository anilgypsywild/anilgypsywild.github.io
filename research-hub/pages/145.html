🔵 Community App Icons Blue Version Preference Study

Final Recommendation: Version A (contingent upon recommended changes)

Study 1: Compassion Familiar User Perception & Selection Summary

Study Type: Quantitative + Qualitative (UserZoom)
Sample Size: 15 participants
Date: April 2025

Objective

Identify the icon (Version A or B) that best supports brand clarity, visual distinctiveness, and cultural alignment for Compassion International’s digital products.

Key Findings

Overall Preference

  • Version A: 53% (8 participants)
  • Version B: 47% (7 participants)

“It’s unique and stands on its own.”
“Looks great.”

Top Selection Drivers (multi-select, % of participants)

  • Modern & visually appealing – 60%
  • Looks unique / stands out – 53%
  • Simple & clear – 47%
  • Professional / trustworthy – 40%

“Visually clean and fresh.”
“Feels polished without being flashy.”

Design Perception Dimensions

Simplicity

  • Very simple: 47% (A) vs. 33% (B)
  • Somewhat simple: 40% (A) vs. 53% (B)

“Recognizable at a glance—intuitive.”

Uniqueness

  • Somewhat or very unique: 74% (A), 73% (B)

“Version A feels less generic.”

Brand Consistency

  • Rated ‘Very well’ aligned with brand: 13% (A), 7% (B)
  • Neutral or lower: 67%+ across both

“Hard to tell if it’s Compassion unless you know.”

Cultural Sensitivity & Symbolism

LGBTQIA+ Symbolism Scale (1 = Not at all, 5 = Extremely)

  • Version A: Most responses rated 1–3
  • Version B: Similar pattern; slightly calmer response tone

“I think of rainbow colors for LGBT+”
“Solid border seems calmer... multi-color was distracting”
(Note: Neither icon uses rainbow colors; perception appears tied to corner tonal variation in Version A.)

Suggested Improvements (participants selected one)

  • Adjust the colors – 27%
  • Make it stand out more – 27%
  • Improve brand consistency – 20%

“If anything, tone down the top left shading.”
“Feels modern but could anchor better to Compassion’s look.”

Recommendation

Proceed with Version A, incorporating refinements:

  • Simplify corner color contrast to avoid misinterpretation.
  • Boost brand alignment by integrating Compassion’s visual language.
  • Preserve strengths: clarity, visual appeal, and uniqueness.

Study 2: Users Not Familiar with Compassion: Perception & Selection Summary

Study Type: Quantitative + Qualitative (UserZoom)
Sample Size: 15 participants
Date: April 2025

Objective

Assess how users unfamiliar with Compassion International interpret and prefer two potential Community App icon designs (Version A and B), with attention to brand clarity, cultural alignment, and potential misinterpretations (e.g., LGBTQIA+ association due to color treatment).

Key Findings

Overall Preference

  • Version A: 60% (9 participants)
  • Version B: 40% (6 participants)

Top Selection Drivers (multi-select, % of participants)

  • Looks modern and clean – 67%
  • Feels visually unique – 53%
  • Communicates purpose clearly – 47%
  • Feels calm / emotionally neutral – 40%

Design Perception Dimensions

Simplicity

Very simple: 53% (A) vs. 33% (B)

Somewhat simple: 33% (A) vs. 53% (B)

Uniqueness

Version A feels less generic.

B is a little too safe or corporate.

Cultural Sensitivity & Symbolism

LGBTQIA+ Symbolism

Scale (1 = Not at all, 5 = Extremely)

  • Version A: Centered around 2–3; moderate concern tied to shaded corner
  • Version B: Skewed toward 1–2; very few concerns raised

Representative Comments

  • “I think of rainbow colors for LGBT+.”
  • ❗ “Version A’s top-left feels like a nod to pride even if it’s not.”
  • “Feels modern but could anchor better to Compassion’s look.”
  • ❓ “Neither screams ‘Compassion’ if you don’t already know.”
  • ❗ “Solid border on B makes it calmer... A’s tones are confusing.”

Suggested Improvements

  • Adjust tonal gradient/shading in Version A’s upper left (to avoid misinterpretation).
  • Reinforce brand linkage visually (font, color, Compassion cross, etc.).
  • Clarify the purpose of the icon if shown standalone.

Final Recommendation: Ver A

TakeAways

  1. Version A was preferred by 60% of users unfamiliar with Compassion: but one in three of them interpreted the icon’s top-left gradient as a potential LGBTQIA+ signal. This shows the reach of the design beyond brand-loyal audiences while signaling risk in misinterpretation.
  2. Brand Recognition: dropped by over 50% among unfamiliar users: even when they liked the design, they couldn’t link it back t to Compassion. This highlights the need for stronger brand anchoring in the visual system, regardless of aesthetic success.
  3. Brand Blindness: Without prior brand exposure, users seem to rely entirely on aesthetic cues, and Version A’s top-left shading prompted LGBTQIA+ associations more frequently (+13% increase).
  4. Emotional Tone is a Signal: “Calmness” and “Trustworthiness” drove up Version B’s relative performance, despite its lower overall preference.

Proceed with Version A, contingent on the following design refinements:

  • Minimize tonal contrast in the upper left to eliminate symbolic confusion.
  • Maintain strengths: standout visual identity, clean aesthetic, and emotional neutrality.
