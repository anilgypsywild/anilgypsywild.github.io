Revised Research Brief

Lifecycle Trust and Giving Impact Study

Lyssna Results are here: [reference omitted]

Background

Occasionally, a Compassion Cause cannot be completed as originally planned due to factors such as church initiated changes, compliance or security concerns, or extended lack of funding. In these cases, Compassion must apply a consistent lifecycle response that protects supporter trust, preserves future giving intent, and scales operationally.

Product has clarified that the final solution must be a single standard response applied across all scenarios. This research evaluates which lifecycle action best serves as that default.

Research Objective

Identify the single lifecycle response that most effectively preserves supporter trust and willingness to continue giving when a Cause ends prematurely, regardless of the underlying reason.

Primary Research Question

Which lifecycle response, when applied consistently across all Cause disruptions, best preserves supporter trust and future giving intent?

Study Design

  • Method: Unmoderated MaxDiff survey conducted in Lyssna.

Scenarios

Scenarios provide context only and do not imply different outcomes.

  1. Church initiated Cause removal
  2. Compliance or security termination
  3. Automatic Cause expiration due to lack of funding

Lifecycle Responses Evaluated

Participants rank the following potential default responses.

  1. Automatically redirect donation to a similar Cause in the same region or country
  2. Automatically redirect donation to a similar Cause in the same category
  3. Automatically redirect donation to another Cause at the same church if available
  4. Allow supporter to select a new Cause
  5. Move donation to a shared fund supporting other underfunded Causes

Key Measures

  1. Preference ranking of lifecycle responses
  2. Trust confidence if the top ranked option is applied automatically
  3. Likelihood to continue supporting Compassion Causes at a similar donation level
  4. Communication expectations when automatic actions occur
  5. Consent thresholds for different lifecycle actions
  6. Open ended feedback identifying trust risks

Appendix: Change Log from v1

  • Scenario framing moved from per scenario solutions to stress testing a single default
  • Decision framing shifted from feature selection to trust preservation
  • Refunds were removed from the ranked solution set
  • Communication expectations are now explicitly measured
  • Giving impact is measured as directional intent rather than pricing
  • Research outputs now align with Product and engineering constraints
