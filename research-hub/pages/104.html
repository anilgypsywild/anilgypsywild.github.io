Net Promoter Score: A worked example

Classify recommendation ratings, state the denominator, and calculate the difference between promoter and detractor percentages.

This is a teaching example using the ratings already listed in the archive. It is not a Compassion study result. The list contains 31 responses; the earlier page incorrectly used 32.

Start with the recommendation question

Use the standard 0 to 10 response scale. Classify 9–10 as promoters, 7–8 as passives, and 0–6 as detractors. These are scoring categories, not a diagnosis of each person’s satisfaction or behavior.

Promoters

19

61.3% of responses

Recommendation ratings of 9 or 10.

Passives

5

16.1% of responses

Recommendation ratings of 7 or 8.

Detractors

7

22.6% of responses

Recommendation ratings from 0 to 6.

Calculate the score

31 responses

NPS = 100 × (19 − 7) / 31 = +38.7, or +39 when rounded to a whole score.

NPS ranges from −100 to +100. It is a score, not a percentage. Passives remain in the denominator even though they do not enter the numerator.

Inspect the example ratings

3, 4, 4, 5, 6, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 9, 9, 9, 9, 9, 9, 10, 10, 10, 10, 9, 9, 9, 9, 9, 10, 10, 9, 10.

There are twelve 9s, seven 10s, five 7s, and seven ratings at 6 or below.

Use the result to guide investigation

Promoters outnumber detractors in this example. Follow up on the reasons behind the ratings and examine segment differences before choosing a product intervention. A single NPS estimate does not demonstrate product quality, retention, growth, or a causal effect of a redesign.

For repeated measurement, retain the same question, response scale, sampling approach, and timing. Report the sample size and uncertainty, and examine whether the people responding have changed.
