Executive Summary (TL;DR)

  • This document summarizes the full beta/pilot research plan for the Compassion Community platform, focused on three key personas: Supporters (Sponsors), Field Church Partners (FCPs), and National Office Staff.
  • It is designed to answer the core Go/No-Go question: Should Compassion Community be scaled?
  • The research strategy uses a mix of quantitative and qualitative methods including CSAT, NPS, SUS, interviews, and diaries to capture emotional connection, usability, satisfaction, effort, and adoption signals.
  • The research flow is intentionally low-burden and spaced across the pilot.
  • All research questions and hypotheses are mapped to five core research questions, ensuring clear traceability from data collection to decision-making. By the end of the pilot, we will learn:
    • Whether sponsors feel more connected and satisfied with their experience
    • Whether FCPs can share content without burnout
    • Whether National Office staff support the platform's scale-up

1. Research Brief (v2)

The study is designed to evaluate the experience across three critical user groups: Supporters (Sponsors), Frontline Church Partners (FCPs), and National Office Staff. The research aligns with executive priorities identified by [name omitted] and leadership, with the goal of informing a Go/No-Go decision for platform scaling post-pilot.

Measurement Objectives

  1. Sponsor Satisfaction & Loyalty
    • Does Compassion Community improve the perceived quality of sponsorship?
    • Do sponsors feel more emotionally connected and fulfilled?
    • Are they likely to recommend the platform or reuse it?
  2. Field Partner Experience (FCP Burden)
    • Can FCPs easily integrate Community into their ministry workflow?
    • Does it feel seamless or like extra work?
    • What motivates or discourages ongoing contribution?
  3. Operational Feasibility (National Office Support)
    • Can the National Office support this effort without major strain?
    • Do they see operational value or anticipate long-term challenges?

Research Study Design

A mixed-methods approach will be used across the three personas. Key instruments include:

  • Quantitative Surveys (CSAT, NPS, SUS, comparative Likert items)
  • Qualitative Interviews and Focus Groups
  • Diary Studies (asynchronous prompts to reduce burden)
  • Usage and Engagement Metrics (where available)

Each method is designed to provide directional and summative insight, with minimal user burden.

Week 6 – Midpoint Survey

  • Objective: Assess usability, burden, and satisfaction after using the platform.
  • Method: Online survey (~6–8 minutes)
  • Instruments:
    • System Usability Scale (SUS): Standard 10-item usability rating
      1. I think that I would like to use this system frequently.
      2. I found the system unnecessarily complex.
      3. I thought the system was easy to use.
      4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system.
      5. I found the various functions in this system were well integrated.
      6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system.
      7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly.
      8. I found the system very cumbersome to use.
      9. I felt very confident using the system.
      10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system.
    • Custom Likert-scale on burden and workflow fit (e.g., “This platform was easy to use,” “It fit into my routine”)

3. Research Questions (RQs), Hypotheses

Research Questions (RQs)

These high-level research questions (RQs) guide the research strategy and inform both survey construction (quant probes) and qualitative inquiry.

  1. Usability & Utility:
    • How usable and intuitive is the Compassion Community app for sponsors, FCPs, and National Office staff?
    • Are users able to complete their tasks (e.g., posting, viewing updates, moderating) with minimal friction?
  2. Emotional Connection & Value:
    • Do sponsors feel more emotionally connected to their sponsored child, FCP staff, or the church community through Community than through letters alone?
    • Does the emotional content of the Community posts (photos, videos, stories) enhance perceived value?
  3. Effort & Burden:
    • Do FCPs and National Office staff perceive the added tasks (posting, moderating, translating) as manageable?
    • Is the process sustainable within current workflows?
  4. Adoption & Loyalty:
    • Do sponsors and FCPs want to continue using the platform after the pilot?
    • Would they recommend Community to others?
    • Does initial satisfaction and usability drive long-term engagement?
  5. Complementarity with Letters:
    • Does Compassion Community complement or detract from the traditional sponsorship experience (letters, photos, direct updates)?
    • Is it perceived as an enhancement or as a replacement?

Hypotheses (Grouped by Persona)

This list of RQs and hypotheses provides the foundation for designing effective instruments, selecting methods, and defining success metrics in the Compassion Community pilot evaluation.

Each hypothesis is traceable to one or more RQs and can be assessed through a mix of quantitative and qualitative data collection across the three core participant groups.

Supporter (Sponsor) Hypotheses

  • H1: Real-time Community posts increase sponsors’ emotional connection compared to child letters.
  • H2: Sponsors report higher overall satisfaction with their sponsorship experience after using Community.
  • H3: Sponsors express a strong intent to continue using Community and would recommend it to other sponsors (high NPS).
  • H4: Sponsors who engage more frequently with the app report higher satisfaction and emotional connection.
  • H5: Posts that include rich media (especially video) generate greater emotional impact than text or static photos.

FCP (Frontline Church Partner) Hypotheses

  • H6: The Community platform is perceived as low-burden by FCPs and fits within their existing workflow.
  • H7: A seamless content posting experience leads to higher satisfaction, while any technical friction reduces willingness to post.
  • H8: FCPs are more motivated to post when they receive positive feedback or engagement from sponsors.
  • H9: By the end of the pilot, most FCPs will want to continue using the platform and support its broader rollout.

National Office Staff Hypotheses

  • H10: National Office staff find the Community platform manageable within existing workloads (training, moderation, translation).
  • H11: Staff observe increased sponsor engagement or excitement resulting from Community.
  • H12: Staff recommend continuing or scaling the Community platform after the pilot based on observed benefits and feasibility.
  • H13: Community is seen as a complement to the existing child letter system, not a replacement.

4. Mapping Community's Strategic Goals to Research Proposed

Strategic Goals

Sponsors 

  • high satisfaction with Compassion Community 
  • higher satisfaction with Sponsorship (compared to their experience without Community) 
  • increased confidence their giving is making an impact 
  • feel more connected to the work they are funding 
  • are likely to stay with Compassion longer, Sponsor again, or give more than they otherwise would have 
     

FCP Staff 

  • high satisfaction with Compassion Community 
  • easy to learn and use Compassion Community 
  • would choose to use Compassion Community even if not required 
     

National Office Staff 

  • easy to learn, use, and train Compassion Community (even during PCA implementation) 
  • fast and easy to translate and moderate with Content Review 
  • recommend expanding Compassion Community to other countries 

Persona Behavior to Track

  1. Sponsors should report:
    • High satisfaction with Community
    • Higher satisfaction with Sponsorship vs. pre-Community
    • Greater confidence their giving makes an impact
    • Feeling more connected to the work they fund
    • Increased loyalty: re-sponsorship, long-term giving
  2. FCP Staff should report:
    • High satisfaction
    • Ease of learning/using the platform
    • Voluntary enthusiasm (would use Community even if optional)
  3. National Office Staff should report:
    • Ease of use, even during PCA rollouts
    • Simple moderation/translation workflow
    • Desire to scale Community

How this Target Persona Behavior is Covered the Proposed Research

  • Sponsor Goals are directly tied to hypotheses H1–H5 and measured via:
    • Comparative CSAT and NPS (Midpoint Survey)
    • Emotional connection Likert items
    • Open-text insights on impact and satisfaction
    • Optional focus groups (emotional storytelling)
  • FCP Goals are addressed in hypotheses H6–H9 and measured via:
    • SUS + burden Likerts (Midpoint Survey)
    • Diary entries on ease of workflow
    • End-of-pilot interviews covering satisfaction and future intent
  • National Office Goals are addressed in hypotheses H10–H13 via:
    • Pre/post surveys, mid-pilot diaries
    • Final interviews capturing readiness and support for scaling

All sponsor-side questions are framed in comparative language (e.g., "Compared to before Compassion Community...") and designed to show change in satisfaction, emotional connection, and trust. Field burden is measured carefully to assess sustainability.
