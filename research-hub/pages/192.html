GitLab Research Report: Guatemala FCP Usability & Workload Analysis (June 2025)

Top Summary (TLDR):


The AI ChatBot and Foundations redesign prototypes significantly outperformed current flows, achieving excellent usability (SUS~87) and low cognitive workload (TLX~30–33)

In contrast, the original Learning and Foundations modules showed poor usability and high effort (SUS <75, TLX >60).

The Cause Upload flow ranked in between, with good usability but high workload.

These results suggest that usability gains must be paired with complexity reduction.

Quantitative findings are strongly supported by qualitative feedback highlighting lower anxiety, higher confidence, and better user understanding.

Research Questions

What is the problem we are trying to solve?

The Create-a-Cause flow currently causes high cognitive load, user anxiety, and usability friction for FCP staff. Learning and Foundations modules feel disconnected from Cause creation and are seen as burdensome.

Why solve this problem now?

We are rolling out new designs (AI ChatBot, Foundations prototype) and need to validate their performance against the current experience. Reducing FCP burden is critical to scale adoption and ensure quality Cause submissions.

What do we know about the problem?

Past studies showed:

  • High rejection rates and duplicate work from poor submissions
  • User confusion around Cause form fields, requirements, and structure
  • Qualitative reports of anxiety and overload, especially during Learning and Cause creation

Recent prototypes were designed to address these concerns.

Research Goals & Metrics

Goals

  • Measure and compare usability (SUS) and cognitive workload (NASA TLX) across prototypes
  • Identify which design(s) reduce effort and improve confidence in task completion
  • Align quantitative metrics with qualitative findings to strengthen design direction

Metrics

  • System Usability Scale (SUS)
  • NASA Task Load Index (TLX)
  • Qualitative pain points aligned to design features (not a focus of this analysis)

Research Methods

  • Moderated Usability Testing
  • Observational Studies
  • Quantitative Surveys (SUS, TLX)

Design of the Study

  • 40+ participants across 6 FCP sites in Guatemala
  • Testing of current and prototype versions of: Create-a-Cause, ChatBot, Learning, and Foundations
  • Task-based flow walkthroughs with in-situ TLX and SUS completion

Research Insights

Insight 1: SUS and TLX Scores by Prototype

  • The heatmap shows a clear performance gap between the new prototypes and the current-state flows in terms of usability (SUS) and workload (NASA TLX).
  • The AI-enhanced ChatBot prototype (GU0441) and Foundations redesign (GU0492) achieved excellent usability scores (SUS 86.4–87.5) and low cognitive burden (TLX 30–33.2), indicating a smooth and supportive experience.
  • In contrast, the original Learning module (GU0984) and the Foundations baseline (GU0499) ranked lowest, with SUS below 75 and TLX peaking above 60, signaling high mental effort and usability friction.
  • The original Cause Upload flow (GU0901, GU0897) landed in the middle, with moderately high SUS (~80–89) but higher workload scores (TLX 52.5–60), suggesting usability improvements alone are insufficient without reducing task complexity.
  • Notably, the ChatBot and Foundations prototype outperformed all other flows on both metrics, underscoring their potential for scaled adoption.

These quantitative insights align tightly with qualitative themes of reduced anxiety, improved confidence, and better comprehension.

Insight 2: SUS Scores by Test Sites

  • This SUS boxplot reveals strong usability performance for the ChatBot (GU0441) and Cause Upload Prototype (GU0901), both showing tight distributions above 85, with most users rating them in the “excellent” usability range.
  • In contrast, Original Learning (GU0984) and Foundations baseline (GU0499) had much wider distributions and median scores below 75, reflecting inconsistent and lower perceived usability. 
  • Notably, Original Learning showed the broadest range, with scores spanning from 45 to 90, indicating some users struggled significantly. 
  • The Foundations prototype (GU0492) improved usability consistency, with most ratings in the 80–90 range.

Overall, this chart reinforces that AI-supported and redesigned flows deliver a smoother, more predictably positive user experience, while current-state tools show usability volatility and higher friction. Tight clusters in the high range suggest strong user consensus and satisfaction with the newer designs.

Insight 3: NASA TLX Scores by Test Site

  • This TLX plot highlights clear workload reduction from the AI-enhanced and redesigned experiences.
  • The AI Touchup prototype (GU0492) had the lowest and tightest TLX scores, consistently around the low 30s, indicating minimal cognitive burden.
  • In contrast, Original Learning (GU0984) and Foundations baseline (GU0499) had the highest workload ratings, clustering around 60, with no users rating them as “low effort.”
  • Most participants rated these current-state tools as moderate to high workload (orange and red dots), signaling mental fatigue.
  • The Foundations prototype (GU0499 – Friday) shows marked improvement, with workload ratings shifting downward and clustering more tightly.

Overall, the plot confirms that redesigned prototypes substantially lower perceived effort, while legacy flows remain cognitively demanding and inconsistent.

Insight 4: Usability vs Workload across Prototypes

  • This dot plot offers a clear side-by-side view of usability (SUS, green) and cognitive workload (TLX, orange) across all tested prototypes and current-state flows. 
  • Top performers like the AI ChatBot (GU0492) and Cause Upload Prototype (GU0441) cluster in the ideal zone. SUS scores above 86 and TLX scores below 35, signaling a highly usable and low-effort experience.
  • In contrast, Original Learning (GU0984) and Foundations baseline (GU0499) sit in the danger zone, with SUS scores in the low 70s and TLX above 60, confirming users found them confusing and burdensome.
  • The Foundations prototype (GU0499 – Friday) shows a strong improvement in usability (SUS 86.1), though TLX remains elevated (61.3), suggesting that while the interface is more usable, the underlying task still feels complex.
  • The Original Cause Upload flows (GU0901, GU0897) hover in the middle with a decent SUS (~80–89) but with moderate TLX scores (~52–60), highlighting a usability ceiling without deeper task rework.

This plot validates the effectiveness of targeted redesigns in both UI and flow logic: AI guidance and structural simplification yielded strong dual gains. Ideal design targets are clearly illustrated here: SUS >85 and TLX <40, achievable through both visual and cognitive design optimizations.

Executive Summary: Guatemala FCP Usability & Workload Study

  1. AI ChatBot Prototype (GU0492) delivered the best overall experience: highest usability (SUS 86.4) and lowest workload (TLX 33.2).
  2. Cause Upload Prototype (GU0441) also scored excellently, with SUS 87.5 and TLX 30, confirming that guided, conversational flows reduce cognitive burden.
  3. Foundations Redesign (GU0499 – Friday) improved usability significantly (SUS 86.1), though TLX remained high (61.3), suggesting opportunity for further streamlining the process.
  4. Current Learning and Foundations modules (GU0984, GU0499) consistently underperformed, showing low SUS (<75) and high TLX (>60); users found them confusing and labor-intensive.
  5. Original Create-a-Cause flows (GU0901, GU0897) showed decent usability (SUS 80–89) but moderate workload (~52–60), indicating they are usable but still mentally taxing.
  6. Redesigned experiences consistently outperformed legacy flows, with lower variation, higher consistency, and stronger participant preference.
  7. The data strongly supports scaling the AI ChatBot and Foundations redesign, as they measurably reduce user anxiety, improve clarity, and enhance platform adoption readiness.
