Why We Are Conducting This Persona Research

This research aims to deepen our understanding of peer-to-peer (P2P) fundraising behavior among Compassion International users. Specifically, the survey focused on identifying key user motivations, behaviors, and platform preferences by examining five distinct personas across two generational groups: Next-Gen (age ≤ 34) and Current-Gen (age > 34).

By uncovering how different segments engage with P2P fundraising, the study provided actionable insights to guide Product Management, Design, and Engineering teams in optimizing the Compassion Causes platform. The findings will inform improvements in usability, feature prioritization, and user experience to better serve diverse user needs.

Case Study Example

P2P Fundraising Persons Survey Statistical-Design

1. Introduction

At Compassion Ventures Group (CVG), meaningful surveys are integral to understanding user behavior and optimizing our fundraising tools. However, poorly designed surveys lead to biased, unreliable insights. The "Info_Beyond_Q" document outlines statistical principles that transform general audience questions into thoughtfully crafted, statistically-measurable, actionable ones.

This report details how these principles were applied to create a statistical design framework and transform product management (PM) questions into a robust survey for our P2P pilot personas research.

By using rigorous methods, the P2P survey uncovered critical insights into generational behavior and fundraising preferences.

2. Statistical Design Framework

The statistical design framework established in the "Info_Beyond_Q" document focused on the following principles:

a. Reliability and Internal Consistency

  • Cronbach’s Alpha: Ensures grouped questions reliably measure the same construct.
    • Target: Alpha values between 0.7-0.9.
    • Example: Fundraising motivations and tool preferences were grouped for consistency.

b. Dimensionality Reduction

  • EFA (Exploratory Factor Analysis): Identifies and eliminates redundant questions.
    • Example: Clustering responses to identify core constructs like urgency and personal connection.

c. Mitigating Bias

  • Non-Response Bias: Addressed with concise language, mobile-friendly formats, and skip logic.
  • Social Desirability Bias: Neutral phrasing ensured honest responses.
    • Example: Instead of "Is donating important to you?" questions were rephrased as "How often do you donate?"

d. Validity and Clarity

  • Construct Validity: Questions were designed to align with intended concepts.
    • Example: "What motivates you to fundraise?" explicitly captured drivers like urgency, community impact, and connection.

3. Transforming PM Questions

Using this framework, PM questions were restructured into statistically sound survey items. The table below illustrates the transformation:

Original PM Question Revised Survey Question
What makes you trust a platform like Compassion Causes? Which factors increase your trust in Compassion Causes? (Select all that apply: Transparency, Security, Personal Recommendations).
What motivates you to start a fundraising campaign? Rank the following reasons for starting a campaign: (a) Personal connection, (b) Community impact, (c) Urgency, (d) Other.
Why do some users donate to multiple causes? What motivates you to support more than one cause at a time? (E.g., urgency, personal connection, thematic bundles).

4. P2P Survey Insights

a. Generational Highlights

The P2P survey revealed distinct preferences between Next-Gen (ages ≤34) and Current-Gen (ages >34):

  • Next-Gen:
    • Preferences: Transparency and visual storytelling (e.g., transformation videos).
    • Behavior: Social media promotion and reliance on analytics tools.
    • Implication: Emphasize simplicity, storytelling, and measurable outcomes.
  • Current-Gen:
    • Preferences: Faith alignment and personal stories.
    • Behavior: Traditional community-driven fundraising methods.
    • Implication: Focus on shared values and detailed communication.

b. Key Insight Example

  • Transparency: While both groups valued transparency, their interpretations differed:
    • Next-Gen: Preferred visual evidence and metrics (e.g., impact dashboards).
    • Current-Gen: Prioritized trust-building through faith alignment and recommendations.

5. Before-and-After Questions

Original PM Questions and Revised Statistical Design Questions

  1. PM Question: What makes you trust a platform like Compassion Causes?
    • Survey Question: Which factors increase your trust in Compassion Causes? (Select all that apply: Transparency, Security, Personal Recommendations).
  2. PM Question: What motivates you to start a fundraising campaign?
    • Survey Question: Rank the following reasons for starting a campaign: (a) Personal connection, (b) Community impact, (c) Urgency, (d) Other.
  3. PM Question: Why do some users donate to multiple causes?
    • Survey Question: What motivates you to support more than one cause at a time? (E.g., urgency, personal connection, thematic bundles).
  4. PM Question: What motivates users to fundraise for Compassion?
    • Survey Question: What motivates you/your group to fundraise? (Select all that apply: Inspiration from need, ability to gather community, tools available).
  5. PM Question: How do users choose fundraising goals?
    • Survey Question: When setting a fundraising goal, do you base it on (a) the amount you think you can raise, (b) the need for the cause, (c) other factors?

6. CVG Takeaway

By leveraging statistical rigor in survey design, CVG achieved actionable insights that bridged generational gaps and informed fundraising strategies.

Thoughtful survey design is not just about asking questions, it’s about ensuring every response reflects reality. These statistical principles should anchor future surveys, enabling deeper connections between supporters and the causes thart are close to their hearts.
