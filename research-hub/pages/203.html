🧪 FCP Research Synthesis & 2026 Research Agenda

Owner: @AShankar5
Scope: Frontline Church Partner workflows (FCPs) across Guatemala, Thailand
Last updated: 2026-01-07

Primary source research (internal GitLab wiki pages):

  • Evaluating AI Assisted Workflow Efficiency (Guatemala, June 2025)
  • SiteVisitToThailand
  • CVG 4D UX Scorecard

1. Context and Purpose

This page synthesizes Frontline Church Partner FCP research conducted across multiple countries, roles, workflows, and prototypes and proposes a 2026 research agenda.

The intent is to align product, design, engineering, and research decisions to an evidence based understanding of how FCPs experience the Community platform while performing real ministry work.

Country specific pilots are used only as supporting evidence to surface generalizable experience patterns. No individual country should be interpreted as a global baseline.

The CVG 4D UX Scorecard serves as the unified measurement system for this work, spanning Doability, Dependability, Delight, and Desire.

2. What This Body of Research Represents

Across roles, workflows, and operational contexts, the research consistently describes not a usability problem, but a frontline accountability system operating under cognitive and emotional load.

FCPs engage with the platform while managing:

  • Hierarchical reporting expectations
  • Limited time and staffing
  • Moral responsibility to represent ministry work truthfully and accurately

As a result, the platform is experienced not as a neutral tool, but as a formal record of work that is subject to review, rejection, and downstream consequences.

This framing explains the recurring qualitative and quantitative patterns observed across studies.

3. Core Qualitative Findings (Consistent Across Contexts)

⚠️ 3.1 Submission Is Experienced as Risk

Across studies, qualitative language clusters around approval, correctness, and fear of rejection.

FCPs routinely hesitate before submitting, delay posts, or abandon tasks when expectations are unclear. This hesitation appears even when task completion is technically possible.

Ambiguity is interpreted as personal exposure, not system failure.

Synthesis
Submission is experienced as risk, not interaction.

3.2 Cognitive Load Is the Dominant Failure Mode

Quantitative evidence consistently shows cognitive workload NASA TLX as the strongest predictor of experience failure.

Across datasets:

  • TLX is strongly negatively correlated with SUS
  • Higher TLX predicts lower CSAT and NPS
  • Elevated workload suppresses reuse and recommendation intent

Guided and AI assisted prototypes demonstrate:

  • High usability SUS approximately 86 to 88
  • Low cognitive workload TLX approximately 30 to 33

Structurally complex or legacy flows demonstrate:

  • Elevated workload TLX above 60
  • Even when visual usability improves

Synthesis
FCP experience quality is workload bound, not feature bound.

🎨 3.3 UI Improvements Without Structural Change Plateau

Several workflows demonstrate moderate to high usability while retaining moderate to high cognitive workload.

Quantitatively, these experiences fall outside the ideal experience zone defined by:

  • SUS greater than 85
  • TLX below 40

Qualitatively, these workflows produce:

  • Continued hesitation
  • Second guessing
  • Reduced confidence despite improved visual clarity

Implication
Visual UX enhancements without task simplification does not reduce perceived risk.

3.4 Persona Alignment Drives Experience Outcomes

Persona alignment consistently explains experience variation more strongly than geography.

Observed role patterns include:

  • Oversight and leadership personas aligning well with accountability structures and reporting requirements
  • Operational and field personas experiencing friction from retries and recovery paths

These patterns recur across countries even when role titles and responsibilities differ.

Synthesis
A single workflow may not emotionally or cognitively serve all FCP personas.

:man_with_white_cane_facing_right_dark_skin_tone: 3.6 Guidance Changes Emotional State, Not Just Efficiency

Guided and AI assisted workflows produce consistent emotional shifts:

  • Increased clarity
  • Reduced anxiety language
  • Higher confidence at submission

These emotional changes align tightly with quantitative improvements in both SUS and TLX.

Interpretation
Guidance functions as emotional scaffolding in accountability heavy environments, not merely as efficiency tooling.

4. Visual Evidence Already Available

Existing research artifacts already contain the core evidence needed for leadership decision making.

🔢 5. Quantitative Evidence Summary (Measurement Spine)

5.1 Usability and Workload by Prototype

Guided and AI assisted prototypes:

  • SUS approximately 86 to 88
  • TLX approximately 30 to 33

Structurally complex and legacy flows:

  • SUS below 75
  • TLX above 60

Mixed flows:

  • SUS 80 to 89
  • TLX 52 to 60

Interpretation
Usability improvements must be paired with complexity reduction.

Target experience zone
SUS greater than 85 and TLX below 40.

5.2 Workload Drives the Experience Ecosystem

Workload demonstrates coherent system wide relationships:

  • TLX negatively associated with SUS, CSAT, and NPS
  • SUS positively associated with CSAT
  • CSAT strongly associated with NPS

Conclusion
Reducing cognitive load produces compounding gains across experience metrics.

🦄 6. 2026 FCP Research Agenda 🌈

The 2026 agenda is system completion oriented, not discovery oriented.

🧩 Q1 2026 — Where Do I Feel Most Likely to Get This Wrong

Focus:

  • End to end submission journeys
  • Hesitation and self questioning language
  • Points of perceived personal risk

Measures:

  • Task success
  • TLX
  • Confidence and clarity signals

Outputs:

  • FCP risk map
  • Baseline cognitive and emotional load by role
  • Initial 4D UX Scorecard baseline

😀 Q2 2026 — What Helps Me Feel Confident Hitting Submit

Focus:

  • Guided versus unguided flows
  • Examples versus blank states
  • AI clarification versus documentation

Measures:

  • Changes in TLX
  • SUS movement into ideal zone
  • Rejection and retry rates

Outputs:

  • Evidence based design and engineering principles
  • Workload and rejection reduction targets
  • Before and after comparisons

😭 Q3 2026 — After I Fail or Get Rejected, Do I Trust This System More or Less

Focus:

  • Longitudinal tracking
  • Memory of rejection versus success
  • Emotional language drift

Measures:

  • TLX and SUS stability
  • Reuse intent
  • Variance reduction across roles

Outputs:

  • Trust durability model
  • Identification of fragile versus stable experiences

Q4 2026 — Is This Helping My Ministry or Just Asking Me to Prove My Work

Focus:

  • Reflective interviews
  • Fit within real ministry work
  • Pride versus burden language

Measures:

  • Sustained workload reduction
  • Stable usability and confidence
  • Adoption consistency

Outputs:

  • FCP defined success criteria
  • Fully operationalized 4D UX Scorecard
  • Continuous listening infrastructure

🧥 Executive Summary

This research corpus demonstrates with qualitative depth and quantitative rigor that:

  1. FCPs are highly motivated
  2. Cognitive load is the primary experience failure mode
  3. Guidance reduces fear, not just effort
  4. Role alignment matters more than geography

The purpose of the 2026 research program is to ensure frontline ministry work is safe, predictable, and dignified at scale, with the CVG 4D UX Scorecard serving as the system that makes success and failure visible.
