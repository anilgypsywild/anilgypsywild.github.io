Trust in Automated Systems

Measure people’s trust in a specific automated system, then compare it with the system’s actual performance and their reliance behavior.

Jian, Bisantz, and Drury’s 2000 instrument contains 12 statements rated on a seven point scale. It is widely called the Trust in Automation Scale or Trust in Automated Systems Scale.

Research objective

Seek calibrated trust: people should rely on the system when it is dependable and recognize when to question, correct, or override it.

Original questionnaire and source

Jian, J.-Y., Bisantz, A. M., & Drury, C. G. (2000). Foundations for an Empirically Determined Scale of Trust in Automated Systems. International Journal of Cognitive Ergonomics, 4(1), 53–71.

Open access validation study: see Table 1 for the complete questionnaire wording. Use the published statements for administration. The guide below describes their content; it is not a replacement questionnaire.

Administration and scoring

Identify the system and the interaction being evaluated. Administer the published instrument after meaningful exposure, using the seven point response scale from 1, not at all, to 7, extremely. Record when the questionnaire was administered and what participants experienced.

For a combined mean, reverse items 1–5 using reversed = 8 − response, then average the 12 scored responses under a prespecified missing data rule. Preserve item level responses. State whether the analysis uses a combined score or separate trust and distrust summaries; do not silently mix scoring approaches.

Report sample size, score distributions, uncertainty, and reliability in the study population. Do not treat a universal alpha or a single “good trust” threshold as a property of every application. Changes to wording, response options, translation, or context require evaluation.

Apply it to AI assisted experiences

  1. 1Establish performance

    Define representative tasks and known system limitations.

  2. 2Observe reliance

    Record whether people accept, verify, reject, or correct outputs.

  3. 3Measure perception

    Collect the trust scale alongside usability and workload evidence.

  4. 4Examine calibration

    Compare expressed trust with appropriate reliance and actual reliability.

Explainability and trust answer different questions

An explanation may help a person understand or challenge an output. It does not automatically make that output correct or the system trustworthy. Evaluate explanation comprehension, error detection, correction, and appropriate reliance alongside the questionnaire.
