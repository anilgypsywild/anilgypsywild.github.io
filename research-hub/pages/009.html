Deep Contrast Report – Donors vs Prospects Motivations (MaxDiff)

Executive Summary

This report contrasts motivational hierarchies from two MaxDiff studies — Prospects (first-time givers) and Donors (repeat and overall) — to inform product, design, and messaging strategies.

Donor Motivations Prospect Motivations
Donors elevate faith alignment, emotional storytelling, and urgency once trust is established. Prospects emphasize clarity, autonomy, and control earlier in their journey.

Both cohorts rank:

  • “I clearly understand where my money is going”
  • “I can make a tangible impact through my donation”

as the top motivators.

Key takeaway: Lead Prospects with clarity and autonomy before layering emotional or faith-based appeals.

Methods

Design: MaxDiff (Best–Worst Scaling)

  • Hierarchical Bayesian estimation
  • Bootstrapped 95% confidence intervals
  • 15 motivational statements

Data Sources:

  • Prospect Motivations MaxDiff Report Oct 2025
  • Donor Motivations MaxDiff Report Sept 2025

Interpretation:
Scores normalized (0–100) to represent relative motivational importance.

Key Contrasts

  1. Autonomy and simplicity rise earlier for Prospects
    “I can choose how much I want to donate” (+3.6) and “The donation process is simple and quick” (+6.0) show higher importance in first-time givers.
  2. Faith alignment moderates for Prospects
    −15.9 points vs Donors — faith becomes relevant only after trust.
  3. Urgency converges but weakens for Prospects
    −9.5 points difference; specificity and tangible proof outperform urgency.
  4. Social proof and gratitude remain weak
    Minimal effect on motivation across both cohorts.
  5. One-time giving preference
    +13.1% difference — Prospects want flexibility before committing long-term.

Product, Design, and Messaging Implications

1. Lead with Clarity and Control

Show exactly where money goes; highlight tangible outcomes and cost breakdowns.

2. Empower Autonomy

Offer sliders, custom amounts, and one-time giving options to reinforce agency.

3. Streamline Experience

Reduce friction and emphasize confirmation and transparency before emotional appeal.

4. Stage Faith and Emotion Post-Conversion

Introduce faith-based storytelling and gratitude messages after the first donation.

5. De-Emphasize Social Proof

Replace “others donated” widgets with concrete evidence of impact (receipts, real updates).

6. Frame Urgency as Specificity

Use time-bound, evidence-based needs instead of generic urgency messaging.

Closing Insight

Prospects are not yet believers in the brand — they are believers in the stewardship of good.
The conversion pathway is:

Clarity → Trust → Faith → Loyalty

Designing experiences that show stewardship before spirituality transitions Prospects into long-term, faith-driven donors.

A3. Relationship of the Present Analysis to These Methods

The analytical pipeline implemented in this study constructs pairwise best–over–worst outcomes for each MaxDiff screen, producing a difference-coded design matrix analyzed via binomial logistic regression (GLM). The estimated coefficients represent aggregate utility scores, which are subsequently mean-centered and scaled to derive relative importance values (0–100) for each motivational attribute.

In methodological terms, this approach corresponds to the aggregate-level logit model described in the literature. When extended using hierarchical priors, the same data structure can be modeled through Hierarchical Bayesian (HB) estimation to capture individual-level heterogeneity in utility weights.

Applicable R implementations for such HB estimation include:

  • rstanarm (using stan_glm with partial pooling)
  • brms (multilevel structures via brm)
  • bayesm (custom MCMC estimation of choice utilities)

The inclusion of a bootstrap-based resampling procedure provides nonparametric confidence intervals around the relative importance scores. This step serves as a statistically defensible complement or fallback to full HB estimation, offering robustness when respondent-level estimation is not computationally practical.

A4. R Libraries Reflected in the Implementation

  • Core libraries: dplyr, tidyr, tibble, ggplot2, forcats, scales, base R stats (glm), and base I/O.
  • Optional libraries for HB workflows: rstanarm, brms, bayesm, cmdstanr, or rstan.

Summary

This flow formalizes the operationalization of MaxDiff best–worst data into interpretable motivational hierarchies.
Each transformation step preserves theoretical alignment with Louviere et al. (2015) and supports optional extension into Hierarchical Bayesian estimation for respondent-level inference.
Bootstrap-based confidence intervals act as a pragmatic robustness check when computational resources or sample sizes constrain full HB estimation.
