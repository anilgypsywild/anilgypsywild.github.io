🏷️ Persona Name Preferences

Note: Results from participants in the US only (N=155)

This report presents key insights from survey data on persona name selection for High-Impact Donors (HIDs), research conducted on UserZoom, here's the results.

  • The analysis identifies the most preferred persona names, alternative selections, and any variations across age groups.
  • By aligning persona names with donor identity and engagement behaviors, these insights aim to optimize platform adoption and messaging strategies.
  • These initial findings reveal a strong preference for legacy-oriented and strategic giving personas, with younger donors gravitating towards digital and community-driven engagement, while older donors prioritize long-term impact and faith-driven philanthropy.

These distinctions provide a foundation for refining donor segmentation, personalized outreach, and effective communication strategies.

🛹 Key Insights from Persona Name Selection

Overall Selection Trends

  • “Legacy Investor” was the most preferred main persona name, reflecting a preference for long-term impact and sustainable philanthropy.
  • “Strategic Philanthropist” was the top sub-persona, emphasizing data-driven and analytical approaches to giving.
  • Faith-based donor segments showed a stronger preference for “Faith-Driven Steward” and “Kingdom Builder”, indicating the importance of spiritual alignment in giving behavior.

Age-Group Differences in Persona Preferences

The analysis revealed no major shifts in persona preferences across age groups, but some notable tendencies emerge:

Younger Donors (Below 40 years)

  • More likely to favor “Cause-Driven Donor” and “Social & Digital Donor”, suggesting that digital engagement and crowdfunding resonate with this group.
  • Showed higher engagement with “Giving Advocate”, highlighting a preference for peer-influenced giving and social impact

Older Donors (40+ years)

  • Stronger preference for “Legacy Investor”, “Strategic Philanthropist”, and “Visionary Benefactor”, reflecting long-term, high-impact giving strategies.
  • More likely to support “Faith-Driven Steward”, indicating that spiritual motivations in philanthropy increase with age.

🥼 Research Recommendations

1. Align Messaging with Donor Preferences

  • Use “Legacy Investor” as the default main persona in communications, but consider segmenting messages to highlight both long-term giving impact and digital engagement opportunities.
  • For younger donors, emphasize community-driven impact through terms like “Cause-Driven Donor” and “Giving Advocate”.

2. Personalization & Segmentation

  • Tailor outreach to different age groups:
  • Under 40: Focus on digital and social engagement, leveraging terms like “Social & Digital Donor”.
  • 40+ Donors: Highlight legacy building, strategic philanthropy, and faith-driven missions.

3. Consider Testing Faith-Based Messaging

• Since faith-driven personas ranked high, testing explicit faith-based messaging for religious donors could enhance engagement.

👣 Next Steps

  1. Refine persona labels for clearer differentiation, ensuring that sub-personas reflect unique engagement styles rather than overlapping attributes.
  2. Integrate insights into onboarding & outreach for the Supporter Platform.

🎯 TakeAway

The data supports “Legacy Investor” as the dominant persona for high-impact donors, with distinct age-based variations in preferences. Incorporating these insights will enhance engagement, messaging effectiveness, and donor platform adoption.

Key Observations from Scores:

  • "Legacy Investor" had the highest score (397), showing strong preference as the main persona.
  • Close competition between "Strategic Philanthropist" and "Impact Investor" suggests an interest in both data-driven giving and social returns on donations.
  • Faith-based personas (Faith-Driven Steward & Kingdom Builder) had solid support, reinforcing the importance of mission-aligned philanthropy.

2. Demographic Insights

Faith & Spirituality Impact on Persona Selection

  • Respondents who marked faith as "Very Important" showed a 50% stronger preference for Faith-Driven Steward.
  • Donors less influenced by faith gravitated toward Strategic Philanthropist and Giving Advocate, indicating a more impact-oriented, data-driven approach.

Technology Comfort & Digital Engagement

  • Donors "very comfortable" with digital tools preferred Cause-Driven Donor and Social & Digital Donor, aligning with tech-savvy engagement styles.
  • Donors less comfortable with technology leaned toward Legacy Investor and Faith-Driven Steward, showing that traditional and mission-based giving resonates with them more.

3. Detailed Analysis of Age-Group Differences

Age Group Most Preferred Persona Least Preferred Persona
Under 40 Cause-Driven Donor (Crowdfunding & social giving) Visionary Benefactor (Long-term funding)
40 - 55 Strategic Philanthropist (Impact-focused giving) Social & Digital Donor (Tech-driven giving)
55+ Legacy Investor (Sustained giving over time) Cause-Driven Donor (Short-term campaigns)

Age-Based Trends:

  • Younger donors (<40) are more action-oriented and engage via social impact.
  • Middle-aged donors (40-55) want measurable impact and ROI on donations.
  • Older donors (55+) focus on long-term, mission-driven giving.

4. Unexpected Trends & Open Questions

A Shift Away from "Traditional" Philanthropy?

  • "Visionary Benefactor" ranked lower than expected, despite its focus on lasting, legacy-driven giving.
  • Are younger donors moving away from "legacy" terminology in philanthropy? A/B testing could explore alternative wording that resonates better with next-gen donors.

Cross-Segment Appeal of "Giving Advocate"

  • The consistent ranking of "Giving Advocate" across age groups suggests that community-driven giving is appealing across all donor demographics.
  • Can the platform increase engagement by leveraging socially-driven donation models?

⏳ Future Persona Evolution Considerations

  • Should sub-personas evolve into donor "engagement styles" rather than rigid labels?
  • Testing donor self-selection into persona categories could yield further insights.
