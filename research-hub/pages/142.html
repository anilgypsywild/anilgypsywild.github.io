Research article

Purpose: The Community Platform FCP Beta Test survey highlights key user insights, addressing pain points, celebrating strengths, and identifying opportunities for improvement. This report provides actionable recommendations to enhance the platform’s usability, value, and engagement across diverse user roles.

Qualitative Theme Analysis with Quantitative Metrics

Overview 

The Community Platform FCP Beta Test survey analysis reveals key insights into user experiences and preferences. The study highlights both pain points and delighters across roles, supported by qualitative feedback and quantitative metrics.

  • Top Pain Points:
    • High data usage limits accessibility, especially in regions with expensive or limited internet.
    • Usability challenges include offline functionality, delayed post approvals, and difficulties in video/photo uploads.
    • Workflow inefficiencies, such as lack of search functionality and limited content creation flexibility.
  • Major Delighters:
    • Transparency and donor communication features stand out, aligning with the app's purpose and earning high praise across user roles.
    • The app’s usability and familiar interface drive ease of adoption.
Key Metrics:
  • Overall CSAT: 6.1 (baseline satisfaction).
Role-Specific Insights:
  • Project Directors: Most satisfied (CSAT 6.2) ; value transparency features.
  • Project Health Workers: Usability concerns dominate (e.g., offline functionality, video uploads).
  • Project Accountants: Mixed feedback; desire improved navigation and flexibility in content creation.
Suggested Next Steps:
  1. Prioritize high-impact features like data optimization, offline functionality, and search tools through MaxDiff and KANO analysis.
  2. Address key pain points with role-specific usability enhancements.
  3. Conduct targeted user interviews and usability testing to refine solutions.

This analysis underscores the app’s potential to drive engagement and impact while identifying critical areas for improvement to enhance user satisfaction and adoption.

Qualitative Theme Analysis

Theme 1: High Data Usage

  • Description: Many users noted the app's high data consumption, which is a significant barrier in regions with limited or expensive internet access.
  • Frequency in Feedback: 6 mentions (~25% of respondents).
  • Quant Metrics:
    • CSAT for affected users: 6.4 (lower than overall average of 6.7).
    • NPS for affected users: 8.2 (indicating loyalty despite frustration).
  • Sample User Quotes:
    • "The app uses data/airtime more than other social apps." – gh0347
    • "Reduction in the usage of data/airtime would help." – gh0347

Theme 2: Usability and Intuitiveness

  • Description: The app was praised for being user-friendly, intuitive, and easy to navigate. Some users found it comparable to familiar social media platforms (WhatsApp), making adoption easier.
  • Frequency in Feedback: 12 mentions (~50% of respondents).
  • Quant Metrics:
    • CSAT for usability praise: 6.9 (above average).
    • NPS for usability praise: 9.0.
  • Sample User Quotes:
    • "The app is very easy to use." – gh0121
    • "The features are familiar, like other apps, which makes it easy to use." – gh0473

Theme 3: Transparency and Donor Communication

  • Description: Users highlighted the app's ability to connect with donors, showcase impact, and improve transparency. This was a major delighter.
  • Frequency in Feedback: 10 mentions (~42% of respondents).
  • Quant Metrics:
    • CSAT for transparency praise: 6.9.
    • NPS for transparency praise: 9.1 (highest scoring theme).
  • Sample User Quotes:
    • "It helps you have a direct conversation with the donor and showcase what is done by the children." – gh0121
    • "For the first time, FCPs can communicate with donors through a simple social media app." – gh0985

Theme 4: Feature Requests (Notifications, Search, Offline)

  • Description: Users consistently requested additional features such as notifications for comments and approvals, a search function, and offline capabilities.
  • Frequency in Feedback: 9 mentions (~38% of respondents).
  • Quant Metrics:
    • CSAT for users requesting features: 6.5.
    • NPS for users requesting features: 8.7 (indicating optimism despite gaps).
  • Sample User Quotes:
    • "Notifications should show on the taskbar for quick responses." – gh0985
    • "If it can be used offline, it will help those without reliable networks." – gh0463

Theme 5: Performance Issues (Post Approval Delays, Uploading Challenges)

  • Description: Some users experienced delays with post approvals and challenges with uploading videos or multiple pictures.
  • Frequency in Feedback: 7 mentions (~29% of respondents).
  • Quant Metrics:
    • CSAT for performance issues: 6.5.
    • NPS for performance issues: 8.3.
  • Sample User Quotes:
    • "Faster approval of posts would improve usability." – gh0984
    • "I had a challenge in an attempt to upload a video." – gh0121

By Role: Key Pain Points and Delighters

1. Project Director/Coordinator

  • Key Pain Points:
    • High Data Usage: The app consumes significant data for uploads and viewing content, which is a concern in low-connectivity areas.
    • Need for Real-Time Notifications: Users desire taskbar notifications for updates on comments and approvals.
    • Relevant Metrics:
      • CSAT: 7.0 (highest among roles, showing resilience despite pain points).
      • NPS: 9.43 (strong promoter group).
  • Delighters:
    • Transparency and Donor Communication: Users appreciate the ability to directly communicate with donors and showcase the impact of their work.
    • Quote: “For the first time, FCPs can communicate with donors through a simple social media app.” – User gh0985
    • Relevant Metrics:
      • NPS: 9.43 (shows high advocacy for this feature).

2. Project Health Worker

  • Key Pain Points:
    • Usability Challenges: Difficulty uploading videos and multiple photos, especially in low-connectivity areas.
    • Offline Functionality: Lack of offline capabilities limits usage in areas with poor connectivity.
    • Content Approval Delays: Slow approvals disrupt workflows.
    • Relevant Metrics:
      • CSAT: 6.71 (moderately high but affected by usability challenges).
      • NPS: 8.0 (moderate promoters, some passive responses due to frustrations).
  • Delighters:
    • Ease of Use: The app’s intuitive design and navigation were frequently praised.
    • Quote: “The app is very easy to use and exhibits the impacts of our work.” – User gh0121
    • Relevant Metrics:
      • CSAT: 6.71 (positive impact despite pain points).

3. Project Accountant

  • Key Pain Points:
    • Workflow Limitations: Users need a search function to locate specific posts or activities quickly.
    • Content Creation Frustrations: Character limits in posts and inability to manage reactions effectively reduce flexibility.
    • Relevant Metrics:
      • CSAT: 6.71 (same as Health Workers, reflecting moderate satisfaction).
      • NPS: 8.57 (indicates optimism but highlights variability in user experiences).
  • Delighters:
    • Efficiency in Sharing Impact: The app allows accountants to share program outcomes seamlessly.
    • Quote: “The app helps others to know what you are doing without stress.” – User gh0985
    • Relevant Metrics:
      • NPS: 8.57 (reflects appreciation of core functionality).

Recommendations for Addressing Pain Points and Enhancing Delighters

  1. Project Director/Coordinator:
    • Optimize data usage and introduce compression for uploads.
    • Add real-time taskbar notifications for approvals, comments, and messages.
  2. Project Health Worker:
    • Develop offline functionality to draft and queue posts.
    • Streamline video and photo uploads for smoother workflows.
    • Expedite post approval processes.
  3. Project Accountant:
    • Implement a robust search feature for better navigation.
    • Expand character limits for posts and introduce reaction tracking.

Executive Summary with Key Takeaways

Summary

  • The "Community" app demonstrates strong potential, with high satisfaction among Project Directors/Coordinators, moderate satisfaction from Project Health Workers, and variable feedback from Project Accountants. 
  • The app’s core strength lies in its ability to enhance transparency and donor communication. 
  • However, key usability challenges, such as high data usage, offline limitations, and workflow inefficiencies, hinder optimal user experiences for some roles.

Key Metrics:

  • Overall CSAT: 6.1/7 (Strong satisfaction baseline).
  • NPS Promoters dominate, especially among Project Directors (70%).
  • Usability Issues (Total): 28 reported, with Project Health Workers encountering the highest frequency.

Key Takeaways:

  1. Delighters:
    • Transparency and donor communication were consistently praised.
    • “For the first time, FCPs can communicate with donors through a simple social media app.” – User gh0985 (NPS: 10, CSAT: 7).
  2. Pain Points:
    • High Data Usage: Noted by multiple users across roles.
    • “The app uses data/airtime more than other social apps.” – User gh0347 (NPS: 10, CSAT: 7).
    • Usability Challenges: Upload delays, lack of search functionality, and offline limitations.
    • “If it can be used offline, it will help those without reliable networks.” – User gh0463 (NPS: 9, CSAT: 6).
  3. Role-Specific Trends:
    • Project Directors/Coordinators: Most satisfied (CSAT: 7.0, NPS: 9.43).
    • Project Health Workers: High usability concerns (Avg. usability issues: 1.57 per user).
    • Project Accountants: Mixed feedback; some low responses (10% low-quality feedback).

Suggest Next Steps for Research

1. MaxDiff Prioritization:

Conduct a MaxDiff survey with the following prioritized features to determine user-preferred improvements:

  1. Reduced data consumption for uploads and viewing.
  2. Offline functionality for drafting and queuing posts.
  3. Faster and automated post approvals.
  4. Real-time notifications for comments, approvals, and messages.
  5. Search functionality for posts, projects, and activities.

2. KANO Model Analysis:

Develop a KANO survey to categorize features:

  • Basic Needs: Reduce data consumption, add offline capabilities.
  • Performance Needs: Faster approvals, search functionality.
  • Delighters: Personalized dashboards (FCP name, logo, mission), expanded emoji options.

3. Conduct User Interviews:

Schedule 30-minute role-based interviews to explore:

  • Usability challenges specific to Project Health Workers (e.g., video uploads, offline functionality).
  • Feature requests and frustrations of Project Accountants (e.g., search, reaction tracking).
  • Delighters and potential enhancements for Project Directors/Coordinators (e.g., advanced reporting tools).

Proposed Timeline for Research

  1. Weeks 1–2: User Feedback Analysis
    • Launch MaxDiff and KANO surveys.
    • Recruit participants for targeted interviews.
  2. Weeks 3–4: Feature Prototyping
    • Begin implementing high-priority features identified in surveys.
    • Focus on usability improvements for offline functionality and data usage.
  3. Weeks 5–6: Feedback Loops
    • Conduct usability testing with participants.
    • Roll out iterative updates based on testing insights.
