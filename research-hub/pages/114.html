📘 Case Study: Using the 4D UX Scorecard to Evaluate Community Platform Rollout in Myanmar, Dominican Republic, and Togo

Here is a case study for UX Scorecard, grounded in the 4D framework, with example Compassion expansion regions like Myanmar, Dominican Republic, and Togo. This version:

  • Establishes trust in the 4D framework’s simplicity and quantifiability
  • Bridges qualitative insight and quantitative rigor
  • Uses real UX metrics like SUS, NPS, CSAT, SUPR-Q
  • Demonstrates how each region operationalizes the framework

📍 Context

Compassion International is actively expanding its Community Platform—a digital tool enabling Frontline Church Partners (FCPs) to share real-time updates with Supporters—across several new countries. To ensure a consistent and measurable UX, we applied the 4D UX Framework to evaluate early FCP experiences.

The 4D framework offers four simple yet powerful questions:

  • Doability – Can users complete the intended tasks?
  • Dependability – Do users trust the system?
  • Delight – Does the experience feel emotionally rewarding?
  • Desire – Will users return and engage again?

To show that 4D is not only strategic but also quantifiable, we mapped each pillar to standard UX metrics used across the industry (SUS, CSAT, NPS, SUPR-Q, Emotion Scale), allowing for both benchmarking and actionability.

🎯 Objective

This case study showcases how quantitative UX metrics were used to assess FCP experience across three early launch regions: Myanmar, Dominican Republic, and Togo. Each region had at least 15 FCPs trained and actively using the Community Platform.

Launch Cohorts: Myanmar · Dominican Republic · Togo

🚀 Why This Works

The 4D Scorecard framework makes it easy for product, design, and field teams to:

  1. Speak the same language – Emotional and technical UX are all represented.
  2. Quantify insights – Each pillar maps to industry-standard UX metrics.
  3. Compare performance across regions – Benchmark and prioritize rollout needs.
  4. Act fast – Metrics link to product hypotheses and can drive backlog decisions.
