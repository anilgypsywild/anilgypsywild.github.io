Lifecycle Response Preferences

  • Author: [account removed]
  • Audience: Research and product teams
  • Date: Feb 05, 2026

Lysnna Results are here: [reference omitted]

Purpose

  • This study evaluates which lifecycle responses best preserve supporter trust and future giving intent when a Cause is disrupted (for example removal, compliance termination, or expiration).
  • The goal is to identify a default behavior that is least likely to damage trust while maintaining clarity and perceived supporter control.

Method Summary

  • Method: Unmoderated MaxDiff survey
  • Sample: n = 51 supporters
  • Stimuli: Five lifecycle response options evaluated repeatedly across scenarios
  • Outputs:
    • Net preference share (MOST minus LEAST)
    • Percentage of MOST vs LEAST selections per option

Scenarios provided context only; results reflect global preference signals, not scenario specific outcomes.

🔢 MaxDiff Quant. Insights

1. Net Preference Share (MOST − LEAST)

This above chart shows relative preference polarity. Values above zero indicate net positive preference; values below zero indicate net aversion.

Key signal

  • Allow me to select a new Cause of my choice is the only option with a strong positive net preference (approximately +50 percentage points).
  • All automatic redirection options fall below zero, indicating they are chosen as LEAST more often than MOST.
  • The most negatively perceived option is automatic redirection to a similar Cause in the same category, followed closely by regional or church based redirection.

🔎Interpretation: Supporters clearly prefer agency and explicit choice over any form of automated reassignment.

2. MOST vs LEAST Percentages

This above chart shows how often each option is selected as MOST or LEAST, normalized within each option.

Key signal

  • Allow me to select a new Cause of my choice
    • Chosen as MOST ~86% of the time
    • Chosen as LEAST ~14% of the time
    • Strong consensus and low controversy
  • All automatic redirection options show the opposite pattern:
    • Chosen as LEAST between ~58% and ~80% of the time
    • Chosen as MOST by a minority of respondents

🔎 Interpretation: Automatic actions are not just weaker than manual choice; they are actively disliked by a majority of supporters.

Cross-Scenario Insight

Across all disruption contexts tested:

  • Preferences remain directionally stable
  • The ranking of options does not meaningfully change
  • The desire for control over destination of funds is consistent regardless of why the Cause was removed

This indicates the results are suitable for informing a single, consistent lifecycle policy, rather than scenario specific branching logic.

Practical Takeaways

  1. Default behavior matters
    • Automatic redirection introduces trust risk even when well intentioned.
    • Manual choice consistently preserves confidence.
  2. Supporter agency is the primary trust lever
    • Giving supporters control outperforms any inferred or automated “best guess.”
  3. Automation should be optional, not implicit
    • If used at all, automatic redirection should require explicit consent and clear communication.
  4. Design implication
    • Lifecycle handling should prioritize clarity, pause, and choice over efficiency.
    • Reducing friction is less important than maintaining perceived respect and transparency.

💲 Exec. Summary (tldr;)

When a Cause is disrupted, the safest and most trust preserving default is:

  • Allow the supporter to select a new Cause of their choice.

All other options introduce measurable preference penalties and should be treated as secondary or opt-in paths rather than defaults.

🌻 Qualitative Interpretation of Lifecycle Handling

This section analyzes three attitudinal questions asked after the MaxDiff exercise. Each subsection follows the same structure:

  • Question asked
    • What the data shows
    • Qualitative interpretation
    • Supporting participant quotes
  • Resulting insight

Question 1

If Compassion handled any of the above scenarios using your most preferred option, how likely would you be to give to Compassion Causes again in the future?

What the data shows

  • 43% somewhat likely
  • 33% very likely

76% report positive likelihood to give again.

👀 Qualitative interpretation

  • Respondents are not signaling withdrawal when a cause is removed.
  • Instead, they are signaling conditional commitment.
  • Continued giving depends on whether the system response aligns with their stated preference.

Neutral responses indicate uncertainty, not rejection. Negative responses are a small minority.

Supporting quotes

  • “It really depends on the context and the cause.”
  • “I would like to have a voice about what happens to the money I donated.”

🔹 Insight derived from this question

Cause removal does not inherently reduce future giving.
Misaligned lifecycle handling does.

Question 2

If Compassion handled any of the above scenarios using your most preferred option, how would this affect your trust in Compassion?

What the data shows

  • 41% somewhat increase trust
  • 31% significantly increase trust

72% report increased trust.

👀Qualitative interpretation

Trust is not passive. Respondents describe trust as something that is earned through behavior when something goes wrong.

Trust increases when Compassion:

  • Explains what happened
  • Preserves donor control
  • Does not act without consent

Trust decreases when actions are taken silently or assumed.

Supporting quotes

  • “Not being transparent about why a cause is removed.”
  • “Failing to notify donors of what is happening.”
  • “Communication needs to be very clear and timely.”

🔹 Insight derived from this question

Trust is strengthened by choice, explanation, and visibility, not by automation.

Question 3

Which of the following actions would require explicit consent from you before being applied?
(Multi-select)

What the data shows

  • 67% say all actions require consent
  • 39% say redirecting to another cause automatically
  • 37% say moving donation to a shared fund
  • 37% say selecting a new cause on the donor’s behalf
  • 6% say none require consent

Qualitative interpretation

  • Consent is framed by respondents as an ethical boundary, not a convenience setting.
  • Many respondents treat donation intent as something that cannot be inferred once the original cause is removed.
  • Even actions designed to help are perceived as inappropriate if done without explicit approval.

Supporting quotes

  • “Don’t assume. Ask.”
  • “They should not automatically allocate my donation unless it was stated very clearly.”
  • “I would like to feel more control of what happens to my donation.”

🔹 Insight derived from this question

Automatic lifecycle actions without consent introduce immediate trust risk, regardless of intent.

🔶 Key Cross-Question Qualitative Insight

When these three questions are read together, a consistent pattern emerges:

  • Respondents remain willing to give again
  • Respondents report increased trust

...Only when their preference and consent are respected

The failure modes identified by respondents are not about cause removal. They are about assumption, opacity, and loss of agency.

💲 Exec. Summary (tldr;)

  1. Retention is conditional: Giving continues when supporters feel involved in the resolution.
  2. Trust is behavior-dependent: trust increases when Compassion explains, asks, and waits for confirmation.
  3. Consent is non-negotiable: Automatic reassignment without permission is widely rejected.
