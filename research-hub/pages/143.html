Research article

Prepared for Office of CMO/[name omitted]

🎯 Purpose of Study

  • This study was conducted to inform the final selection of an app icon for the Community App within Compassion International’s Supporter experience.
  • The goal was to evaluate the following five icon designs based on Christian donor preferences (age 55+), and ensure alignment with Compassion’s brand without triggering unintended associations.

📌 Executive Takeaways

  1. ✅ Study confirmed that lighter-border icon designs are acceptable and preferred.
  2. ❌ Heavy, solid ColorBlock (Blue Dark) was consistently rejected for looking “unprofessional,” “cluttered,” and due to “color mismatch.”
  3. ✅ All participants were Christian and heterosexual, with no LGBTQIA+ coded responses or symbolic interpretations.

👥 Participant Profile

Sample Size: 15

This is triple the recommended sample size of 5 for qualitative Studies: [reference omitted]

Consistent with Nielsen Norman Group’s foundational usability research, testing with 5 users typically uncovers over 85% of core usability issues. If not relying on large, one-time studies, NN/g also recommends iterative rounds of testing with small groups. Our sample of 15 users aligns well with this methodology. This approach maximizes insight while ensuring continuous design improvement and efficient use of resources.

Illustrative Task: Gathering User Feedback on an Icon Design

Users/Participants were:

  1. Introduced to the study with a clear framing around branding, simplicity, and professionalism.
  2. Asked to evaluate five icons after reviewing a design philosophy for each.
  3. Asked to rank their most and least preferred icon, and select reasons for their choices.

💡 Results Summary

✅ Most Preferred Icon

  • Thread Border (Blue) – 27%
  • Thread Border (White) – 27%
  • ColorBlock (Blue Dark) – 27%
  • ColorBlock (Blue) – 13%
  • ColorBlock (White) – 7%

Research Insight:

Thin border variants (Thread Border Blue/White) tied for top preference. ColorBlock Dark performed equally well. ColorBlock White was least preferred.

Why Was It Preferred?

Top reasons for selection:

  • Visually simple and clear – 73%
  • Looks unique and stands out – 40%
  • Matches Compassion branding – 33%
  • Feels trustworthy/professional – 20%
  • Modern/appealing – 13%

Design Implications:

Clarity was the dominant metric driving positive impression, followed by uniqueness and brand fit.

❌ Least Preferred Icon

  • ColorBlock (Blue Dark) – 53%
  • ColorBlock (White) – 27%
  • Thread Border (White) – 13%
  • Thread Border (Blue) – 7%
  • ColorBlock (Blue) – 0%

Research Insight:

ColorBlock Dark (the heavy solid icon) was the most rejected. Color harmony, visual simplicity, and professionalism were critical for avoiding negative perception.

Why Was It Disliked?

Top reasons:

  • Colors don’t work well together – 47%
  • Too complex/cluttered – 33%
  • Unprofessional – 33%
  • Doesn’t match branding – 20%
  • Generic – 20%

Design Implications:

The ColorBlock Dark icon raised visual and compositional concerns - particularly around visual noise, color mismatch, and lack of professionalism.

⚠️ LGBTQIA+ Color Interpretation

Key Validation Points:

  1. Design explanations were provided to participants in advance.
  2. ✅ No participant flagged rainbow-like or LGBTQIA+ associations in open-ended or closed feedback.
  3. Participants were all Christian, screened for alignment, and unlikely to overlook or tolerate symbolism that conflicts with traditional Christian values.
  4. All gender identity questions excluded “other” or “prefer not to say” responses.

Takeaway

No data supports that icons were perceived as LGBTQIA+ affiliated.
However, if leadership deems the design visually risky, revisions can be made without invalidating the study’s integrity.

📔 Research Recommendations

The research supports the following:

  • The solid ColorBlock designs were consistently dispreferred.
  • The Thread Border designs (Blue or White) and ColorBlock Dark performed similarly at the top , but only the Thread variants were not flagged as problematic.
  • Either of the lighter Thread icons can move forward without concern.
