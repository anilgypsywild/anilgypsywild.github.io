🧪 Causes Usability Benchmark Task Set (July 2025)

Each task includes:

  • Task Description
  • Task Rationale
  • User-Facing Instruction
  • Metrics

Task 1: Discover and Select a Cause

Objective:
Evaluate the usability of the Cause Discovery experience, including navigation, search, and filtering tools.

Rationale:
This task validates how intuitive and effective the Cause Discovery UX flow is for first-time and returning users seeking alignment with personal values.

Participant Instruction:

“Imagine you’re considering making a donation. Explore the site and search for a cause that aligns with your personal values. Use filters or search tools as needed, and select one that feels most meaningful to you.”

Metrics:

  • Task Time:
    Automatically measured from task start to cause selection.

  • Task Success:
    Did the participant successfully select a cause?

  • Ease of Use (7-point scale):

    “How easy or difficult was it to find a cause that matched your values?”
    Scale:
    1 – Very difficult
    7 – Very easy

  • Open-Ended Reflection:

    “If you were to donate again in the future, would you use the same approach to find a cause, or try a different path? Why?”

Task 2: Understand Cause Details & Allocation

Objective:
Assess users’ comprehension of how their donation will be used, and evaluate the clarity and trustworthiness of the information provided on the Cause detail page.

Rationale:
This task measures user confidence and perceived transparency in the donation process, both of which are critical to building trust and increasing conversion.

Participant Instruction:

“Take a few moments to carefully review the details of the cause you selected. Focus on understanding how your donation will be used and whether that information feels clear and trustworthy.”

Metrics:

  • Time on Page:
    Automatically recorded to gauge user engagement with the Cause detail content.

  • Trust Confidence Score (Threshold ≥75%):

    “How confident are you that your donation will be used as described?”
    Scale:
    1 – Not at all confident
    7 – Extremely confident
    (Consider ≥6 or 7 as a success threshold.)

  • SUPR-Q Item – Information Clarity:

    “The information on this website is clear.”
    (7-point Likert scale from Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree)

  • Open-Ended Reflection:

    “Do you feel confident in how your donation would be used? What influenced that feeling?”

Task 3: Simulated Donation + MWTP Prompt

Objective:
Simulate a real-world donation experience to measure behavioral willingness to donate and understand how interface design influences trust and decision-making.

Rationale:
This task evaluates the usability, clarity, and persuasive effectiveness of the checkout flow. It also captures users’ absolute Marginal Willingness to Pay (MWTP), providing insight into perceived value and emotional engagement.

Participant Prompt (Unchanged):

“Proceed to donate to this cause as if you were making a real donation. Select an amount and complete the donation process.”

MWTP Add-on Prompt (Post-Task Instruction):

“Now that you’ve completed the donation flow, imagine you had up to $25 to give. What’s the maximum amount you would be willing to donate to this cause?”

Notes for Analysis:

  • Captures absolute MWTP (vs. relative allocation).
  • Can be regressed against task-level predictors such as Ease of Use, CSAT, and Trust.
  • Use as a behavioral indicator of donation intent and platform persuasion.

Metrics:

  • Task Time:
    Automatically recorded from donation prompt to confirmation.

  • Ease of Use (7-point scale):

    “How easy or difficult was it to complete the donation process?”
    Scale:
    1 – Very difficult
    7 – Very easy

  • Allocation Amount (MWTP):

    Numeric entry box (bounded: 0–25 USD)
    Prompt: “What is the maximum amount you would be willing to donate to this cause?”

  • Open-Ended Reflection:

    “What influenced your decision about how much to donate?”
    (e.g., emotional appeal, clarity of information, trust in the platform)

Task 4: Navigate Using Filters (Wayfinding UX)

Description:
Assess the usability of category filters and navigation labels in helping users locate specific types of causes.

Rationale:
This task evaluates how clearly labeled and functionally effective the filtering system is, ensuring users can efficiently navigate to relevant cause categories without confusion.

Instruction:

“Use filters to find a cause related to ‘Education’ or ‘Emergency Relief.’”

Metrics:

  • Time on Task:
    Duration from task initiation to successful identification of a relevant cause.

  • Filter Use (Click Tracking):
    Record all filter or category interactions to understand wayfinding patterns.

  • Ease of Use (7-point scale):

    “How easy or difficult was it to use the filters to find a relevant cause?”
    Scale: 1 – Very difficult to 7 – Very easy

Task 5: Evaluate Trust Indicators (Social Proof)

Description:
Assess how users seek and interpret credibility cues such as testimonials, reviews, badges, or partner logos prior to making a donation.

Rationale:
This task measures how effectively Compassion Causes communicates trustworthiness through social proof and third-party validation. It helps identify which elements influence user confidence before donating.

Instruction:

“Before donating, check to see if you can find reviews, testimonials, or other signs that Compassion is a trustworthy organization.”

Metrics:

  • Trust Score (7-point scale):

    “How much do you trust Compassion Causes based on what you saw?”
    Scale: 1 – Do not trust at all to 7 – Completely trust

  • Click Tracking on Social Proof Elements (if any)

  • Net Promoter Score (NPS):

    “How likely are you to recommend Compassion Causes to a friend or colleague?”
    Scale: 0 – Not at all likely to 10 – Extremely likely
    (NPS = % Promoters – % Detractors)

  • Open-Ended Reflection:

    “What made you feel confident or hesitant about trusting Compassion Causes?”

Task 6: Catch Trial – Search for a Non-Existent Cause

Description:
Evaluate how users respond when searching for a valid-sounding cause that does not exist, in order to test the system’s error handling, clarity of feedback, and the user’s recovery behavior.

Rationale:
This controlled failure scenario helps identify frustration points, evaluate the UX under stress, and assess how well users adapt when their expectations are not met. It provides insight into resilience, transparency, and fallback strategies.

Instruction:

“Search for a cause that supports clean water access in urban communities. Use filters and the search bar.”
(Note: No such cause exists in the system.)

Metrics:

  • Time on Task:
    Duration from search start to task abandonment, switch, or user-initiated recovery.

  • Frustration Indicators:

    • Rage clicks (repeated or rapid-fire interactions)
    • Looping behavior (revisiting filters, search, or menus)
    • Rapid back/forth navigation

  • CSAT – Perceived Task Difficulty (7-point scale):

    “How difficult was it to find what you were looking for?”
    Scale: 1 – Not at all difficult
    7 – Extremely difficult

  • Error Recovery – Open-Ended:

    “What did you do when you couldn’t find the cause you were looking for?”
    (Optional probe: What would have helped in that situation?)

Task 7 (Optional): Post-Donation Feedback (Email, Sharing, Updates)

Description:
Evaluate the quality and emotional resonance of the post-donation experience, including confirmation, appreciation, and follow-up engagement opportunities.

Rationale:
This task measures emotional closure, perceived gratitude, and signals that reinforce trust and future giving behavior. It helps assess whether users feel seen, valued, and connected after completing their donation.

Instruction:

“Check for a confirmation or thank-you message. Look for ways to share your donation or receive updates about its impact.”

Metrics:

  • Simulated Open Rate (Observed Behavior):
    Did the user notice or engage with the thank-you or confirmation message?

  • CSAT – Follow-up Experience (7-point scale):

    “How satisfied were you with the confirmation or follow-up experience?”
    1 – Not at all satisfied
    7 – Extremely satisfied

  • Engagement Score (Binary or Tiered):

    • Looked for or clicked on “Share,” “Get Updates,” or “Track Impact” = 1
    • Ignored or missed all follow-up elements = 0

  • Open-Ended Reflection:

    “Did this make you feel confident and appreciated as a donor? Why or why not?”

Summative UX Metrics

  • SUS via SUPR-Q (System Usability Scale), CSAT (1–7), NPS (0–10 Likelihood to Recommend)
