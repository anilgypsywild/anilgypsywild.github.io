☯️ User Stories for User Research vs. Product Research

  • User stories are commonly associated with product development, where they help define features and functionality.
  • However, user research also relies on user stories—not for building products, but for guiding usability testing, baseline measurement, and qualitative insights.
  • In the context of Compassion, we need to distinguish between user stories for research (helping us understand user behavior, needs, and barriers) versus user stories for product development (defining system functionality).
  • This research doc outlines the differences, provides examples, and presents a case study for using user stories in research for the Supporter Platform.

1. Understanding the Difference

Aspect User Stories for Product Development User Stories for User Research
Purpose Define what a product should do Define what needs to be studied
Ownership Owned by PMs & Engineers Owned by Researchers
Example Format “As a donor, I want to see my giving history so I can track my contributions.” “We need to understand whether High-Impact Donors find impact reports engaging and if they influence future giving.”
Output A working product feature Insights, metrics, and usability improvements

Why This Matters for the Supporter Platform

  • The Supporter Platform is designed to connect high-impact donors (HIDs) with frontline church partners and the children they support. However, simply building features isn’t enough.
  • We need research-driven user stories to understand what works, what doesn’t, and what needs improvement before finalizing the experience.

2. Applying User Stories to the Supporter Platform

Example of a Product User Story for the Supporter Platform:

“As a High-Impact Donor, I want to receive detailed reports about the children I support so I feel more connected to the impact of my donations.”

  • This informs a product requirement—a feature where donors get notifications and updates.
  • The development team would build an impact reporting section in the platform.

Example of a Research User Story for the Supporter Platform

“We need to understand how High-Impact Donors interpret impact updates and whether they find them meaningful or persuasive enough to continue donating at the same or higher levels.”

• This informs a research study—a usability test or survey to assess comprehension and emotional response.

• The research team would conduct user testing on impact reports, measuring engagement, clarity, and influence on donation behavior.

Key Takeaway:

  • 🔹 A product user story results in a feature.
  • 🔹 A research user story results in insights that improve that feature.

3. Example Case Study: Testing Impact Reports in the Supporter Platform

Background

The Supporter Platform aims to provide High-Impact Donors with clear, detailed reports about the impact of their contributions. The product team built an Impact Reports section, but there were concerns about whether these reports effectively drive continued engagement and sustained giving behavior.

Product Story vs. Research Story

  • Product Story: “As a High-Impact Donor, I want to see personalized impact reports on the children I support.”
  • Research Story: “We need to test whether High-Impact Donors actually engage with and understand impact reports, and if these reports influence future giving.”

Research Approach

  • Usability Testing with High-Impact Donors
    • Donors were asked to navigate the Impact Reports section and explain their takeaways.
    • Researchers observed where they hesitated, got confused, or lost interest.
  • 2. Comprehension Surveys
    • A post-task survey asked donors to summarize the impact information in their own words.
    • Goal: Identify gaps in understanding, whether donors found the details compelling, and if they felt an emotional connection.
  • 3. Behavioral Metrics & Retention Tracking
    • Engagement tracking: How much time did donors spend reading reports?
    • Action tracking: Did they click on related donation links after reading?
    • Retention indicators: Did impact reports contribute to ongoing engagement (e.g., increased donation frequency, repeat logins)?
  • Example Findings & Insights
    • 40% of High-Impact Donors skimmed reports without fully reading them.
    • 25% misunderstood the reported impact metrics (e.g., how funds were allocated).
    • Only 15% took action (e.g., increased donations, shared updates).
    • The majority of donors preferred shorter, visual-based summaries over long text-heavy reports.
  • Research Informed Outcome & Adjustments
  • The Impact Reports UI was restructured to prioritize key insights upfront.
  • Personalized video messages from frontline church partners were introduced to enhance engagement.
  • A “Next Steps” prompt was added to encourage ongoing support or new donations.
  • Follow-up research was planned to track post-update engagement levels.

Final Takeaway

The product user story built the feature, but the research user story improved it.

Best Practices for Writing Research-Driven User Stories

  1. Focus on the research goal, not the feature.
    • ✅ “We need to understand how High-Impact Donors engage with impact updates.”
    • ❌ “The platform should provide impact updates.” (This is a product story.)
  2. Define the success metric.
    • ✅ “We need to measure whether impact reports influence donor retention and future giving.”
    • ❌ “We want to show impact updates in the platform.”
  3. Keep research stories open-ended to allow discovery.
    • ✅ “We need to explore what information makes High-Impact Donors feel most connected.”
    • ❌ “We assume donors will want more visuals in reports.” (Avoid assumptions.)

Recommended Readings from Anil's Bookshelf

  1. Nielsen, J. (1993). Usability Engineering. Morgan Kaufmann.: Discusses the role of user research in improving digital products.

  2. Krug, S. (2014). Don’t Make Me Think, Revisited: A Common Sense Approach to Web Usability. New Riders: Covers how usability testing validates assumptions about user behavior.

  3. Goodwin, K. (2011). Designing for the Digital Age: How to Create Human-Centered Products and Services. Wiley.: Provides frameworks for defining and integrating user research insights into product development.

  4. Rubin, J., & Chisnell, D. (2008). Handbook of Usability Testing: How to Plan, Design, and Conduct Effective Tests (2nd Ed.). Wiley.

    Details methodologies for conducting usability tests and analyzing user engagement.

  5. Gothelf, J., & Seiden, J. (2013). Lean UX: Designing Great Products with Agile Teams. O’Reilly Media.: Explores how research-driven user stories fit into agile development cycles.
