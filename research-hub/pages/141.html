Donor Dave Persona No More

📢 Debunking "Donor Dave" : How Oversimplified Personas Fail & A Better Approach for CVG

📚 Peer-reviewed research shows that:

1. 'Donor Dave' = WEIRD bias. Well-documented, even in Nature, since 2010.

  • Henrich, J., Heine, S. & Norenzayan, A. Most people are not WEIRD. Nature 466, 29 (2010). [reference omitted]
    • Rosales C. (2024). Behavioral Science is WEIRD and This Should Concern Us…. The Decision Lab. Retrieved February 24, 2025, from [reference omitted]
    • Stereotypical persona naming, like "Donor Dave," is biased, reductionist, and fails to capture the diversity of high-impact donors.

2. All Names are Pseudonyms”: A Critical Reflection on Pseudonymizing Names in HCI shows that:

  1. Naming Personas is Not Neutral – It Embodies Power & Bias
    1. The act of naming personas is a form of identity construction. When we use oversimplified or playful names like “Donor Dave” or “Generous [name omitted]”, they impose assumptions about behavior, background, and intent without research validation
    2. Pseudonymization and simplification erase complexity, creating stereotyped and Western-centric archetypes rather than reflecting actual user diversity.
    3. This is particularly problematic for global organizations like Compassion International, where names should reflect cultural and contextual diversity rather than reinforcing a single, dominant narrative.

3. HCI research (Dankwa, 2021) critiques Western-centric naming conventions, arguing that they “erase identity, impose external values, and reinforce systemic bias rather than engaging with users’ lived realities.”

  • Reductive Naming Leads to Research & Design Failures
    • Simplistic persona names do not help UX teams make better decisions—they create false certainty about user behavior.
    • A persona like “Donor Dave” implies a generic, homogenous donor with predictable actions, when in reality, donors exist across a spectrum of motivations, constraints, and engagement levels.

4. Research on critical race theory in HCI (Delgado & Stefancic, 2017) emphasizes that researchers must avoid reinforcing dominant cultural biases through standardization and simplification, particularly when working in global and cross-cultural settings.

💡 CVG Takeaway from HCI Research

  1. Ethical Persona Naming: Respect the User’s Identity
    • If personas represent real donor behaviors, they should reflect authentic engagement patterns rather than being a caricature.
    • HCI research recommends context-aware naming—for example, names that:
      • Reflect user motivations rather than labels (e.g., “Mission-Driven Advocate” instead of “Donor Dave”).
  2. Avoid cultural erasure by recognizing diverse engagement styles and global representation.
  3. Are data-driven, derived from qualitative and quantitative insights rather than internal assumptions.
  4. A strong persona should be backed by behavioral insights, not just catchy alliteration.
  5. Learning from HCI Research:
    1. Use real behavioral insights rather than catchy but biased labels.
    2. Frame personas around engagement drivers, e.g.,
      1. “Impact-Oriented Philanthropist” instead of “Donor Dave”
      2. “Hands-On Sponsor” instead of “Generous [name omitted]”
      3. “Long-Term Cause Advocate” instead of “Faithful Fred”
  6. Ensure persona names are validated with research participants, particularly for sensitive topics like donor engagement in faith-based giving.

As HCI ethics research (Brear, 2018) highlights, “communities should have agency in how they are represented rather than being renamed based on external assumptions of what ‘fits’ their role.”

🚫 Why "Donor Dave" is Problematic

  • Lacks Cultural Nuance: Assumes a generic Western male donor identity, ignoring donors from diverse socioeconomic, ethnic, and geographic backgrounds.
  • Reinforces Stereotypes: Perpetuates the idea that all donors fit into a singular profile, which alienates high-impact contributors with different motivations.
  • Ignores Data & Behavioral Insights: A name like "Dave" carries demographic bias—it subconsciously excludes women, non-Western donors, digital-native givers, and faith-driven philanthropists.

✅ How "Donor Dave" Maps Into Real High-Impact Donor Personas

Rather than flattening "Donor Dave" into a generic character, we analyze how his actual giving behavior and motivations align with a richer persona model.

1️⃣ If "Donor Dave" is a Data-Driven ROI Seeker → He’s a 🛠️ Strategic Impact Investor

💡 Traits:
✔️ Prioritizes impact measurement, predictive analytics, and data-backed ROI.
✔️ Interested in AI-driven philanthropy insights & long-term sustainability.
✔️ Prefers quantifiable social impact over emotional storytelling.

🗣️ What Donor Dave Might Say:
"I want to see the numbers. If I invest $100K, I need to know the projected outcomes and efficiency metrics. Show me the data on how my money is driving systemic change."

🔹 Better Persona Name: "The Strategic Impact Investor"
📌 Better Representation: This persona approach respects his analytical mindset while removing cultural bias.

2️⃣ If "Donor Dave" is Giving Through His Company → He’s a 🏢 Corporate Partner & Business-Aligned Donor

💡 Traits:
✔️ Gives through a business or CSR program rather than as an individual.
✔️ Wants brand alignment, measurable social ROI, and corporate impact reports.
✔️ Supports co-branded philanthropy projects, employee giving, and donation matching.

🗣️ What Donor Dave Might Say:
"We don’t do philanthropy for charity’s sake. It has to align with our company’s mission, employee engagement, and overall brand impact. What’s the PR and business case for this initiative?"

🔹 Better Persona Name: "The Corporate Philanthropy Partner"
📌 Better Representation: Moves away from a singular "Dave" narrative and recognizes company-driven giving behaviors.

3️⃣ If "Donor Dave" is an Emotion-Driven Giver → He’s a ❤️ Heart-Driven Donor

💡 Traits:
✔️ Donates because of deep personal connection & storytelling impact.
✔️ Prefers letters from beneficiaries, personal thank-yous, and face-to-face interactions.
✔️ Engages with mission trips, photos, and emotional storytelling over spreadsheets.

🗣️ What Donor Dave Might Say:
The spreadsheet doesn’t move me. When I see a child’s face, hear their story, and receive a personal letter, THAT’S when I know my donation is making a difference."

🔹 Better Persona Name: "The Heart-Driven Giver"
📌 Better Representation: Centers emotional motivation over financial identity.

4️⃣ If "Donor Dave" Engages His Family in Giving → He’s a Generational Giver

💡 Traits:
✔️ Views philanthropy as a family legacy and passes giving habits down.
✔️ Involves children and grandchildren in donation decisions.
✔️ Looks for ways to educate younger generations about giving.

🗣️ What Donor Dave Might Say:
"Giving isn’t just my thing—it’s part of our family culture. My kids need to see that impact, which is why we discuss our donations together."

🔹 Better Persona Name: "The Generational Philanthropist"
📌 Better Representation: Recognizes intergenerational giving patterns rather than reducing donors to individuals.

5️⃣ If "Donor Dave" is a Social & Digital-First Donor → He’s a 🌱 Next-Gen Digital Giver

💡 Traits:
✔️ Engages in digital philanthropy (crypto giving, social media fundraising, crowdfunding).
✔️ Wants real-time updates and gamified impact dashboards.
✔️ Prefers decentralized giving rather than structured endowments.

🗣️ What Donor Dave Might Say:
"Why isn’t this automated? I should be able to donate via crypto and see my impact through a live dashboard. This process feels outdated."

🔹 Better Persona Name: "The Next-Gen Digital Philanthropist"
📌 Better Representation: Removes Western-biased assumptions and highlights behavior-based segmentation.

🌡️ Final Takeaways

1. Replace "Donor Dave" with Data-Backed Personas

Instead of a one-size-fits-all donor archetype, we create personas that reflect real behaviors, motivations, and engagement styles.

📌 Why This Approach is Better:
✅ Removes cultural and gender biases embedded in names like "Dave".
✅ Incorporates real donor motivations instead of assumed demographics.
✅ Provides actionable insights for PMs & engineers to personalize giving experiences.

Recommendation: Drop "Donor Dave." Use data-driven, research-backed personas that empower inclusive donor engagement.

2. If It Feels Like a Nickname, It’s a Bad Persona Name

  1. Persona naming should be thoughtful, strategic, and grounded in real user behaviors.
  2. If a persona name sounds like a corporate training exercise, it’s likely reinforcing bias rather than driving research insights.
  3. Compassion International has a moral and ethical obligation to represent donors and beneficiaries with dignity, respect, and accuracy. That starts with responsible UX research practices—including persona naming.

🧯 Suggested Next Steps

  1. ✅ Audit all existing persona names for bias and oversimplification.
  2. ✅ Ensure personas reflect behavioral engagement, not stereotyped assumptions.
  3. ✅ Use evidence-backed naming conventions that respect identity and agency.
  4. ✅ Engage research participants in the persona validation process.
