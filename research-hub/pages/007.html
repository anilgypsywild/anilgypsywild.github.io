Quantifying Donor Motivations: MaxDiff Insights and Prospect Roadmap

Prepared For:

  • @EKim5 (Marketing PM) : to align donor motivators with campaign messaging and outreach strategy.
  • @SMcMillen1 (Product Owner, Causes) : to prioritize product features that emphasize clarity, urgency, and transparency in the donation flow.
  • @AThompson8 (UX Director) : to validate usability priorities and ensure motivator-driven design decisions ladder up to the 4D UX Scorecard.
  • @dsharer (Engineering Lead) : to scope and implement features (e.g., progress visuals, personalization) that directly support top donor motivators.
  • @SLamb2 (Designer) : to translate motivator insights into clear, compelling visual and interaction design choices.

1. Objective

To identify and quantify the key motivational drivers for first-time and repeat giving on the Compassion Causes platform, using MaxDiff (Best-Worst Scaling) to establish a clear hierarchy of donor motivations and segment differences.

2. Methodology Recap

  • MaxDiff design: Respondents shown sets of 4–5 motivators; selected most and least motivating.
  • Output: Utilities scaled into 0–100 importance scores.
  • Sample: Balanced across CurrentGen (35+), NextGen (18–34), First-time donors, and Repeat donors.
  • Analysis: Overall importance hierarchy, segment comparisons, rank heatmaps, dumbbell charts, and bootstrap confidence intervals.

3. Key Findings

Overall Relative Importance

Top motivators across all donors (95% CI):

  1. Transparency: “I clearly understand where my money is going”
  2. Impact: “I can make a tangible impact through my donation”
  3. Faith alignment: “Supporting this cause aligns with my Christian faith and values”
  4. Personalization: “I can see a specific project or child I am supporting”
  5. Urgency: “The cause feels urgent and needs support right away”

Lowest motivators:

  1. Progress bar/percentage funded
  2. Peer support (“I see other people in my network are supporting this cause”)
  3. Organizational thanks

Top motivators across donors are clarity, tangible impact, and faith alignment. Personalization and urgency also matter but rank lower. Progress bars, peer cues, and generic thanks have minimal influence.

5. Insights by Function

Market Research

  • Confirms MaxDiff as a prioritization tool that reveals transparency and impact as dominant motivators.
  • Segment splits show opportunities for message tailoring in campaigns: urgency + emotional resonance for younger donors, trust + faith alignment for older donors.

Product Ownership

  • Core product flows must foreground impact transparency and clarity of use of funds.
  • Urgency tags can be selectively used to boost NextGen and Repeat donations.
  • Simplify or de-emphasize progress bars, as they underperform in importance.

UX Leadership

  • Findings strengthen the case for streamlined flows with embedded clarity.
  • UX should emphasize faith and impact messaging without clutter.
  • Segment-specific UX interventions (e.g., urgency cues for younger donors) can be piloted.

Engineering

  • Limited ROI for progress bar engineering investment.
  • Prioritize transparency features (transaction clarity, allocation breakdowns).
  • Build lightweight urgency flags that are flexible for A/B testing.

Design

  • Visual design should foreground transparency and impact (clear use of funds, stories tied to outcomes).
  • Faith-aligned visual cues resonate strongly for CurrentGen; urgent/emotional imagery for NextGen.
  • De-prioritize social proof elements (peer donations, network sharing).

Research Next Steps

  • Recommend a follow-up conjoint or message test to validate combinations of motivators (transparency + urgency vs transparency + faith).
  • Explore field A/B tests with urgency cues targeted at younger cohorts.
  • Run usability studies on transparency-focused flows (allocation breakdowns, impact stories).

6. Recommendations

  1. Prioritize clarity and transparency in all messaging and UI.
  2. Tailor urgency cues for younger and repeat donors.
  3. Highlight tangible impact consistently; tie stories directly to donation results.
  4. De-prioritize progress bars and peer visibility, as these do not motivate giving.
  5. Embed faith-aligned framing in older-donor-targeted flows and campaigns.

A2. Mapping MaxDiff Insights to Strawman Proposal

Discovery / Define (Stages 1–2)

  • Quant insights confirm problem statement: donors drop when transparency and clarity are weak.
  • Baseline numbers: Top 5 motivators capture ~55% cumulative weight, top 10 exceed 90%.
  • Downstream: Personas sharpened with data-driven segments (NextGen vs CurrentGen).

Design (Stage 3)

  • Success criteria for prototypes include clear impact communication, urgency cues, and faith alignment.
  • Designs tested with MaxDiff priorities as benchmark (e.g., if transparency is not obvious, fail criteria).

Build (Stage 4)

  • Engineering implements high-weight motivator features first (progress indicators, impact visuals).
  • Avoids building features with <5% weight (e.g., peer endorsement).

Post-Launch (Stage 5)

  • Validate if top motivators drive better 4D metrics:
    • SUS/CSAT gains for clarity
    • NPS uplift from trust/faith alignment
    • DAU/retention from transparency
  • Longitudinal tracking verifies if 60% transparency preference translates into actual repeat engagement.

A3. Why These Quant Results Matter

  • Strategic: Hard data from MaxDiff anchors stakeholder decisions in what donors value most, not opinions.
  • Operational: Provides numeric thresholds (55% from top 5 motivators, 60% weight transparency) to measure ROI on features and design choices.
  • Downstream: Reduces scope creep by deprioritizing motivators with statistically insignificant weights; strengthens executive confidence with quant evidence.
