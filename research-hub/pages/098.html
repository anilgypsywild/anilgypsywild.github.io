Methods and their purpose

Here’s a table to help you choose UX research methods based on the research type: Each method provides unique insights depending on the research focus and goals.

Research Type UX Research Methods Purpose
Generative Research Ethnographic Studies, Contextual Inquiry, User Interviews To explore user needs, motivations, pain points, and behaviors in-depth to inspire new ideas.
Evaluative Research Usability Testing (moderated/unmoderated), Heuristic Evaluation To assess how well users can complete tasks and identify usability issues with an existing design.
Behavior Analytics Heatmaps, Clickstream Analysis, Session Replays To observe and analyze user behavior patterns on a site or app, identifying trends and optimizations.
Focus Groups Focus Group Sessions To gather qualitative feedback, user opinions, and preferences in a group setting for new concepts.
Surveys & Questionnaires Surveys, Likert Scale Questions, NPS, CSAT To gather quantitative data on user satisfaction, needs, and overall attitudes toward the product.
Diary Studies Diary Study, Cultural Probes To capture user behavior, motivations, and routines over time in real-world contexts.
Card Sorting Open/Closed Card Sorting To understand user mental models and organize content effectively for navigation structures.
A/B Testing Split Testing To compare different versions of a design or feature to see which performs better for users.
Field Studies Observational Studies, Field Research To observe users in their natural environments, identifying real-world interactions with the product.
Competitive Analysis Benchmarking, Comparative Usability Testing To compare usability and features of the product against competitors.

Which Method to use When?

Here's a breakdown of when to use each UX research method based on the research phase and goals: Using the appropriate method at each phase ensures actionable insights, aligning design decisions with real user needs and behaviors.

Method When to Use Purpose
User Interviews Early stages or anytime in the product lifecycle Gain deep insights into user needs, motivations, and pain points to inform product strategy
Surveys After major releases or to collect broad user feedback Gather quantitative data on user satisfaction, preferences, and demographics
Usability Testing Mid to late design phase and pre-launch Evaluate ease of use, identify usability issues, and ensure users can complete tasks
Ethnographic Studies Early stages when discovering user contexts and behaviors Observe users in their real environments to understand workflows, culture, and pain points
Focus Groups Conceptual stages when gathering qualitative group feedback Gather group opinions and preferences on concepts or early designs
A/B Testing Post-launch for feature optimization Compare two design or feature versions to find which performs better
Card Sorting When designing information architecture or navigation structures Organize content based on user expectations to improve navigation
Field Studies Discovery phase, pre- or post-launch Observe users interacting with the product in real-world contexts
Heuristic Evaluation Early design stage or during usability checks Identify usability issues based on established principles or heuristics
Clickstream Analysis After launch to analyze user journeys Track behavior patterns, most-used paths, and drop-off points to optimize user flows
Diary Studies When needing long-term user insights (behavior, attitudes over time) Capture in-context, time-based behaviors and emotions over days or weeks
Competitive Analysis Pre-design or redesign phases Understand the strengths and weaknesses of competing products to inform feature prioritization
Heatmaps Post-launch or with live prototypes to track interaction patterns Visualize engagement, identify popular areas, and understand user interest areas
Contextual Inquiry During early design to understand tasks within real contexts Observe and interact with users to gather task-specific insights
