Shneiderman’s Eight Golden Rules of Interface Design

Use the rules to inspect an interface systematically, then investigate the consequential risks through observation and testing.

Ben Shneiderman’s original Eight Golden Rules provide the source framework. The questions below are practical interpretations for evaluating an experience.

RuleEvaluation questionEvidence to record
ConsistencyDo equivalent actions, labels, and states behave predictably?Conflicting terminology, patterns, or outcomes.
Universal usabilityCan people with different abilities, devices, languages, and experience use the flow?Context specific access barriers and missing support.
Informative feedbackCan people tell what happened after an action?Missing, delayed, ambiguous, or disproportionate feedback.
ClosureIs it clear when a meaningful sequence has finished?Uncertain completion, missing confirmation, or unclear next steps.
Error preventionDoes the interface prevent foreseeable mistakes and support recovery?Error conditions, consequences, and recovery demands.
ReversalCan people safely undo or revise their actions?Irreversible choices, lost work, or unnecessary anxiety.
User controlCan people initiate, interrupt, and leave interactions appropriately?Forced actions, unwanted changes, or blocked exits.
Memory loadIs the information needed for an action available at that moment?Required recall, repeated entry, or cross-screen comparison.

Turn a review into a researchable issue

Record the task, screen or interaction, observed design condition, affected rule, likely consequence, and evidence. Distinguish an expert prediction from a difficulty observed with a participant. Explain severity through impact, frequency, persistence, and context.

Recommend a change, state what it is expected to improve, and identify how the team will evaluate it. Also record effective elements that a redesign should preserve.

Use the framework with judgment

The rules organize an expert review; they do not establish that a product is usable or prove that a recommendation will work. Combine the review with relevant accessibility standards, domain constraints, task performance, and user research.
