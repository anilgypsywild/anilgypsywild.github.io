Measurement infrastructure · Reporting guide

Longitudinal UX Scorecards

Track whether an experience improves across releases while retaining the context needed to interpret a change.

This is a reporting structure, not a completed six cycle study. Use the archived group scorecard as an example of the measures that need definitions and denominators before longitudinal comparison.

Retain a stable measurement spine

FieldWhat to retainWhy it matters
Experience and releaseProduct, workflow, design version, and measurement dates.Identify what actually changed between observations.
Population and sampleEligibility, recruitment, device, context, and sample size.Distinguish experience changes from changes in who responded.
BehaviorTask definitions, success rules, time, errors, and denominators.Compare equivalent actions and outcomes.
PerceptionQuestionnaire version, exact response anchors, scoring, and missing data rules.A rescaled or adapted questionnaire is not automatically comparable with the original.
AdoptionActive use definitions, cohorts, exposure, and observation window.Separate intention, initial use, and sustained engagement.
UncertaintyDistributions, effect estimates, interval method, and study design.A point estimate alone cannot establish meaningful improvement.
DecisionOwner, decision threshold, chosen action, and next review.Connect measurement with an accountable product response.

Interpret the trajectory before naming a cause

Check whether tasks, users, instruments, timing, and implementation changed. Examine seasonality, recruitment, missing responses, and competing changes. Repeated observational scores do not isolate the effect of a redesign.

Preserve local meaning in a shared structure

Use the 4D scorecard to organize the evidence. A lower task time may help a donation workflow; longer voluntary engagement may matter in a community experience. Retain each measure’s direction, unit, and context rather than average incompatible measures into a single score.

Report the decision, then the evidence

State the decision needed, summarize the change and uncertainty, explain relevant limitations, and record what happens next. Link to the instrument definitions and study record so the result remains inspectable.
