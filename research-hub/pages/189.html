Research Protocol: SCP–FCP Concept Validation (Pastor-to-Pastor Portal)

  • Prepared for: @AThompson8 , @CDant
  • Date: October 29, 2025 (updated)
  • Study Type: Concept Validation (Mixed Qual–Quant)
  • Research Mode: Moderated + Recorded (Lyssna Prototype Flow)

1. Background

  • The SCP–FCP Pastor-to-Pastor Portal is a conceptual digital experience designed to enable direct, Christ-centered collaboration between Supporting Church Partners (SCPs) and Frontline Church Partners (FCPs).
  • The initiative emerged from recurring field feedback: pastors report limited human connection and transparency when partnerships are mediated solely through Relationship Managers (RMs).

This study seeks to validate whether a direct, prayer-anchored, and mission-driven connection tool strengthens compassion, accountability, and spiritual resonance across church partnerships.

2. Research Problem

Current ministry connections rely heavily on administrative channels, causing:

  1. Low relational visibility (“I don’t know who my partner pastor is.”)
  2. Limited prayer reciprocity (requests filtered or delayed through RMs)
  3. Reduced sense of shared impact (delayed or missing feedback loops)

The proposed SCP–FCP Portal introduces a pastor-to-pastor channel for prayer exchange, progress updates, and joint mission tracking.

This research evaluates the perceived value, usability, and emotional resonance of this concept before production investment.

3. Research Objectives

Objective Key Question Decision Intent
Concept Validation Do pastors perceive the portal as valuable and spiritually aligned with their mission? Proceed / Revise / Pause
Faith Resonance Does the experience feel peaceful, uplifting, and Christ-honoring? Qualify for ministry investment
Adoption Is the experience simple enough for regular ministry use? Define readiness for pilot

4. Method Overview

  • Method Type: Moderated Concept Test (via Lyssna)
  • Design: 6-screen clickable prototype walkthrough
  • Participants:
    • 3 SCP pastors (U.S./large church contexts)
    • 3 FCP pastors (Global South, e.g., Ghana, Thailand)
    • 1 Relationship Manager observer
  • Session Duration: 30–40 minutes
  • Data Type: Mixed (Quant + Qual)

5. Measures & Instruments

The study applies CVG’s 4D Framework (Discover, Define, Design, Disciple) and a quantitative UX strawman using short-form validated instruments:

Construct Measurement Source Example Output
Effort Nasa TLX (if necessary) Ease & clarity of use
Emotional Resonance Serenity, PSI, & Meaningfulness scales (adapted) Spiritual grounding, trust, purpose
Adoption Intent Frequency via NPS Predicted usage patterns: promoters, detractors, neutrals.
Faith Alignment Qualitative coding under Disciple Evidence of ministry fit

Appendix

This appendix provides the following overview:

  1. A moderation script for the Lyssna concept test (screen-by-screen walkthrough).
  2. A validated emotional resonance framework grounded in ACM HCI and psychology literature.

It serves as the operational “how-to” guide for field moderators and a defensible measurement reference for leadership and IRB review.

A1. Session Overview

  • Format: Moderated concept walkthrough using six clickable prototype screens
  • Duration: 30–40 minutes
  • Participants: SCP pastors, FCP pastors, Relationship Managers, DI/Cultural leads
  • Tone: Respectful, Christian, non-technical, conversational
  • Moderator Rule: Read prompts verbatim unless clarification is required

A2. Moderation Script

Section 0 – Setup & Warm-Up

Moderator Introduction:

Thank you for meeting with us today. We are exploring a Compassion tool to help two churches work together directly in Christ—sharing prayer, collaborating on ministry goals, and acting quickly for real needs.
This is an early concept. We want your honest feedback about whether it feels valuable, clear, and spiritually meaningful. There are no right or wrong answers.
We will record the session to ensure accuracy. Is that alright?

Warm-Up Questions:

  1. How do you currently stay connected with partner churches or pastors (local or international)?
  2. When someone in your church needs urgent prayer or support, how do you share that request? (phone, WhatsApp, Facebook etc.)
  3. Do you use social platforms for ministry? If yes, which ones feel appropriate—or distracting—and why?

(Baseline sentiment on secular vs. sacred digital contexts.)

Section 1 – Screen 1: Dashboard Overview

Moderator Shows: High-level dashboard with SCP–FCP connection, shared mission, and quick-links (Prayer Requests, Updates, Resources, Impact).

Pre-Explanation:

  1. What do you think this page is for?
  2. Who would use this—pastor, staff, RM, someone else?

Context to Provide:

This dashboard is the home page for two churches partnered in ministry. It shows shared goals, requests, and impact updates.

Follow-Ups / Likert Items:

  • How meaningful is what you see here for doing real ministry work in Jesus’ name?
    1 = Not meaningful … 5 = Extremely meaningful [Meaningfulness]
  • Could you see yourself opening this page during ministry work?
    1 = Never … 5 = Definitely Yes [Adoption Intention]

Section 2 – Screen 2: Pastor Profile

Moderator Shows: Profile for Rev. Jasper (Ghana) – church vision, ministry focus, resources.

Pre-Explanation:

  1. What does this screen show you?
  2. Does it give enough context about the other pastor?

Provide Context:

This allows you to learn about your partner pastor directly—without relying solely on a Relationship Manager.

Follow-Ups / Likert Items:

  • How personally connected do you feel to this pastor? 1 = Not at all … 5 = Deeply connected [Relational Trust]
  • Would this level of information build trust between your churches? Yes / Maybe / No

Section 3 – Screen 3: Shared Mission Focus

Moderator Shows: Example partnership—Restoration of Abused Families, with 3–4 joint goals.

Questions:

  1. How clear is the shared mission? (Very / Somewhat / Not clear)
  2. How urgent or important does this feel to you personally? (Very / Somewhat / Not)
  3. Would this change how you pray, preach, or mobilize volunteers? (Yes / Maybe / No – why?)
  4. How spiritually important is this page to you? 1 = Not … 5 = Extremely important [Spiritual Resonance]

Section 4 – Screen 4: Resource Exchange

Moderator Shows: FCP lists resources they have and needs they seek from SCP.

Questions:

  1. Do you understand what is asked of you? (Yes / Somewhat / No)
  2. Would your church know how to respond? (Self / Staff / Not sure)
  3. Do you feel overwhelmed by these requests? (Overwhelmed / Reasonable / Too light)
  4. How mentally difficult would it be to act on this? 1 = Very easy … 5 = Very hard [Cognitive Load]
  5. How frustrating would it feel to work through this? 1 = Not … 5 = Very frustrating [Frustration]

Section 5 – Screen 5: Prayer Request Flow

Moderator Shows: Emergency prayer request form with status updates.

Questions:

  1. What can you do here? (Open text)
  2. How spiritually powerful is this feature for you? 1 = Not … 5 = Extremely powerful [Spiritual Resonance]
  3. Would you use this in your ministry within six months? 1 = Definitely not … 5 = Definitely yes [Adoption Intent]
  4. Where do you currently do this? (Facebook / WhatsApp / Text / Phone / Other)
  5. Do Facebook or TikTok feel like the right place for this kind of request? (Yes / No – why?) [Platform Fit]

Section 6 – Screen 6: Impact Dashboard

Moderator Shows: Impact metrics – children reached, testimonies, resources used.

Questions:

  1. What is this dashboard telling you? (Open text)
  2. Would this help you report impact to your church? (Yes / Maybe / No – how?)
  3. How often would you view this dashboard? 1 = Daily … 5 = Rarely or Never [Adoption Frequency]
  4. Does this help you feel Compassion is a good steward of resources? 1 = Strong no … 5 = Strong yes [Stewardship Perception]

Section 7 – Closing Questions

  1. Compared to Facebook/TikTok/GoFundMe, how does this experience feel? (More peaceful / Same / More chaotic – why?) [Emotional Comparison]
  2. Does this tool help you live out your calling in Christ? 1 = Strong no … 5 = Strong yes [Spiritual Alignment]
  3. Should Compassion invest to build this? (Build / Build with changes / Not worth building – why?) [Investment Intent]
  4. In one word, how would you describe this experience? (Open text)

Moderator Close:

Thank you for sharing your thoughts. Your insights help us discern whether this concept should be built and how to build it in a way that honors Christ and strengthens church relationships.

A3. Emotional Resonance Measurement Framework

A. Construct 1 – Spiritual Peace & Grounding

Adapted from the Spiritual Well-Being and Brief Serenity Scales (The Brief Serenity Scale: A Psychometric Analysis of a Measure of Spiritual Peace)

Sample Items:

  • Using this made me feel spiritually grounded and focused on God’s work.
  • I felt a sense of peace while imagining using this.
  • I felt that what I was doing here truly mattered in the Kingdom.
    (Likert 1–5 Strongly Disagree → Strongly Agree)

B. Construct 2 – Relational Trust & Connection

Adapted from Parasocial Interaction literature (Research Trends on Parasocial Interactions and Relationships)

Sample Items:

  • After seeing this, I feel like I personally know the other pastor.
  • I would feel comfortable reaching out to this pastor directly.
  • I trust that this pastor’s needs are real.
    (Likert 1–5 Strongly Disagree → Strongly Agree)

C. Construct 3 – Meaningfulness & Purpose in Technology Use

Adapted from ACM HCI wellbeing and digital support research (Understanding Mental Wellbeing and Tools for Support)

Sample Items:

  • This experience supports what I believe I am called to do in ministry.
  • This experience helps me care for people in crisis.
  • This experience feels respectful of my time and attention.
    (Likert 1–5 Strongly Disagree → Strongly Agree)

D. Construct 4 – Adoption & Frequency of Use

Aligned with digital health and support platform retention models (ACM CHI 2024)

Sample Items:

  • I would use this tool weekly or more in my ministry.
  • I would recommend this tool to other pastors.
  • I would replace existing channels (e.g., WhatsApp) with this for prayer or partnership.

CVG 4D Pillar Measurement Focus Example Items

Define Concept Value & Clarity “Would you use this?” / Adoption Intent

A5. Analysis Plan

  1. Quantitative:
    • Compute means for Likert items by construct.
    • Compare SCP vs FCP participants for variance.
    • Report Composite Emotional Resonance Score = avg(Spiritual Peace + Relational Trust + Meaningfulness).
  2. Qualitative:
    • Code verbatims under CVG 4D themes.
    • Extract quotes illustrating resonance (“peaceful,” “purposeful,” “trusting”).
  3. Decision Signal:
    • ≥ 80 % value perceived → Proceed to Design Validation
    • 60–79 % → Iterate prototype
    • < 60 % → Reframe concept
