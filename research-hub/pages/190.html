Research Brief

🗺️ Part 1: Evaluating FCP Capacity, Confidence, and Digital Self-Efficacy Outcomes

  • Product Area: Frontline Church Partner (FCP)/ Community App
    • Create a Cause and related activities
  • Research Type: Post-activity outcome evaluation (quantitative with lightweight qualitative support
  • Status: Proposed (Jan 26, 2026)

1. Background and Origin of the Ask

As the FCP Community App matures, stakeholders have proposed KPI questions intended to capture the broader benefits of FCP participation, beyond task completion.

The underlying intent of these KPIs is to assess whether FCP engagement with the app contributes to:

  1. Increased confidence using digital tools
  2. Improved ability to complete online tasks
  3. Greater understanding of project sustainability
  4. Increased confidence mobilizing local community resources

These KPIs are positioned as capacity-building outcomes for FCPs, not as usability or performance metrics.

This research brief evaluates whether the proposed KPI questions are:

  1. Appropriate for FCP contexts
  2. Measuring the intended constructs
  3. Interpretable for before–after analysis

It also proposes minimal revisions to improve validity while preserving original intent.

2. Research Objective

Evaluate whether participation in FCP Community activities is associated with perceived gains in:

  1. Digital self-efficacy for ministry work
  2. Procedural confidence completing online tasks
  3. Understanding of locally sustainable projects
  4. Confidence communicating and mobilizing within the local community

3. Conceptual Framing

These KPIs are not measures of technical literacy. They primarily assess perceived capability and confidence, which are widely accepted outcomes in:

  • Development programs
  • Capacity-building interventions
  • Low-resource and frontline contexts

The appropriate theoretical framing is:

  • Digital self-efficacy, not digital skill mastery
  • Empowerment and agency, not training completion

4. Measurement Approach

Research Design

Retrospective pre–post self-assessment

Participants respond once, after completing the activity, using the following framing:

Thinking about before and after this activity, please respond to the statements below.

This method is appropriate when:

  • True baseline data is unavailable
  • Participants’ understanding of the construct evolves during the activity

This approach mitigates response-shift bias and is widely used in development and education research.

🧪 Quantitative Interpretation Guidance

  • Scores above 5 indicate perceived positive change
  • Distribution, variance, and directional movement are more meaningful than absolute means
  • Results should be interpreted as capacity and confidence signals, not proof of skill acquisition

Required Qualitative Companion Question

To improve interpretability and capture unanticipated outcomes, include one open-ended question:

What is one thing you feel more capable of doing now than before this activity?

Optional behavioral grounding:

Since completing this activity, have you shared your project with anyone in your community?
(Yes/ Not yet)

⚠️ Risks and Constraints

Risk Mitigation
Over-claiming literacy gains Frame outcomes as confidence and capability
Social desirability bias Use relative before–after wording
Construct overlap Interpret KPIs in grouped domains, not individually

🗒️ Part 2: Output & Use

This research supports:

  • Directional evaluation of FCP capacity-building outcomes
  • Product and program narratives around empowerment
  • Longitudinal tracking of confidence and agency signals
  • Note: It does not certify technical proficiency or training completion.

Thank You Screen

Thank you for your time and for the work you do in your community.

Quick Notes

  • This measures confidence and capacity, not digital literacy certification
  • Use retrospective pre–post framing exactly as written
  • Treat results as directional signals
  • Look at distribution and change, not just averages
  • Q6 is critical for interpretation and storytelling

🛤️ Reporting Guardrails

  • Do not compare these KPIs numerically to SUS or TLX
  • Use as supporting evidence for Delight and Desire
  • Report changes quarter over quarter
  • Pair with at least one qualitative quote when possible

💵 Executive Interpretation

These KPIs answer:

  • Do FCPs feel more capable?
  • Do they feel safer and more confident doing their work?

They do not answer:

  • Are FCPs digitally literate?
  • Are workflows optimized?
