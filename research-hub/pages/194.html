Introduction

This document presents an enhanced version of the FCP Survey incorporating insights from Contextual Interviews, Thailand learnings, and Survey Design Best Practices.

The A version contains the original survey questions, while the B version presents the enhanced questions, each with a rationale for the modifications.

Survey Sections with Enhancements

1. General Satisfaction

A Version (Original Question):

  1. On a scale of 1-7, how satisfied are you with the FCP Community app?
  2. How likely are you to recommend this app to other FCPs? (0-10)

B Version (Enhanced Question with Contextual Insights & Survey Design Best Practices):

  1. Thinking about your daily workflow, how well does the app support your tasks and communication needs? (1-7 scale, from Not At All to Very Well)

    Rationale: Contextual interviews in Peru showed that FCPs view platform usability through the lens of task efficiency, not just overall satisfaction. This rewording improves construct validity, ensuring that the responses directly measure usability in relation to actual work processes rather than generic satisfaction.

  2. If you needed to explain the value of this platform to another FCP, how would you describe it?

    Rationale: Open-ended questions like this reduce social desirability bias and provide deeper insights into perceived platform benefits, beyond a simple Likelihood-to-Recommend (LTR) score.

2. Trust Scale

A Version (Original Question):

  1. The app is straightforward and transparent. (1-7 scale)
  2. The app aligns with our mission and values. (1-7 scale)
  3. I feel confident in the app’s intentions, actions, and outputs. (1-7 scale)

B Version (Enhanced Question with Contextual Insights & Survey Design Best Practices):

  1. The app clearly communicates how and when my updates will be shared with donors. (1-7 scale)

    Rationale: Contextual interviews in Peru and Thailand highlighted that lack of clarity on content sharing processes causes trust issues. This improved wording aligns with Shneiderman’s Rule of Transparency, making the question more actionable.

  2. How well does the platform reflect the values and mission of your FCP? (1-7 scale, with an additional open-ended follow-up question if rated below 4)

    Rationale: Thailand research found misalignment between perceived app functionality and organizational mission. Adding a conditional follow-up question ensures we capture why users feel the platform does/does not reflect their mission.

3. Usability Scale (Lite)

A Version (Original Question):

  1. The app’s features and functions meet my requirements. (1-5 scale)
  2. The app is easy to use. (1-5 scale)

B Version (Enhanced Question with Contextual Insights & Survey Design Best Practices):

  1. How easily can you complete the following tasks on the app? (Uploading an update, sharing an impact story, checking donor engagement, navigating past updates). (1-5 scale, with "Not Attempted" as an option)

Rationale: By breaking usability into specific, measurable tasks, this question aligns with ISO 9241 usability principles and reduces vagueness from subjective “easy to use” phrasing.

  1. Have you experienced difficulties using the app due to internet connectivity issues? If so, what happened?

Rationale: Peru research emphasized poor internet access as a major blocker. This ensures the survey captures infrastructure-related usability issues and not just platform design problems.

4. User Experience Feedback

A Version (Original Question):

  1. How does this app make you feel? (Emoji-based scale from very happy to very sad)

B Version (Enhanced Question with Contextual Insights & Survey Design Best Practices):

  1. The last time you used the app, what emotion best described your experience? (Joy, Frustration, Confidence, Confusion, Other: ___)

Rationale: Cognitive Load Theory suggests that emojis may not fully capture nuanced emotions. Giving concrete emotion labels ensures higher reliability in emotional sentiment measurement.

5. Content Creation and Engagement

A Version (Original Question):

  1. What is one thing that frustrated you the most while using the app?

B Version (Enhanced Question with Contextual Insights & Survey Design Best Practices):

  1. When writing or posting updates, what is the most challenging part for you?

Rationale: This refines the scope to content creation challenges, aligning with Peru and Thailand findings that FCPs struggle with crafting updates. The improved wording ensures more actionable insights.

6. Retention & Future Engagement

A Version (Original Question):

  1. How likely are you to continue using the app after the beta test? (1-5 scale)

B Version (Enhanced Question with Contextual Insights & Survey Design Best Practices):

  1. What would encourage you to continue using the app after the beta test? (Check all that apply: More training, Better donor feedback, Improved content creation tools, Faster content approval, Other: ____)

Rationale: Thailand research indicated that usability alone does not drive long-term retention—structured engagement and clear impact feedback are key factors. This question provides clearer insights into retention drivers.

Takeaways

This enhanced B version applies key survey design principles from Peru Contextual Interviews, Thailand learnings, and best practices in survey methodology. By:

  1. Improving construct validity through task-specific questions
  2. Reducing social desirability bias by adding behavior-based questions
  3. Enhancing reliability by aligning survey phrasing with real-world FCP experiences
  4. Minimizing cognitive load by simplifying complex response structures

Next Steps

  1. Pilot this enhanced B version survey with a small FCP group for cognitive testing.
  2. Compare response quality and completion rates with A version to validate improvements.
  3. Use Mixpanel data to correlate survey responses with actual user behavior.
  4. Refine based on feedback before launching full-scale data collection.

By integrating these research-driven enhancements, we ensure the survey effectively captures real-world FCP needs and experiences while maintaining statistical rigor and usability best practices.
