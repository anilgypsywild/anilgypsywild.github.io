Research article

Context

This was an unmoderated usability test for Compassion Community with 5 users matching the sponsor persona.

Readout presentation

Insights grouped by research goal

  1. Evaluate the clarity and emotional impact of the "Your comment wasn’t posted" landing page.

    1. 1 out of 5 users were confused if the rationale are mistakes they made or what they should say
    2. 4 out of 5 users rated the reason their comment wasn't posted very clear. 1 out of 5 rated it somewhat clear, but had a question because they didn't think their comment was a direct violation of the community guideline.
      1. "I haven't offered any donations, I just asked a question. So yeah that'd be a question." - P3
    3. 3 out of 5 users suggested adding an option to appeal/request clarification or a way to edit and resubmit directly from the screen

  2. Assess whether sponsors understand why their comment was rejected after reading the landing page content.

    1. 5 out of 5 users understood why their comment wasn't posted and referenced the two rationale blocks
      1. "The comment wasn't posted because I went against community guidelines" - P5
    2. 5 out of 5 users rated it easy or very easy to explain why their comment wasn't posted
    3. There were mixed responses around if the user agreed with the reason for rejection (2 fully agreed, 2 somewhat agreed, and 1 felt neutral). Users typically would view the community guidelines as the "source of truth," even if they disagreed with aspects of it.
      1. Somewhat agreed - "I'm saying some context would help because I do not know what the community guidelines were...I need to review the community guidelines to be 100% sure [that I violated them]" - P1
      2. Neutral - "It wasn't a terrible comment.. wouldn't it help someone to offer more donations?" -P2

  3. Test if sponsors can find and understand the Community Guidelines from the landing page.

    1. 4 out of 5 users found the community guidelines and rated it easy to find them
      1. "That wasn't the clearest. It could've been a different color to make it more clear you can click on it" -P4
      2. It took one user 10 clicks around the page to find the community guidelines (P2)

  4. Explore where and when sponsors expect to see Community Guidelines (e.g., during onboarding, after rejection, or elsewhere).

    1. 4 out of 5 users expected to find the community guidelines on the landing page, but some cited it could be more clear visually that it's a link
      1. "It didn't look like a link" - P2
    2. 3 out of 5 users preferred to see the guidelines before posting a comment
      1. "If I read it once and understand and absorb it I might not make these mistakes at all" -P1
      2. "You should be accepting the terms and conditions that you accept the community guidelines" -P3
    3. 2 out of 5 users preferred to see the guidelines after a comment is rejected, with one citing they'd want to see it in context (right when they post the comment)
      1. "I'm not going to remember it even if I see it" -P4
      2. "As soon as I click on the post [to submit it], I should see the rejection reason" -P5

  5. Identify what sponsors want to do next after a comment is rejected and what features would support that.

    1. From the landing page
      1. 2 out of 5 users wanted more detail on what triggered the rejection
        1. "I want to see the full guidelines and how they validated it" - P1
        2. "Even if you highlight the area [of violation] it's referring to even that would be helpful" -P4
      2. 1 out of 5 users suggested a way to validate the comment as the user is typing, rather than having a whole review process
        1. "There's times when you don't even check notifications.. you should be able to revise in the moment" - P5
      3. The most popular "next actions" after seeing this page were "avoid posting similar comment in the future" (4/5 users), "edit and repost my comment" (3/5 users), and "abandon the comment and move on" (2/5 users).
    2. After users viewed the community guidelines page, the most popular "next actions" were
      1. "avoid posting similar comment in the future" (4/5 users), "edit and repost my comment" (2/5 users), and "abandon the comment and move on" (2/5 users).
      2. 1 user who previously responded "edit and repost" didn't select it this time (didn't explain why)

  6. Understand how a rejected comment affects sponsors’ motivation to engage again.

    1. 3/5 users said this rejection message has no impact on their willingness to continue engaging in the community.
      1. "I'm really glad they found it if anything it's positive" -P1
    2. 1/5 users said the rejection would make them somewhat discouraged to post again because they weren't posting a hurtful comment but one of good intent.
      1. "Especially for something that wasn't hurtful or hateful to post.. I would probably be really discouraged if I was offering donations and it was rejected" -P2
      2. Consider: Providing a guideline-specific path forward to encourage good actions that can't be taken in Community, but can be taken elsewhere (ex. you can't donate in Community, but you can donate to this Cause for your FCP)

    Additional insights

    1. 5 out of 5 users found the content on the community guidelines page easy to understand
      1. "The only thing missing would be examples for each guideline" -P1
      2. 2 users were confused by a mismatch between the community guidelines and the rejection rationale block on the landing page (missing personal information), indicating a need for consistency between the two screens.
      3. Some words users used to describe the community guidelines included: "cordial", "respectful", and "safe"
