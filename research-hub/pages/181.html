Community App Metrics

These hypotheses directly emerge from the Community App Metrics Vision and provide a strong foundation for: A/B tests, Behavioral data modeling, and Survey-sensor triangulation

🔭 1. Derived Product Hypotheses from Vision Diagram

Adoption Hypotheses

ID Hypothesis
H1 Increasing the number of active monthly FCP users leads to an increase in the number of Supporter users over time.
H2 Onboarding support that enables early adoption (first-time login and upload) within 7 days leads to higher monthly user retention.

Engagement Hypotheses

ID Hypothesis
H3 Higher numbers of posts with visible engagement (e.g., likes, comments) will increase the number of repeat visits by both FCPs and Supporters.
H4 Engagement with content (views, comments, reactions) will predict higher User Satisfaction scores (CSAT and NPS).
H5 Users who react to at least one post in their first visit are more likely to return within 14 days.

Emotional Sentiment Hypotheses

ID Hypothesis
H6 When emotional sentiment (measured by connectedness, trust, or impact perception) scores ≥5.5 on a 7-point scale, likelihood of user retention increases.
H7 FCP content that evokes high emotional sentiment is more likely to lead to Supporter engagement (e.g., reaction, comment).

Time to Value Hypotheses

ID Hypothesis
H8 Faster time to first engagement (e.g., first post, first reaction) results in higher monthly active user counts.
H9 Time to value is a leading indicator of app retention and a lagging predictor of User Satisfaction.

App Performance Hypotheses

ID Hypothesis
H10 Faster app performance (e.g., post load, reaction times) increases the likelihood of user actions per session.
H11 Technical usability issues reduce the likelihood of FCPs uploading more than one piece of content per month.

Ministry KPIs Linkage Hypotheses

ID Hypothesis
H12 Increases in Engagement and Adoption are positively correlated with growth in donor Revenue and Retention.
H13 High-scoring Emotional Sentiment from Supporters mediates the effect of content engagement on donation behavior.

📊 2. Derived Product Hypotheses from Metrics Table

These are product hypotheses derived directly from the primary, secondary, and methodological metrics described. These hypotheses are framed to be testable, measurable, and aligned with Community platform, and engagement objectives.

Adoption Hypotheses

ID Hypothesis
ADOPT-H1 FCPs who log in at least weekly will be more likely to post content in the following month.
ADOPT-H2 Supporters who access the platform weekly are more likely to view and react to content than those who access it monthly.
ADOPT-H3 Time spent in app is positively correlated with future monthly active user status.
ADOPT-H4 A higher number of unique users per FCP predicts higher sponsor engagement metrics (views, comments).

Engagement Hypotheses

ID Hypothesis
ENG-H1 Posts that include comments or reactions are viewed more often than posts without interaction.
ENG-H2 Posts with emotional sentiment (positive comments or emojis) drive higher repeat visits to the app.
ENG-H3 A higher ratio of “likes/views” is associated with greater reported satisfaction among Supporters.
ENG-H4 Engagement increases when content includes personal language or narrative, as coded from post content.
ENG-H5 Posts shared outside the app (social shares) receive more comments and reactions than internal-only posts.
ENG-H6 The presence of video in a post increases likelihood of comments compared to static photos.

Satisfaction Hypotheses

ID Hypothesis
SAT-H1 Supporters who engage with content (views, reactions) will report higher CSAT/NPS than those who only receive notifications.
SAT-H2 FCPs who complete a post creation task without tech issues or rejections will report higher satisfaction (MERL survey).
SAT-H3 Platform NPS is higher among users who see content within the first 7 days of onboarding.

Time to Value Hypotheses

ID Hypothesis
TTV-H1 The shorter the time from first login to first view of a post, the higher the likelihood of that user returning within a week.
TTV-H2 Time from first login to first content reaction (like, comment) predicts higher likelihood of weekly engagement.
TTV-H3 Shorter time to first comment (by supporter or FCP) increases overall app session duration.

App Performance Hypotheses

ID Hypothesis
PERF-H1 Delays in post creation (measured by moderation or upload time) reduce likelihood of FCP posting again within 7 days.
PERF-H2 Apps with lower rejection rates (e.g., approved comments) see higher satisfaction scores from FCPs.
PERF-H3 Higher app downtime correlates with reduced weekly engagement from supporters.
PERF-H4 Users with faster image/video response times are more likely to engage with 3+ posts per session.

🪄 4. Quant-to-Qual Framework for Validating Core Community Platform Hypotheses

This a quant-to-qual translation framework for Key Product Hypotheses, designed to be used in surveys or interviews. Each hypothesis includes:

  1. A qualitative question (open-ended, exploratory)
  2. A quantitative plug-in version (Likert-style or structured)
  3. An optional follow-up for probing or triangulation

This structure helps us gather rich insights and turn them into scalable metrics.

1. FCP BURDEN: “Is it manageable or an added burden?”

Hypothesis: FCPs do not feel the Community Platform adds burden or cognitive load.

A. Qual Question

  • “Can you describe how it felt to upload a photo or video using the Community Platform compared to your usual ministry tasks?”

B. Quant Plug-In

  • Uploading content to the platform felt easy to integrate into my existing ministry duties.
    → 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 7 (Strongly Agree)
  • The time or energy required to share content felt…
    → 1 (Very manageable) to 7 (Very burdensome)

C. Follow-Up (Qual/Quant)

  • “What made the task feel easier or harder than other tasks you do for Compassion?”
  • I would be willing to do this type of sharing regularly.
    → Yes / No / Not sure

2. FCP SATISFACTION WITH PLATFORM WORKFLOW

Hypothesis: FCPs report higher satisfaction when post creation is smooth (low tech issues, rejections).

A. Qual Question

  • “What happened the last time you tried to share something using the app? Was anything frustrating or smooth?”

B. Quant Plug-In

  • I was able to upload my post without any errors or confusion.
    → 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 7 (Strongly Agree)
  • My post was approved without needing changes.
    → Yes / No / I don’t know
  • The process of uploading felt…
    → 1 (Very frustrating) to 7 (Very smooth)

3. SUPPORTER EMOTIONAL CONNECTION & FULFILLMENT

Hypothesis: Supporters feel more emotionally connected with authentic, immediate content from FCPs compared to static letters.

A. Qual Question

  • “How did seeing a real-time update from a church or child make you feel compared to getting a letter or photo in the mail?”

B. Quant Plug-In

  • I felt emotionally connected when viewing this photo/video.
    → 1 (Not at all connected) to 7 (Extremely connected)
  • The content felt more immediate and real than traditional letters.
    → 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 7 (Strongly Agree)
  • This content made me feel like my giving really matters.
    → 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 7 (Strongly Agree)

C. Follow-Up (Qual)

  • “What, if anything, made this content feel more meaningful or authentic to you?”

4. CONTENT TYPE IMPACT (Video vs. Static)

Hypothesis: Videos evoke more emotional impact and interaction than photos or text.

A. Qual Question

  • “Do you remember any video or photo you saw on the platform that really stood out? What made it memorable?”

B. Quant Plug-In

  • The video/photo I saw made me want to learn more or interact.
    → 1 (Not at all) to 7 (Very much so)
  • Video updates feel more personal than written letters.
    → 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 7 (Strongly Agree)

5. FCP MOTIVATION LOOP

Hypothesis: FCPs are more likely to continue uploading when they receive feedback (comments, reactions).

A. Qual Question

  • “Have you ever gotten a comment or reaction on something you posted? How did that feel?”

B. Quant Plug-In

  • Seeing a response to my post motivated me to share again.
    → 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 7 (Strongly Agree)
  • I believe sponsors appreciate the updates I provide.
    → 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 7 (Strongly Agree)
