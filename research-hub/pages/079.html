💡 Key point: this review is about matching each decision to the right evidence.

📨 The Original Request

The March 11 intake combines three asks:

  1. Evaluate whether the prototype direction is working
  2. Assess whether homepage click-through can move from 24% to 35%
  3. Assess whether overall conversion can move toward 4.5%

It also combines multiple desired outputs:

  • prototype feedback
  • usability findings
  • KPI movement
  • directional strategy for next steps

⚠️ Takeaway: the intake is not one research question; it is three questions in one document.

📐 How The Request Was Processed

The intake was translated into the actual decisions it implies:

Intake ask Real decision Valid evidence source
"Is the experience working?" Should we build this direction? prototype study
"Can CTR move from 24% to 35%?" Will real visitors explore causes more often? live analytics or experiment
"Can conversion reach 4.5%?" Will more real visitors donate? live analytics or experiment

Two things are true at once:

  • the asks are strategically related
  • they are not methodologically interchangeable

📏 Takeaway: once the intake is translated into decisions, it becomes clear that one study cannot answer all three well.

📊 What The Current Data Suggests

The existing GA snapshot gives a directional story:

Step Reported rate
Homepage -> Cause list 24%
Homepage -> Cause detail directly 10%
Cause list -> Cause detail 32%
Cause detail -> Add to cart 19.5%
Cart -> Begin checkout 87.3%
Checkout -> Purchase 68.6%

The strongest interpretation of this pattern is:

  • discovery looks weaker than it should
  • checkout mechanics look materially healthier
  • the larger issue is likely upstream, not at the end of checkout

🔍 Takeaway: the available data points more strongly to a top-of-funnel problem than to a late-funnel problem.

🧮 Why That GA Story Is Plausible

A simple reconstruction shows the current snapshot is directionally coherent:

detail reach
= direct detail reach
+ (cause-list reach x detail from list)
detail reach
= 10% + (24% x 32%)
= 17.68%
implied purchase rate
= 17.68% x 19.5% x 87.3% x 68.6%
= about 2.06%

This does not prove the funnel is perfectly measured.

It does show the current artifact is plausible enough to inform where the team should start.

📈 Takeaway: the GA artifact is directionally useful, but not strong enough to settle every question.

⚠️ Why The Current GA Snapshot Is Not Final

The current GA artifact is directional, not decision-grade.

For example, it includes both:

detail from list = 32%

and

detail from list when list is first page seen = about 5% to 6%

Those are different denominators.

Other limitations:

  • no sample counts
  • no confidence intervals
  • no exact event definitions
  • no segment cuts by device, channel, or cohort

🚫 Takeaway: current GA evidence is useful for orientation, but not sufficient for a hard KPI claim.

📏 Why One Prototype Study Cannot Answer All Three Questions

🧯 The measurement changes with the method.

Prototype study:

task click rate
= people who clicked the target
  divided by
  people given the task

Live site:

homepage CTR
= people who clicked into cause exploration
  divided by
  eligible homepage impressions

This may sound similar in casual conversation because both use the word click, but analytically they are not the same:

  • different denominator
  • different exposure context
  • different motivation context
  • different metric

🚩 Takeaway: a prototype click rate is not a live homepage CTR.

📉 Why Sample Size Matters

The requested lift is:

35% - 24% = 11 points

At a baseline of 24%, the approximate 95% margin of error for a small prototype sample is:

Sample size Approx. margin of error
12 +/- 24.2 points
18 +/- 19.7 points
30 +/- 15.3 points
50 +/- 11.8 points

This means:

  • prototype-scale samples are useful for directionality
  • they are too noisy to prove that kind of KPI lift

🔬 Takeaway: the requested effect is smaller than the uncertainty in a small prototype study.

📊 What A Real KPI Test Would Require

Approximate requirement to detect 24% -> 35%:

about 269 observations per arm

Approximate requirements for conversion:

Comparison Sample per arm
2.0% -> 4.5% ~789
3.3% -> 4.5% ~4,084

This is the scale difference.

  • CTR claims require live traffic
  • conversion claims require even more live traffic

📊 Takeaway: business-metric claims belong in analytics and experimentation, not in prototype testing alone.

🔬 What We Can Answer Well Right Now

The first research step is still highly valuable.

A prototype study can answer:

  1. what people notice first
  2. whether the story is clear
  3. whether the design builds trust
  4. whether users understand where to go next
  5. where hesitation or confusion occurs
  6. whether the direction is strong enough to move into build

It cannot cleanly answer:

  • whether live CTR will hit 35%
  • whether live conversion will hit 4.5%

✅ Takeaway: the first workstream should be optimized for concept quality, not KPI proof.

🧭 Proposed Research Strategy

Sequence the work by decision.

Step 1

Run one mixed-methods prototype study in Lyssna:

  • first click
  • task success
  • clarity
  • trust
  • hesitation and confusion

Step 2

Build a GA4 baseline:

  • canonical funnel
  • pathing
  • segment leakage

Step 3

Validate KPI lift in live traffic:

  • CTR
  • conversion
  • treatment versus control

➡️ Takeaway: the strategy is not to do less research; it is to do the right research in the right order.

🌱 Why Start With Step 1

🚅 This is the shortest defensible path.

If Step 1 goes well:

  • we gain confidence in the concept before build
  • we identify usability and messaging issues early
  • we reduce the risk of building the wrong thing

If Step 1 goes poorly:

  • we revise before investing further
  • we avoid attaching KPI expectations to a weak concept

🚀 Takeaway: starting with prototype evaluation is the fastest credible way to de-risk the work.

🤝 What I Need From The Team Today

Decisions for alignment:

  1. Confirm that the first question to answer is concept validity, not KPI lift
  2. Confirm that CTR and conversion will be treated as later live-measurement questions
  3. Confirm that the immediate deliverable is a prototype study and research shareout
  4. Confirm sequencing relative to other open work

📝 Takeaway: the most important decision today is not the final metric target; it is the order of operations.

🏃 Proposed Next Step

Move now on the first workstream.

Immediate next step:

  • approve the prototype study scope
  • finalize the Lyssna flow
  • align on participant criteria
  • field the study
  • review findings before moving to GA4 baseline and live validation

Discussion can then focus on:

  • scope
  • sequencing
  • ownership
  • timing

🏁 Takeaway: if we align on the first question now, the rest of the research plan becomes much cleaner.
