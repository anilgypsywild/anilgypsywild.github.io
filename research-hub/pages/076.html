Story Behind the Story: Lyssna Interactive Test Plan

Date: 2026-04-07

Status

Both mirrored Lyssna versions have been built and are currently pending internal review before launch.

  • Version 1: current Causes experience first, then SBS
  • Version 2: SBS first, then current Causes experience

Study Objective

The goal is to compare the current Compassion Causes browsing experience against the proposed Story Behind the Story (SBS) experience using a lightweight, directional interactive study.

This first round is intended to help the team evaluate:

  1. whether participants can identify a cause they would consider supporting
  2. whether the navigation, categories, filters, and labels are understandable
  3. whether the updated SBS experience feels at least as clear, usable, and compelling as the current experience

This study is not intended to prove live CTR lift or live conversion lift.

Conditions

  • Control: current Compassion Causes site
    • [reference omitted]
  • Variant: SBS prototype
    • [reference omitted]

Study Design

  • Design type: within-subjects comparative test
  • Platform: Lyssna
  • Device: desktop only
  • Mirrored order:
    • Version 1: Control first, then Variant
    • Version 2: Variant first, then Control

Each participant sees both experiences so the same person can respond to both conditions. The mirrored order is used to reduce presentation-order bias.

Screener

The screener is intentionally narrow and is used only for:

  1. Christian identity
  2. online donation habit
  3. prior exposure to Compassion Causes

Screener questions

  1. Do you currently identify as Christian?
    • Qualify: Yes
    • Disqualify: No, Prefer not to say
  2. Have you donated online to a charitable cause before?
    • Qualify:
      • Yes, within the last 12 months
      • Yes, but not within the last 12 months
      • No, but I would consider it
    • Disqualify:
      • No, and I would not consider it
  3. Before today, had you ever visited Compassion Causes?
    • Qualify: all options

Additional persona and audience-segmentation variables are captured in a short end-of-study survey rather than in the screener.

Study Flow

Welcome screen

Participants are told:

  1. they will use two versions of a Causes browsing experience
  2. the study is about ease of finding and evaluating causes
  3. they should not proceed to checkout or donation
  4. the study is being taken on desktop

Experience 1

The first experience is either Control or Variant, depending on the mirrored version.

Task 1: Choose a cause

Instruction:

Please use this experience to find the cause you would be most likely to support. You may click through as needed. Please stop once you reach the cause you would choose.

Follow-up:

  1. Why did you choose that cause?
  2. How easy was it to find a cause you would consider supporting?
  3. What helped you make that choice?

Task 2: Narrow the list

Instruction:

Imagine you want to narrow the list to causes that feel most relevant to you. Please use the experience's categories, filters, countries, or other navigation options to narrow the choices and identify one cause you would consider supporting. You may click through as needed, but please stop once you have reached the cause you would choose.

Follow-up:

  1. How clear were the categories, filters, countries, or other navigation options?
  2. Did those options help you narrow the choices? Why or why not?
  3. What, if anything, felt unclear or confusing?

Experience 1 ratings

Participants then answer:

  1. Overall, how satisfied were you with this experience? (CSAT, 1-5)
  2. How easy was it to use this experience overall? (1-5)
  3. How clear were the categories and labels overall? (1-5)
  4. How much would you trust Compassion Causes to use donations responsibly based on this experience? (1-5)
  5. Based on this experience, how likely would you be to continue toward supporting a cause? (1-5)
  6. How likely would you be to recommend this experience to someone looking to support a cause? (NPS, 0-10)
  7. What most influenced your trust, satisfaction, or willingness to continue?

Experience 2

The second experience is the opposite condition.

The same two tasks and the same rating questions are repeated.

Final comparison

After seeing both experiences, participants answer:

  1. Which experience made it easier to identify a cause you would consider supporting?
  2. Which experience made the categories and labels clearer?
  3. Which experience felt more trustworthy overall?
  4. Which experience would make you more likely to continue toward supporting a cause?
  5. What mattered most in your preference between the two experiences?

Options for Questions 1-4:

  • First experience
  • Second experience
  • No meaningful difference

End survey: audience and persona variables

The final survey captures additional segmentation variables for later analysis:

  1. age range
  2. importance of Christian faith in giving decisions
  3. browsing style:
    • knows what they want before arriving
    • wants help narrowing options
    • prefers to browse and discover
  4. what information matters most when choosing a cause:
    • outcome / impact
    • category / type
    • country / location
    • urgency
    • progress toward goal
    • visuals
    • story / explanation
  5. comfort using categories, filters, and navigation
  6. primary device used when browsing similar experiences
  7. motivational preference:
    • outcomes / impact
    • specific causes / projects
    • urgency / immediate need
    • stories / emotional connection
  8. types of causes most interesting to explore

These are intended to support later analysis of possible current-gen vs next-gen style differences and other persona-related patterns without biasing the screener.

Key Metrics

Behavioral

  1. completion path through the live website tasks
  2. choice behavior during cause selection
  3. use of categories, filters, countries, and navigation options

Rating-based

  1. CSAT
  2. ease of use
  3. clarity
  4. trust
  5. willingness to continue
  6. NPS

Qualitative

  1. why a cause was selected
  2. what helped participants choose
  3. what felt clear or confusing
  4. what influenced trust, satisfaction, and willingness to continue
  5. why participants preferred one experience over the other

Planned Readout

The readout will summarize:

  1. where SBS appears stronger than the current Causes experience
  2. where SBS still creates confusion or friction
  3. whether the first-round evidence supports moving forward, revising, or pausing before further build work
  4. whether internal expectations differ from external participant feedback

Operational Note

Internal review links have been shared first so the product and design team can confirm that:

  1. the task flow makes sense
  2. the instructions match the intended use cases
  3. the study is ready for recruitment

Once the internal review is complete, recruitment can begin and the first results can be analyzed on the planned delivery timeline.
