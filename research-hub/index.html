A Structured Guide for Conducting Research

This document provides a comprehensive framework for UX research at Compassion Ventures Group (CVG). It serves as a reference for researchers, PMs, and designers to conduct studies efficiently while ensuring methodological rigor and alignment with business objectives.

Each section is mapped to the corresponding research guidelines, methods, and metrics based on internal documentation.

🔍 Using GitLab for Research Management

📄 Reference: 🎡 GitLab for Researchers

GitLab is used at CVG to structure, track, and execute UX research studies. The research process includes:

✔️ Research Intake Request – ART-Form, Reviewing stakeholder needs, defining scope.
✔️ Study Design & Setup – Choosing methods, drafting research plans.
✔️ Execution – Running user interviews, surveys, usability tests.
✔️ Data Analysis – Synthesizing findings, interpreting quantitative and qualitative results.
✔️ Reporting & Documentation – Publishing insights in GitLab for cross-team collaboration.

GitLab Issue Template:

  • Title: [Research Study] [Project Name]
  • Research Objectives
  • Target Participants
  • Methods (e.g., surveys, usability testing)
  • Timeline & Milestones

Research Participation & NDAs

📄 Reference: 🪪 UX Research Participation NDA

All user research must adhere to strict confidentiality agreements to protect participant data and intellectual property.

✔️ Confidential Information Includes: Research data, test materials, feedback, business strategies.
✔️ Obligations: Researchers and participants must not disclose or reuse research data outside of the approved study scope.
✔️ Duration: Confidentiality remains in effect for two years from disclosure.

☯️ Quantitative vs. Qualitative Research: Selecting the Right Method

📄 Reference: ☯️ Quant & Qual Research Methods

The choice between quantitative and qualitative methods depends on research goals:

Quantitative (Measurable Data) 📊 Qualitative (Exploratory Insights) 🎤
"How many?", "How often?" "Why?", "How?"
Large sample sizes Small but deep insights
Surveys, A/B testing, analytics Interviews, usability tests, ethnographies
Statistical validation Thematic analysis

✔️ Quantitative methods are suited for benchmarking, segmentation, and large-scale validation.
✔️ Qualitative methods provide depth in understanding user motivations and behaviors.

🚥 RITE Metrics: Real-Time UX Evaluation

📄 Reference: 🚥 RITE Metrics

Rapid Iterative Testing and Evaluation (RITE) is used at CVG to quickly refine designs based on user feedback. Key metrics include:

✔️ Task Success Rate – Percentage of users completing a task successfully.
✔️ Completion Time – Average time taken per task.
✔️ Error Rate – Frequency and types of errors observed.
✔️ System Usability Scale (SUS) – Standardized usability score.
✔️ Customer Satisfaction Score (CSAT) – User-reported satisfaction.
✔️ Net Promoter Score (NPS) – User loyalty measurement.

⚕️ Measuring UX with SUPR-Q

📄 Reference: ⚕️ SUPR-Q for UX Evaluation

The Standardized User Experience Percentile Rank Questionnaire (SUPR-Q) measures UX across:

✔️ Usability – Ease of task completion.
✔️ Credibility – User trust in content and design.
✔️ Appearance – Professionalism of the interface.
✔️ Loyalty – Likelihood of repeat usage or recommendation.

Data is standardized into a 0-100 scale for comparative analysis.

Read More: ⚕️ SUPR-Q Metrics

⚖️ Net Promoter Score (NPS) in Research

📄 Reference: ⚖️ NPS Score Analysis

NPS measures customer loyalty and advocacy through a single question:

"How likely are you to recommend this product?" (0-10 scale)

✔️ Promoters (9-10) – Highly satisfied users.
✔️ Passives (7-8) – Neutral users.
✔️ Detractors (0-6) – Users with negative experiences.

Formula:
📊 NPS = % Promoters - % Detractors

A higher NPS correlates with product success and retention rates.

🗒️ Survey Design Best Practices

📄 Reference: 🗒️ Beyond Qs: Statistical Survey Design

To ensure statistical validity in surveys:

✔️ Avoid leading or double-barreled questions (e.g., "Do you like our app and find it useful?").
✔️ Use neutral, specific wording (e.g., "How satisfied are you with navigation?").
✔️ Apply reliability metrics (Cronbach’s Alpha, Likert scaling).

Bias mitigation techniques include:

  • Randomized Response Techniques (RRT) – To reduce social desirability bias.
  • Non-Response Adjustments – Weighting responses to match actual user demographics.

📐 The Eight Golden Rules of Interface Design

📄 Reference: 📐 Ben Shneiderman’s Design Principles

1️⃣ Strive for consistency – Ensure uniformity across UI components.
2️⃣ Design for universal usability – Accommodate diverse users.
3️⃣ Offer informative feedback – Provide clear system responses.
4️⃣ Support closure – Indicate task completion effectively.
5️⃣ Prevent errors – Design to minimize user mistakes.
6️⃣ Support reversibility – Enable undo/redo where possible.
7️⃣ Empower users – Avoid restrictive workflows.
8️⃣ Reduce cognitive load – Keep information accessible.

🍕 Summative Metrics for Long-Term Evaluation

📄 Reference: 🍕 Summative Metrics

Summative evaluation provides trend analysis over time, focusing on:

✔️ System Usability Scale (SUS) – Industry-standard usability score.
✔️ UMUX-Lite – A condensed usability assessment.
✔️ Task Completion Rate – Percentage of successful user interactions.
✔️ CSAT – Subjective user satisfaction score.
✔️ Accessibility Audit – Compliance with WCAG standards.
