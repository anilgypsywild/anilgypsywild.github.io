From The Marmot Ate Beans

THE MARMOT ATE BEANS

DIY Guide to Incarceration: A Guide to Losing Your Freedom in Eight Easy Steps

✦

Project Overview

At this point you are wondering what incarceration feels like to your Trader Joe’s shopping, podcast-listening, adventure-curious little soul.

Excellent!

Let us conduct an experiment. Shall we?

This is especially for those of you whose understanding of prison comes from cable television, Netflix documentaries, prestige dramas, podcasts, escape rooms in quaint wine-country downtown shopping districts, or some romantic notion that being incarcerated might finally provide enough uninterrupted time to write your novel.

It will not.

Prison is not a writer’s retreat with worse catering. It is not Yaddo with baloney. It is not MacDowell with a toilet beside your face.

Prison is the systematic subtraction of daily choices until you discover how much of your dignity was hiding inside decisions so trivial you never recognized them as freedom.

So let us simulate it, as good theoretical scientists do, because suffering becomes more respectable once a PhD puts a protocol around it.

You can do this at home.

Step 1: Select the installation site

Find the smallest guest bathroom in your house. I assume you have one. You bought this book, so statistically speaking, there's probably a second toilet somewhere near your laundry room enjoying more privacy and constitutional protection than I did.

And for that purchase, you have legally acquired the right to read these words while contributing, ideally, enough to my future finances for another meal, another pen, perhaps a few more sheets of A4 paper on which I can continue explaining why you should never voluntarily experience the conditions under which these pages were originally written.

Anyway, moving on.

The bathroom should have extraordinarily little natural light. No window is preferable.

Basement bathroom? Perfect. You are already halfway to county jail, but with better resale value.

You are already making excellent progress toward incarceration, and unlike the State of Nevada, I am not even charging you an administrative assessment fee.

Now remove everything. Decorative towels nobody is allowed to use. Scented candles pretending shit doesn't stink. Succulents in overpriced ceramic pots. Lotions with French names. Pottery Barn. Bed Bath & Beyond. The little wicker basket of rolled-up washcloths arranged like ammunition for a war nobody intends to fight. The seashells collected from a beach you visited once in 2017. The framed inspirational quote instructing your guests to breathe, relax, and be present, while they sit on a porcelain throne contemplating their digestive failures.

Whatever suburban artifacts you have accumulated to convince visitors that defecation in your home is not a basic biological necessity but an exclusive, spiritually restorative, five-star boutique experience. Your little porcelain kingdom has been overthrown.

Leave the toilet. You will need that. In fact, the toilet is now furniture, plumbing, witness, roommate, confession booth, and architectural thesis.

Step 2: Assemble materials

Now acquire two mattresses approximately two inches thick.

Calling them mattresses is generous. Think of industrial padding designed by somebody who considers the human vertebral column an optional luxury.

Wrap them in the thickest, least breathable plastic you can find. If you do not possess sufficiently unpleasant plastic, drive to Home Depot. Wander around until you locate something that looks as though it was designed to protect chemical equipment during maritime transport. Get that.

Excellent. That is your bed cover.

Next: one plastic cup, approximately twenty-two ounces; one food container; one fucking spork, the magnificent penal innovation produced when civilization decided that incarcerated people could not be trusted separately with spoons and forks; one towel so degraded that if a guest found it in your bathroom, they would assume you had accidentally left a cleaning rag beside the sink.

That towel is now your good towel.

Clothing is simple.  Take off everything you are wearing. Find prison-style trousers and a shirt three to five sizes too big.  If the trousers fit, you have failed the simulation. Go back to the previous step.

Acquire flip flops approximately two sizes too big. Ideally one strap should appear structurally uncertain.

Now look at yourself. Good. You are almost ready. But incarceration requires one additional piece of equipment.

Another human being.

Step 3: Choose your Cellie

Drive to the nearest gas station at the oddest hour of the day. I recommend 2:30 A.M., when America becomes honest for about twelve minutes under fluorescent light.

You are still wearing oversized prison clothes and defective flip-flops. Otherwise, the experiment lacks methodological validity. Now select a stranger of your own gender. I will use the male-gender for the example, but this proposal scales to all genders. No discrimination from this author.

But you need to be discriminating here. This person will be sharing your basement bathroom for the next two weeks, so random sampling is insufficient. We require inclusion criteria.

A few missing teeth would help. Visible tattoos. Preferably, names, dates, memorials, zip codes, symbols whose meanings you do not understand and should not ask about. The tattoos should appear to have been administered by somebody whose formal artistic training concluded around the age of eleven.

Hair should be irregular. Geographical knowledge should ideally terminate approximately twenty miles beyond wherever in McMinnville this redneck was born. He should possess strong opinions about subjects he cannot explain. This is essential.

He must also be capable of laughing enthusiastically at his own jokes, because there will be no Netflix in your fish tank and therefore his jokes will constitute a substantial portion of your entertainment.

Your volunteer should possess the absolute conviction that nothing that has ever happened to him was his fault. Nothing. Not one fucking molecule of consequence has ever touched this man and found consent.

The arrest? System. The drugs? Somebody else. The fifth DUI? Bad luck. The assault? That motherfucker started it. The parole violation? Bullshit. The woman who left? Bitch. The children who no longer answer? Their mother poisoned them.

You will encounter this philosophical position frequently enough that you should become acquainted with it early. For additional authenticity, your new companion should be willing to spend several hours explaining why heroin is more natural than cannabis.

I am not inventing this. This was an actual conversation in Fish Tank. You will initially attempt to identify the logical structure of the argument.

Do not. You have only two weeks of organic heroin seminars.

Congratulations. You now have a cellmate.

Bring him home. Show him the basement bathroom. Watch his face carefully. If he says, “Not bad,” congratulations.

You chose correctly and should immediately reconsider your life.

There should be two plastic-wrapped mattress pads on the floor, your twenty-two-ounce cups, sporks, food containers, oversized clothing, broken flip flops and one toilet. No privacy screen. Evidently. Privacy was part of your previous life.

Step 4: Remove the Beginning, Middle, and End

Now find some reading material.  Do not make the amateur mistake of selecting books you want to read.

Go to a thrift store dumpster. Better yet, locate novels with the covers missing. The title should be gone. The author’s name should preferably be gone too.

For optimal Fish Tank fidelity, remove the first three chapters. Then remove the final three. Perfect.

You now possess a four-hundred-page novel about a wealthy San Francisco socialite in the 1960s whose marriage is collapsing, whose sister may or may not be sleeping with somebody, and whose husband apparently once drove somebody to an airport for reasons that become increasingly consequential.

You will never discover how the story begins. You will never discover how it ends.

This is your entertainment and your soul’s future existential state.

I read a book like this. To this day I do not know its title.

(Dear reader, if you happen to recognize it from that description, please contact me. The suspense is still fucking killing me.)

Finally, obtain a Bible.

Preferably one from which several of the useful pages have already disappeared because previous residents discovered that Bible paper has nicotine applications unrelated to theology.

Do not be offended, please! The Word of Christ has survived worse distribution problems.

Now place your books beside your plastic mattress. Look around. Toilet. Cellmate. Cup. Spork. Damaged fiction. Partially cannibalized Bible. Excellent. Your cell is ready.

Now we need a correctional officer.

Step 5: Build your own Correctional Officer (C.O.)

Your volunteer correctional officer requires different qualifications than your cellie.

Do not choose someone creative.  Creativity is dangerous here. Watch out!

What you need is consistency, volume and a mildly demonic enthusiasm for interrupting sleep. Give this person the key to your basement bathroom.

This is critical. You do not keep a copy. The entire experiment collapses if, at 2:47 in the morning, you can suddenly remember that you own the fucking house! That is civilian thinking. Stop it.

You do not. Not anymore. For the next two weeks, your volunteer C.O. owns the door. His first responsibility is food. Twice a day, or according to whatever schedule he chooses, he slides you a tray.

Keep the menu scientifically controlled: White bread. Baloney slices. Mustard of a color and consistency suggesting an unwell bird has recently passed overhead. Cream of wheat. Oatmeal. One percent milk. Occasionally an apple. A banana. An orange. No seasoning. No interesting vegetables. No little bottle of hot sauce you remembered buying at Whole Foods.

You are conducting an experiment, not hosting a fucking Fish Tank brunch for TED Talk survivors and their emotionally available rescue dogs.

The grains are hard to destroy. Oatmeal remains oatmeal despite persistent institutional effort. Cream of wheat likewise possesses a type of culinary immortality.

Be grateful. You will learn gratitude. I assure you.

Step 6: Disable Sleep and Privacy Settings

Your correctional officer’s second responsibility is sleep-disruption.

This requires discipline. Have him walk past the bathroom unpredictably between eleven at night and one in the morning, or between three and five.

He should yell. Not beautifully. Not theatrically. Just enough to remind your nervous system that sleep is now a conditional privilege administered by an idiot with keys.

Homophobic insults are historically accurate to my experience, but the crucial variable is not vocabulary. It is unpredictability.  Your nervous system must never learn when the interruption is coming.

Give him the brightest flashlight. Not a tasteful camping headlamp that you got for cheap at a sale at REI.

Something capable of interrogating those Ray-Ban-wearing Tom Cruises flying upside down over Air Force One with this flashlight’s beam.

When you finally fall deeply asleep on your plastic-wrapped two-inch mattress, he should open the viewing slot, or whatever approximation you have engineered, and shine the beam directly into your face. Wake up.

Count! Motherfucker!!

Go back to sleep. Perhaps. Then do it again. This is where the experiment begins changing.

The first night, you will be angry. The third, exhausted. After several nights, your body will begin learning that sleep is not entirely yours. That lesson lasts longer than you might expect.

You may discover years later that a light entering a dark room still reaches some primitive part of you before your conscious mind understands where you are.

That is not the entertaining part of incarceration television usually shows.

A flashlight enters your eyes.

You wake up. That is all.

No violin. No lesson. No redemption arc. Just light, fear, plastic, and your stupid mammalian heart trying to restart.

Now remove showers.  You may clean yourself however the rules permit, with the magnificent towel we selected earlier, but you do not merely decide that you would enjoy a hot shower. Again, you are confusing preferences with freedom.

You will also become remarkably comfortable performing bodily functions in front of your new gas station friend, which is a sentence no civilization should require a grown man to write, but here we are.

This may concern you initially. Very Good. Preserve that concern while you can.

Soon enough you will urinate, defecate, brush your teeth, pick your nose, inspect your skin, scratch whatever…needs scratching and conduct the quotidian maintenance of the human animal approximately three feet from another human animal doing the same fucking thing.

Eventually you will discover a new form of intimacy: the person who looks away. You will love this man.

Not because he gives you privacy. He cannot. Because he gives you the imitation of privacy. That distinction will become enormous.

Step 7: Spiritual Enlightenment, Trader Joe’s Edition

We now proceed to the biochemical phase of our experiment.

If you are the sort of person who shops at Trader Joe's, tracks your sleep, measures your protein intake, and listens to podcasts about optimizing mitochondrial function while driving to your $200 monthly yoga membership, I have excellent news.

Your optimization problem has been solved. We have eliminated almost every variable.

Unfortunately, the variable being optimized is your capacity to tolerate misery.

Cortado? Gone. Your little espresso sacrament has been excommunicated.

Your morning latte?  Gone.

Sugar whenever you want it? Gone.

Alcohol? You guessed it. Gone.

Cannabis? Evidently gone. (You are not serving time in Jamaica, mon. Your Fish Tank is not in Kingston, and nobody is sliding you a state-sponsored-sacramental spliff through the tray slot. I do not know whether you can get cannabis in a Jamaican jail, and with much respect to the entire island, I have no intention of conducting that field study, my bredren.)

Whatever little chemical punctuation marks you used to organize the day have disappeared.

Congratulations! You are detoxing. You are becoming healthy as a sheep being prepared to walk among wolves. “He is brought as a lamb to the slaughter.” (Isaiah 53:7, KJV)

And while your body is adapting, something strange begins happening to your mind. You start missing people. Not elegantly. Desperately.

People who annoyed you. People who disappointed you. People whose calls you ignored because you were busy. People you believed you needed distance from. You begin wanting one syllable from them.

Just your name. Just: “Hello?”

You will discover that the human voice can become contraband too. 

And somewhere around then, if you are anything like me, Christ becomes less theoretical.

This is where my incarceration simulation begins offering services normally available only at more expensive retreats.

You do not need to fly to Peru. You do not need a shaman.

You do not need toad venom, MDMA, ayahuasca, or whatever substance Mike Tyson and whichever celebrity appeared on your podcast this week recommend for encountering death, Christ, the universe, the inner child, the outer child, or the motherfucking cosmic architecture of consciousness.

Your basement guest bathroom. Two weeks. One gas station stranger. A plastic mattress. Baloney. Sleep deprivation. No privacy. You are welcome..

But there is one consequence of this little experiment that I cannot package as a joke.

What happens to the human soul when almost everything it loves has been taken away?

Step 8: Congratulations. You have successfully installed Hell.

Well, the answer is not spiritual enlightenment at Trader Joe’s prices, you’ve hit the 666 Jackpot! You’ve actually installed Hell!

Somewhere in that room you may develop a tangible closeness to Christ. Or, more accurately, you may discover what the absence of Christ feels like.

Prison had begun to make hell intelligible.  Not flames. Not little red demons with agricultural equipment.

Separation.

The terrible possibility of being separated from love itself!

And once that notion enters your mind, ordinary life begins changing value. You will think about your loved ones. Your child. Your parents. The friend whose message you did not answer. The woman you assumed would always be there. The neighbor whose name you never learned.

You will remember entire years during which these people existed within touching distance while you behaved as though time were an unlimited natural resource.  You will remember evenings spent watching Netflix beside someone you claimed to love without looking at them.

Hours donated to social media. Facebook or some other sewage bin receiving more of your attention than the food pantry three miles from your house.

Your child asking a question while you looked at your phone.

Your mother calling at an inconvenient time. Your father repeating a story you had already heard.

You will remember being annoyed. How luxurious annoyance will seem!

Then other things return. Sunrise in your own zip code. Sunset.  Walking outside because you decided to.

The coffee shop where the same overworked barista handed you a drink fifty mornings in a row. You barely knew her name. You tipped like a stingy motherfucker.  Now you would give twenty dollars merely to stand there for three minutes while somebody steamed milk.

A grocery store. Vegetables. A clean shirt. Opening the refrigerator.

Choosing. That verb becomes almost obscene!

Choosing. What do I want for breakfast? Where should I go? Should I take a shower now or later? Do I want the window open? Would I like to walk? Should I call someone?

Outside, these are not even decisions. Inside, they are wealth!

You will not be thinking much about quarterly reviews. Bonuses. Performance ratings. Whether somebody with a slightly inferior résumé got promoted ahead of you.

The email your manager sent at 4:47 p.m. that ruined your entire evening because its punctuation seemed fucking hostile. Those anxieties belonged to a civilization in which you possessed enough freedom to manufacture secondary suffering.

Your cellie will cure some of that. He may also spend forty-five minutes explaining why heroin is more organic than cannabis.

Spiritual development is not linear. 

But eventually one of two things will happen. Either you will emerge persuaded by his pharmacological thesis, in which case my experiment has failed catastrophically, or you will begin understanding that the banal, trite, commonplace things you once treated as background scenery were the very substance of your life.

A towel. Coffee. A locked bathroom door. A child’s voice. A friend. Sunrise. Someone saying your name.

You had been standing inside abundance and calling yourself deprived.

Prison did not give me those things. It took them away. That is how I finally saw them.

Dear reader, we are not done yet.

You have only survived two simulated weeks in your basement Fish Tank. You have shared a toilet with your gas station friend. You have slept on plastic. You have been awakened by flashlights. You have detoxed from caffeine, sugar, alcohol, cannabis, privacy, silence, and the dangerous narcotic known as choosing what you would like for dinner.

You are feeling strong?  You are thinking: I could do prison?

No! You stupid idiot!!

That is precisely the conclusion this experiment was designed to prevent.

Prison is not fun. Prison is not a PhD. Prison does not give you credibility. Prison does not make you interesting.

Prison is not a sabbatical, but I repeatedly called mine one because the alternative vocabulary scared me.

And prison certainly does not give you time to author a book.

Prison fucks you.

Not necessarily in the dramatic ways television has trained you to expect. Sometimes, certainly. Violence exists. Predation exists. Fear exists. But the more efficient damage is usually banal. It is subtraction.

Take a human being.

Remove the keys. Remove the wallet. Remove the phone. Remove the belt. Remove the shoelaces. Remove the ability to close a bathroom door. Remove the ability to decide when to shower. Remove the ability to decide when to sleep. Remove the ability to decide when to wake up. Remove the ability to walk outside. Remove the ability to buy an apple.

Remove the ability to call your child when you miss him.

Now give the human being a number. Repeat the number often enough. Eventually he will understand the experiment.

This is why I want you to try the basement bathroom before you romanticize imprisonment!

If you are actually facing prison, listen carefully

And if you are reading this because you are facing prison, then listen carefully.

• Do not waste your remaining freedom rehearsing terror.

• Prepare. Learn how your facility works. Find out what property you are allowed.

• Arrange for someone trustworthy to help you from outside, including putting money on your books. But understand what money inside prison means.

Money is not freedom. Money buys tolerability. Soap. Underwear. Writing paper. Stamps. Coffee. Ramen noodles. Enough beans and rice to make dinner resemble something your body recognizes as food.

Do not arrive imagining commissary is a shopping trip. It is logistics. And do not enter prison believing everybody who calls you brother is your brother.

Be kind. Be respectful. Share when you can.  But observe.

Listen more than you speak.

Do not borrow casually. Do not create debts you do not understand. Do not carry things for people. Do not hold things for people.

Do not allow somebody else’s contraband to become your problem because he told you it would only be for five minutes. Five minutes is enough to double your sentence or worse. Do not involve yourself in economies whose rules you do not understand.

Do not become interesting. 

That may be the most useful sentence I can give you in this entire book.

Do not become interesting. 

You do not need to prove that you are tough. You do not need to prove that you are educated.  You do not need to prove that you are innocent. You do not need to explain your case to every man who asks.

You certainly do not need to become an amateur lawyer because somebody three bunks away has discovered a constitutional argument while smoking something manufactured in a bathroom.

Protect your body. Protect your mind. Protect your release date.

Read. Write. Exercise.

Pray, if you pray. I bet you will try to pray at least once.

Learn to tolerate boredom without converting boredom into stupidity.

And if the institution gives you a legitimate path toward greater freedom, a job, camp, transitional housing, work release, do not fuck with the deal.  I watched men do exactly that. Phones. Drugs. Tobacco. Violence. Escape. A moment’s appetite exchanged for months or years of additional confinement.

Freedom is too expensive to trade for something that fits inside your palm.

And there is one more thing.  You will become desperate for love. Expect it. Isolation performs interesting mathematics in the heart. A person you resented outside can become the person you would give anything to hear for thirty seconds. Do not confuse that desperation with revelation. Prison can clarify love. It can also distort it.

Write the letter. Make the call. Apologize where you owe an apology. But do not construct an entire future from loneliness inside a bunk.

Your heart is incarcerated, too. Give it time to get out. Then decide.

That is the part nobody put in my intake paperwork.
